# Business, Branding, and IP

Risk-awareness reference, not legal advice.

Personal customization and commercial sale have different risk profiles. LEGO states that the minifigure and other brand assets are protected IP/trademarks in relevant jurisdictions; BrickLink's IP guidance says the standard minifigure design is protected and that selling work based on others' IP may require authorization.

Build an independent business name and visual identity. Do not use the LEGO logo as your business logo or imply official sponsorship/authorization. Describe genuine donor elements and compatible third-party components accurately and preserve provenance.

Do not assume purchase of a genuine blank part grants permission for every commercial decoration. Characters, logos, uniforms, franchises and likenesses can involve separate rights holders.

A lower-risk creative catalog emphasizes original characters, original factions, genuinely public-domain subject matter after review, and commissioned original art with documented reproduction rights.

Store product rights status, component provenance, artwork source, designer, release date and takedown/contact procedure.

Before buying expensive print equipment, validate demand with prototypes and outsourced runs. Track conversion, repeat customers, selling price, defect/replacement rate, labor minutes and gross margin by process.

Packaging should clearly identify your own brand and avoid confusing trade dress. Consumer-product/small-parts labeling and safety obligations depend on jurisdiction and product; obtain qualified advice before commercial launch.
