# Decals and Finishing

Waterslide is the most accessible route from digital art to a convincing prototype.

## Clear vs white film
Clear film minimizes visible edge and preserves substrate color, but ordinary home printers cannot print opaque white and colors become substrate-dependent. White film provides opacity/white but requires precise cutting or intentional white boundaries. Buy media specifically for inkjet or laser according to the printer.

## Workflow
Clean the part; print an actual-size paper test; print fixed-scale decal sheet; seal inkjet artwork if required by the media; cure; cut closely; soak per manufacturer; position; remove trapped water/air gently; dry fully; then apply only a tested compatible protective finish.

## Failure modes
Silvering/haze = trapped air/poor contact. Bleeding/cracking = sealing/coating incompatibility. Wrinkles = too much softener or excessive compound curvature. White border = cutting issue on white film. Muddy color = clear film over dark substrate. Peeling = contamination/adhesion/edge issue. Cloudy coat = humidity, incompatibility or excessive application.

Heads and arms are difficult compound/curved surfaces. Prefer segmented art, controlled seams and calibrated setting/softening methods rather than forcing oversized flat film.

Adhesive sticker film is easier but thicker. Waterslide is thinner but more delicate. Direct printing is generally preferable when the target is a premium factory-like appearance.

Protect friction/joint/connection surfaces from coating buildup. Test gloss, satin and matte because sheen mismatch can expose an otherwise excellent custom.
