# Semantic pass 13 — batch 24: Dagonist recruitment targeting

This batch analyzes several letters/bulletins associated with the Dagon-aligned Hooded Figure.

## Recruitment by dispossession

BOOK 058B8FD1 targets people who feel society has discarded them:
- low social station;
- livelihoods destroyed by bandits;
- failed crops/weather;
- general powerlessness through bad fortune.

The pitch explicitly says noble birth, wealth, and innate magical ability are unnecessary. Ambition, open-mindedness, and willingness to use taboo tools are presented as sufficient.

This is a social-revolutionary recruitment frame consistent with the cult's Dagon doctrine of ambition/change.

## Recruitment by boundary-pushing

BOOK 058B8FCB targets someone who requested a dangerously taboo volume for College-related research.

The recruiter praises willingness to push boundaries and tells the recipient to keep the contact secret.

The note appears to have been intended for Quinergus, creating a direct attempt to recruit a legitimate/independent researcher through intellectual transgression.

## Recruitment of successful criminal/occult actors

BOOK 058D2516 approaches the head of a rapidly growing organization through praise of ambition and an offer to "lend" more power.

Other letters to Bashnag (0591EB83) and an unnamed vampire/contact (0597F354) continue supply-for-services arrangements and direct them to the same relocated trapper's shack near the Lord Stone.

The Bashnag letter explicitly warns that excessive betrayal risks falling into the Webspinner's sphere, contrasting Dagon's ambition with Mephala's betrayal.

## Mobility and operational security

Multiple letters give the same current location: an old trapper's shack near/east of the Lord Stone.

A later note says the Dagonist abandons the shack after plans succeed and leaves it to a student.

This indicates a mobile cell/contact point rather than a permanent temple.

## Recruitment pattern

The Dagonist targets people at several stages:
- powerless outsiders who want agency;
- scholars willing to violate taboos;
- successful criminal/occult leaders who want more power;
- existing associates who can provide supplies.

The common selection criterion is ambition, not class or profession.
