# Semantic pass 13 — batch 08b: Gatekeeper rings, trade ban, and euphemism

This batch covers the second half of Hooded Recruiter 0557C32F's recruitment dialogue plus the Vigilant Report (BOOK 055AEE8D).

## Rings as recruitment/access tokens

After introducing the Gatekeeper doctrine, the recruiter offers gem-studded rings.

He calls them "purely ceremonial" and says selling them cheaply helps spread the faith.

He also gives a practical warning: do not enter the Yorgrim Overlook "temple" without wearing one of the rings.

The ring therefore functions at minimum as an access/recognition token.

## External evidence that the rings are not merely ceremonial

A Vigilant report about matching rings says:
- one was recovered from a captured conjurer;
- the conjurer said the rings were gifts from "the masters";
- he claimed the ring gave power to steal souls and raise the dead;
- the Vigilants did not think the ring was Daedric in origin;
- they ordered matching rings destroyed and their carriers subjected to Stendarr's mercy.

The Revenant-Moon letter independently says Gatekeepers are spreading powerful rings that tempt new members, including seeding them in halls of the dead.

Together these sources make the recruiter's "purely ceremonial" description a clear public-facing minimization/euphemism rather than a complete account of the ring system.

## Trade restriction

When asked whether the gems are illegal, the recruiter says the East Empire Trading Company has a trade ban on the Gatekeepers because of "religious differences".

He uses the phrase "Independent Traders" in a deliberately evasive way around the supply.

This is direct evidence that the Gatekeepers face at least one formal commercial restriction while continuing to move goods through alternative/criminal channels.

## Evidence boundary

The EETC-ban reason is the recruiter's characterization. The ban itself is strongly asserted; the claim that it exists *only* because of religious discrimination should remain his framing unless an EETC source is found.
