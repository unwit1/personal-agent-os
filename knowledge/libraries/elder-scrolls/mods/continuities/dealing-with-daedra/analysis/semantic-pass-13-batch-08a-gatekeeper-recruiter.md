# Semantic pass 13 — batch 08a: Gatekeeper recruiter and Gentleman's Club shipment

This batch covers the first recruitment/logistics cluster of Hooded Figure NPC 0557C32F.

## Recruitment through fear of death

The Hooded Figure describes himself as "a missionary of a sort".

He says he looks for people who have witnessed a great deal of death because repeated exposure forces them to think about what happens when they die. He then offers "the reassurance of certainty".

This is a targeted Gatekeeper recruitment strategy: mortality anxiety is treated as the opening for conversion.

## Trial errand

Before explaining the order, the recruiter asks the prospective member to deliver a box to the Eastmarch Gentleman's Club.

The box contains a tattoo kit and a large quantity of ink.

He says many of the Club's workers use the ink because it produces a crisp, rich purple.

When asked why priests make tattoo ink, he compares the practice to churches selling alcohol or woodcrafts to fund operations.

The shipment therefore independently corroborates an ordinary commercial relationship between the Gatekeepers and the Gentleman's Club.

## Ghost doctrine

After the delivery, the recruiter explains the order's public theological pitch.

He says their order prepares the soul during life so that it lingers rather than passing into the unknown afterlife.

He frames the choice philosophically: remain as a shadow of oneself, face an unknown afterlife, or perhaps face nothing.

He further claims their created ghosts are more potent and self-aware than ordinary ghosts.

The first proposition—deliberate preparation for postmortem persistence—is consistent with the Gatekeepers' mechanically real wraith systems. The comparative claim that their ghosts are more self-aware/potent remains recruiter testimony unless measured elsewhere.

## Significance

The Gatekeepers do not recruit only through necromantic power.

Their pipeline combines existential fear, ordinary delivery work, commercial ties, apparently benign craft goods, philosophical discussion, and only then an invitation into necromantic practice.
