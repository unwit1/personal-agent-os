# Ja'zhan

- Continuity: `tes.mod.vigilant`
- Role: historical witness to Mary/Moura's healing and execution
- Primary form: `NPC_ 020FED32` / `zzzCHJazhan` — **Ja'zhan**

## Eyewitness account of Mary

Ja'zhan preserves one of the clearest personal-witness accounts in the Mary/Moura cluster.

He says:
- Mary saved a boy from the plague;
- he personally saw the Alessians burn the **maiden of Mara** at the stake;
- she did not scream;
- she eventually collapsed into ash.

This testimony independently supports the central persecution narrative preserved elsewhere in VIGILANT.

## Cross-source corroboration

Ja'zhan's account broadly agrees with:
- *The Eight Saints of Cyrod*, which identifies Moura's true name as Mary and describes her as a healer persecuted by the Alessian Order;
- *The Grand Inquisitor*, which structurally places Mary the Dark Maiden in the **Mara** role during a burning memory;
- Pope Megus, who remembers Mary's healing and regrets that “we” burned her;
- Sir Caius, who describes Mary as a healer/priestess of Mara burned by the Alessians.

The witnesses do not make every theological or supernatural claim equally reliable, but they strongly converge on the basic historical pattern of Mary's healing ministry and execution.

## “Maiden of Mara”

Ja'zhan's description of Mary as the **maiden of Mara** is important but should not automatically be interpreted as literal divine descent.

Within the wider source cluster:
- Mary is associated with Mara worship;
- *The Grand Inquisitor* assigns Mary the Dark Maiden to alias **Mara**;
- Megus wonders whether she was a daughter of Mara.

These together show deliberate religious identification and symbolism. They do not by themselves prove that Mary is literally the goddess Mara or her biological/divine daughter.

## Ash imagery

Ja'zhan's statement that Mary collapsed into ash after the burning is consistent with VIGILANT's repeated death/remains/relic imagery.

It also helps contextualize later claims that Mary appears again in Coldharbour. The contrast between complete physical destruction and later recurrence reinforces that her subsequent manifestations cannot be explained as simple uninterrupted bodily survival.

## Reliability

Ja'zhan is especially useful because his statement is framed as **personal observation** rather than second-hand rumor.

High-confidence as testimony:
- he says he witnessed Mary's execution;
- he describes the burning and ash;
- he identifies her as associated with Mara and plague healing.

Still unresolved:
- whether the remembered scene is perfectly historical;
- the exact relationship between this execution and later memory/Coldharbour forms;
- theological claims about Mary's relationship to Mara.

## Canon boundary

Ja'zhan's eyewitness narrative and its connection to Mary/Moura are VIGILANT continuity material. The mod uses established Alessian, Mara, and plague concepts as substrate but supplies this specific witness history itself.

## Authored trilogy — direct VIGILANT books

Ja'zhan is not only an encounter/witness NPC. VIGILANT directly credits him as author of three books:

- `BOOK 02054ED4` — **What Khajiit Saw**
- `BOOK 02054ED5` — **What Khajiit Heard**
- `BOOK 02054ED6` — **What Khajiit Said**

These books substantially deepen his worldview.

### What Khajiit Saw

Ja'zhan condemns Cyrodiilic nobles who adopt Khajiiti children while abusing them.

His account centers on a five-year-old child subjected to severe physical abuse and winter confinement.

The point of the account is not simply anti-human prejudice: Ja'zhan frames cruelty toward children as evidence that outwardly cultured people can conceal predatory behavior.

### What Khajiit Heard

Ja'zhan recounts a wealthy retired knight of the Alessian Order after the cleansing of Malada.

According to the account:
- the knight believed status gave him power over subjects' lives;
- a servant's nine-year-old child accidentally injured one of the knight's hounds;
- the child was imprisoned overnight;
- the next morning the knight turned the child loose and had his hounds hunt and kill him in front of the child's mother.

This directly gives Ja'zhan an anti-Alessian historical/moral critique independent of his Mary testimony.

The knight remains unnamed in the text and should not be assigned to a known Alessian character without additional evidence.

### What Khajiit Said

The final book explicitly rejects what Ja'zhan calls Alessian **harmony** when that harmony requires innocent suffering.

Its repeated child images include:
- the child confined in the cold latrine;
- a child lost in red mist;
- a child struggling with a rope.

Ja'zhan says unheard suffering must be atoned for and that no truth is worth a system in which children must suffer for atonement.

He says he would maintain that position even at a chopping block.

## Ja'zhan's ethical position

Across the trilogy and his Mary testimony, Ja'zhan consistently opposes:
- institutional cruelty hidden behind civilization or religious authority;
- punishment of innocents;
- abstract harmony or truth purchased through victimization;
- Alessian persecution.

This makes him a significant ethical witness in VIGILANT rather than merely comic relief or a skooma merchant.
