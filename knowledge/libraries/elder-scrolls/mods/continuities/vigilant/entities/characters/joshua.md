## Aredhel's account

Aredhel's note says that during the Blood Matron disaster:
- Jacob was killed;
- **Joshua fled in terror**;
- the remaining Vigilants surrendered to the Blood Matron.

This makes Joshua the explicit flight/survival branch of the failed Vigilant expedition.

## Joshua's Final Note

Joshua's own final note strongly corroborates Aredhel.

He says:
- a door sealed behind him;
- he had abandoned his friends and tried to escape;
- he had believed he could face any vampire;
- when confronted by **her**, he could not even stand his ground and ran;
- he blames Stendarr for asking mortals to do what even Stendarr could not;
- he hears a lullaby;
- he believes **she is coming** and desperately wants to escape her reach.

The note does not name the woman directly.

Within the surrounding Windhelm/Blood Matron source cluster, the strongest referent is Lamae/Blood Matron, but Lorekeeper should retain the pronoun-level uncertainty in the note itself.

## Faith crisis

Joshua's final note is also a direct religious breakdown.

He explicitly turns on Stendarr and asks how the Vigilants could be expected to do something even Stendarr could not accomplish.

This is important for VIGILANT's broader institutional theme:
- Vigilants preach certainty and mercy;
- catastrophic encounters expose fear, helplessness, compromise, and resentment;
- Jacob, Aredhel, and Joshua each respond differently to the same disaster.

### Response split

- **Jacob** → death/near-death and Molag Bal bargain.
- **Aredhel** → surrender and willing acceptance of Blood Matron blood.
- **Joshua** → flight, isolation, despair, apparent final pursuit.
- **other Vigilants** → surrender but resistance to bloodthirst, according to Aredhel.

This gives the Windhelm incident a much richer survivor/failure topology.

## Lullaby motif

Joshua's last line says he hears a lullaby and believes the woman is near.

The source currently does not establish:
- whether the lullaby is literal sound;
- a magical compulsion;
- a Blood Matron signature;
- a memory or hallucination.

Keep it as a distinctive but unresolved motif.
