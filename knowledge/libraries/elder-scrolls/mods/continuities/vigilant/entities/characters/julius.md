# Julius Bruiant

- Continuity: `tes.mod.vigilant`
- Primary quest form: `NPC_ 020461D8` / `zzzCOJuliusChildOblivion`
- Other forms: `02061404` / `zzzCOJulius`; `023288E4` / `zzzCODregsOfSithisJulius`; Dwemer-spider variant `0204BFF2`
- Role: son of Marcus and Julia Bruiant; central figure of *Child of Oblivion*

## Evidence profile

Julius is unusually document-driven: his primary *Child of Oblivion* form currently has no ordinary INFO dialogue uniquely attributed by the structural resolver. His story instead emerges from quest aliases/objectives, family journals, the Ilinalta report, Bartholo's investigation, Bal/Molag Bal dialogue, and one short note written by Julius.

## Timeline

Marcus' 4E 199 journal proves Julius was already alive before Marcus' separate affair/pregnancy in Markarth; the unborn child in that entry should not be identified as Julius.

In 4E 200, Julia takes Julius and leaves Marcus. Marcus records both disappearing; Julius is later found alone in the forest, terrified and unable to speak. Julia is subsequently found dead at Lake Ilinalta under a supernatural curse.

Afterward Julius remains mute and increasingly strange. Marcus says Julius becomes attached to the newly hired butler Bal. Lamthor reports seeing Julius mutter incantations to the estate's dogs and blames Bal's influence.

In 4E 201, during Bartholo's investigation, Julius initially avoids the Vigilant but later spends time outside Bartholo's room immediately before Bartholo's final trapped journal entry.

Marcus' final note says Julius is no longer human and identifies him as a **Child of Oblivion**. Molag Bal later describes Julius' hatred as exceptionally powerful, even comparing it to Lamae's. The quest ultimately gives the player the objective to defeat Julius.

## Julius' own voice

`BOOK 02065930` / *Julius' Note* contains only a plea to his father not to do "this." The referent should not be guessed without placement/script context.

## Reliability note

Most claims about Julius come from frightened adults, investigators, or Molag Bal. The contrast between sparse first-person evidence and extensive external interpretation is itself important.
