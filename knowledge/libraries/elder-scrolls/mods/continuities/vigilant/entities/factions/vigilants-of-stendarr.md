# Vigilants of Stendarr — VIGILANT continuity

- Continuity: `tes.mod.vigilant`
- Type: anti-Daedric religious order / player-entry institution
- Major figures: Altano, Jacob, Aredhel, Gwyneth, Bartholo, the player character
- Major quest family: `zzzAoMMq00`–`zzzAoMMq10`
- Opening quest: **Vigilant of Stendarr**

## Narrative function

The Vigilants of Stendarr provide the player's institutional entry point into VIGILANT.

The opening quest has the player join the Vigilants and meet **Altano**.

The early numbered quest chain then moves through:
- vampire hunting;
- Daedric investigations;
- summoners and witches;
- questions of mercy and coercion;
- Molag Bal-associated relics and temptations;
- Altano's eventual betrayal.

The organization is therefore not only a background faction. Its moral language structures the first major act of the mod.

## Altano and coercive mercy

Altano begins as a Vigilant mentor.

His behavior increasingly exposes tension inside the institution's anti-Daedric mission:
- he insists Balor should die despite the player's report that Balor is not hostile;
- he threatens the player's place among the Vigilants for refusing;
- he later orders the killing of a witch and her child;
- he repeatedly frames killing as Stendarr's mercy.

VIGILANT uses Altano to test the difference between:
- protection;
- religious certainty;
- coercion;
- mercy used as justification for violence.

Altano's later corruption does not automatically erase his earlier agency. His dying explanation says he eventually heard and followed a compelling voice, but the ESM does not establish that all earlier harsh decisions were mind control.

## Jacob

Jacob is an older Vigilant who rescues the amnesiac Altano and gives him his name.

His earlier history now has a fuller documentary reconstruction:
- the 4E 180 Windhelm Maiden Statue investigation;
- Rahel's contemporaneous journal;
- Jacob's warning about the statue;
- Aredhel's statement that Jacob was killed;
- Jacob's later first-person account that Molag Bal revived him for the price of his wife's soul.

This makes Jacob one of the clearest examples of VIGILANT placing a Vigilant directly inside the conflict between duty, vampiric/Daedric threat, death, survival, and compromise.

Detailed files:
- `entities/characters/jacob.md`
- `entities/characters/rahel.md`
- `analysis/windhelm-maiden-statue-incident.md`

His later history in **Old Regrets** becomes one of the mod's clearest examples of a Vigilant compromising his principles under desperation.

This makes Jacob an institutional counterexample to a simple heroic-Vigilant model.

## Bartholo and Gwyneth

The Bruiant Mansion / **Child of Oblivion** layer begins with Gwyneth sending the player to investigate missing Vigilant **Bartholo** and the Bruiant family.

This extends the Vigilants' investigative presence beyond Altano's personal quest chain and into the mansion/Child of Oblivion arc.

## Aredhel / Renald complexity

Aredhel has connections to the Vigilant narrative and later Coldharbour material, including the **Pitier** identity cluster and Diagna equipment.

As elsewhere in VIGILANT, institutional identity can persist across radically different forms and contexts.

Lorekeeper should preserve specific quest-era identities rather than assume that every later Coldharbour manifestation functions as an active member of the contemporary Vigilants.

## Temple and collapse

The Temple of Stendarr is central to the pre-Coldharbour storyline.

Altano eventually returns with Molag Bal-associated material, and the Temple is devastated during his betrayal.

The later Coldharbour topology even contains a configured cross-layer door relationship involving the Temple of Stendarr and Mathmalatu Priory, though its runtime meaning remains script/state dependent.

The institution's collapse therefore acts as one of the major thresholds into VIGILANT's deeper dream/Coldharbour structure.

## Mercy versus domination

VIGILANT repeatedly places Stendarr's language of mercy beside Molag Bal's rhetoric of salvation through submission.

This creates a thematic conflict rather than a claim of metaphysical identity.

The player is repeatedly asked whether:
- mercy can justify killing;
- protection can become domination;
- suffering excuses surrendering freedom;
- religious authority can become coercive.

The Vigilants are the first institutional frame through which the mod explores those questions.

## Official substrate versus VIGILANT extension

The Vigilants of Stendarr are an established Skyrim faction dedicated to combating Daedra, vampires, and similar threats.

VIGILANT builds an original storyline around that institution:
- Altano's recruitment and fall;
- Jacob's bargain;
- Bartholo's disappearance;
- the Temple disaster;
- the player's route from Vigilant work into Coldharbour.

Those events belong to the mod continuity.

## Reliability

High-confidence:
- the main quest chain begins with joining the Vigilants;
- Altano is a Vigilant mentor and later antagonist;
- Jacob is an older Vigilant tied to Altano;
- Bartholo is a missing Vigilant in the Bruiant investigation;
- the Temple of Stendarr is a major story location.

Interpretive:
- whether Altano's conduct represents institutional doctrine or personal extremism;
- how much institutional authority survives after the Temple disaster;
- whether later Coldharbour Vigilant-like identities are literal members or reconstructed forms.

## Canon boundary

The Vigilants of Stendarr are established Elder Scrolls lore.

Altano, Jacob's VIGILANT-specific bargain, Bartholo/Bruiant events, the Temple collapse, and the player's institutional arc are `tes.mod.vigilant` material.
