## Ritual account

The first-person narrator describes:
- Marukh's corpse rising;
- seventy-seven signs being carved into the air;
- faithful continuing to pray despite burning eyes;
- Marukh opening a crack in reality;
- intense light pouring through;
- the faithful being reduced to ashes;
- the narrator's own body crumbling.

The narrator initially interprets this as entry into Aetherius.

## Failed expectation

Beyond the light, the narrator instead perceives:
- a barren/godless wasteland;
- no expected paradise;
- a crisis of meaning and faith.

The text does not name the realm.

Do not identify the wasteland as:
- Coldharbour;
- Aetherius;
- the Void;
- a specific Oblivion plane

without separate evidence.

## Theological reversal

The narrator moves from ecstatic faith to rejection:
- questions divine trials;
- rejects dependence on gods;
- swears by a black soul;
- vows to cleanse the blood/taint of Anu from the world.

This is a first-person ideological transformation.
