# Laza and the White Owl — source map

Continuity: `tes.mod.vigilant`

Primary source:
- `BOOK 021E7B13` — **Laza and the White Owl**

Exact text:
- `books/021E7B13-laza-and-the-white-owl.txt`

## Scene

Laza meets the **White Owl** on a green meadow.

The Owl observes Laza's **weeping eyes** and gives a sequence of transformations.

## Transformation sequence

The White Owl says:

1. a **shepherd** may become a **wolf** by laying down the staff;
2. the wolf may roam the forest and hunt;
3. a wolf may become the **wind** by abandoning its fangs;
4. as wind, no place is out of reach;
5. this could allow the traveler to reach those they lost;
6. but if the wind becomes the **wind of Order**, the fangs return;
7. the wind becomes a wolf again.

This is direct VIGILANT text.

## Core motif

The source establishes a transformation vocabulary:

**shepherd → wolf → wind → wind of Order → wolf**

The changes are framed through:
- staff;
- fangs;
- hunting;
- unrestricted movement;
- grief for lost people;
- Order as a condition that restores predatory form.

The text does not explain whether these transformations are:
- literal bodily metamorphosis;
- spiritual states;
- metaphysical roles;
- allegorical instructions.

Keep the mechanism unresolved.

## Relationship to Laza mantle

The separate `BOOK 020DB22F` says one may **take on the name of Laza** and become an immortal hunter.

Together, the two texts support:
- Laza as a role/mantle;
- shepherd and hunter imagery as central to that role.

They do not prove every Laza bearer follows the exact same transformation sequence.

## White Owl identity boundary

Later Vicn material supplies stronger identity evidence for **White Owl → Jhunal the White**.

That later continuity evidence should be retained as a cross-Vicn resolution layer.

Within this exact VIGILANT text, however, the speaker is only **the White Owl**.

Do not silently rewrite the VIGILANT source as if it names Jhunal.
