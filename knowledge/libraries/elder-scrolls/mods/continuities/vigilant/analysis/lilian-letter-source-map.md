## Direct statement

Lilian writes to **Papa** that:
- she made flower crowns;
- one was for him;
- she wants him to get well;
- she wants him to come home.

Within the family document cluster, Papa maps to **Taranis**.

## What it establishes

The letter directly establishes:
- Lilian is Taranis's daughter;
- Taranis is absent from home;
- Lilian understands him to be ill or afflicted;
- she expects or hopes his condition can improve.

This gives the curse storyline a child's-eye perspective independent of Carene and Taranis.

## Flower-crown motif

Lilian's flower crown is independently echoed in Carene's letter, where Carene tells Taranis that Lilian made a flower crown for him.

That is useful cross-document corroboration of the family unit.

It does not establish any magical significance for the crown.

## Reliability

This is straightforward first-person family correspondence.

Unresolved:
- whether Lilian knows the true nature of Taranis's sword curse;
- where Taranis is when she writes;
- whether the letter reaches him before the later bargain.
