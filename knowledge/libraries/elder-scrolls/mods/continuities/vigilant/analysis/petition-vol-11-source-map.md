## Belharza dispute

Pelan protests the imprisonment of Emperor Belharza.

Amicus denies Belharza's legitimacy and proposes:
- replacing the official story;
- preparing a human Belharza;
- fabricating memories;
- fabricating history;
- reshaping belief and divine form.

Pelan argues that the Amulet of Kings chose Belharza and therefore validates him.

## Historical rewriting

The strongest direct ideological statement is Amicus's willingness to create a chosen truth when existing reality refuses to conform.

He explicitly links:
- belief;
- memory;
- history;
- divine form.

This is deliberate programmatic rewriting, not accidental forgetting.

## Ayleid witnesses

Amicus confirms that friendly Ayleids were slaughtered because their long memories preserved inconvenient forms of:
- Alessia;
- Morihaus;
- Pelinal.

This makes witness elimination part of the memory-control program.

## Reliability

- Amicus's ideology;
- Pelan's opposition;
- their competing interpretations.

It does not independently prove Amicus's metaphysical theory that belief can successfully manufacture gods/history.
