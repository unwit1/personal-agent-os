## Direct testimony

Joshua says:
- a door sealed behind the group;
- he abandoned his friends and tried to escape;
- he had believed he could face any vampire;
- he could not stand before **“her”**;
- he ran;
- he blames Stendarr for placing them in an impossible situation;
- he hears a lullaby;
- he believes the woman is approaching;
- he wants to flee beyond her reach.

## Corroboration with Aredhel

Aredhel independently says:
- Joshua fled in terror.

Joshua's note confirms this directly from Joshua's own perspective.

This makes **Joshua fled** one of the strongest cross-source facts in the Windhelm/Blood Matron disaster.

## Identity boundary

Joshua never names **“her.”**

Within the wider source cluster, the strongest contextual candidate is the Blood Matron/Lamae network.

But the note itself establishes only:
- a female vampire-associated threat;
- overwhelming fear;
- lullaby imagery.

Do not silently replace “her” with Lamae in quotations or source-local claims.

## Stendarr accusation

Joshua says:
- they were asked to do what even Stendarr could not have done;
- he turns his fear and anger toward Stendarr.

This is Joshua's crisis-of-faith reaction.

It is not neutral proof that:
- Stendarr literally attempted the same feat;
- the threat was objectively beyond divine power.

## Lullaby motif

The final line introduces a **lullaby** immediately before Joshua believes the woman is arriving.

This should be indexed with:
- Blood Matron;
- sleep/awakening motifs;
- Maiden Statue warning;
- vampire corruption.

It remains sensory testimony from a terrified witness.
