# VIGILANT ↔ official Elder Scrolls comparison — seed pass

Continuity: `tes.mod.vigilant`

This layer compares VIGILANT's historical/mythic claims against **licensed Elder Scrolls source texts**. It does not treat every licensed in-universe book as omniscient truth: when official sources disagree, that disagreement is retained.

## Comparison vocabulary

- **established-tradition** — VIGILANT uses a relationship/event strongly represented across licensed TES sources.
- **compatible-contested** — VIGILANT follows one licensed in-universe interpretation, but licensed sources dispute or qualify it.
- **vigilant-extension** — VIGILANT adds a specific event, mechanism, identity theory, or causal explanation not established by the compared licensed sources.
- **reframing** — VIGILANT uses licensed material but changes its narrative function or puts it inside a manipulated-memory/dream framework.
- **unresolved** — current source set is insufficient for classification.

## Alessia, Morihaus, and Belharza

### Alessia ↔ Morihaus

**Classification: established-tradition.**

*The Adabal-a* is traditionally attributed to Morihaus and presents him as Alessia's lover/consort, though its own editor's note says the attribution cannot be historically verified. *The Song of Pelinal, Volume 5* likewise treats Morihaus's love for Perrif/Alessia as famous tradition and describes Morihaus as Kyne's son/man-bull.

VIGILANT therefore does not invent the Alessia–Morihaus relationship. Its memory scenes are a mod-specific dramatization of an established mytho-historical tradition.

Licensed sources:
- *The Adabal-a* — Oblivion / Skyrim / ESO.
- *The Song of Pelinal, Volume 5: On His Love of Morihaus* — Knights of the Nine / Skyrim / ESO.

### Belharza as child of Alessia and Morihaus / ancestor of minotaurs

**Classification: compatible-contested.**

ESO's *On Minotaurs* argues that minotaurs descend from Alessia and Morihaus through their son **Belharza the Man-Bull**, and interprets the Belharza Stone as showing proto-Alessian persecution of minotaurs.

ESO's *The Truth of Minotaurs* explicitly attacks that argument, saying the claimed minotaur descent from Alessia and Morihaus has no historical basis and instead says Belharza died on elven spears.

Therefore VIGILANT's Belharza testimony—Alessia as his mother and Morihaus as his father—fits a licensed tradition, but Lorekeeper must not present that tradition as uncontested objective history.

Licensed sources:
- *On Minotaurs* — ESO.
- *The Truth of Minotaurs* — ESO: Summerset.
- *The Song of Pelinal, Volume 5* — identifies Morihaus as a man-bull and records his love for Alessia.


### Belharza and the Amulet of Kings

**Classification: established-tradition for succession/wearing; vigilant-extension for protective mechanism.**

Licensed tradition has Belharza succeeding Alessia as the second Emperor and wearing/inheriting the Amulet of Kings after her death. That makes VIGILANT-Belharza's statement that his doubts vanished when the Amulet "recognized" him broadly compatible with established imperial/Dragon-blood tradition.

VIGILANT goes further when Belharza says the Alessian Order **could not kill him because of the Amulet**. The compared licensed material establishes dynastic/covenant significance and eligibility to wear the Amulet; it does not establish an automatic personal anti-assassination effect. That protection claim therefore remains VIGILANT-specific testimony.

### Alessian persecution / dispossession of Belharza and minotaurs

**Classification: compatible-contested + vigilant-extension.**

*On Minotaurs* interprets the Belharza Stone as evidence that proto-Alessian forces attacked or drove off minotaurs. This provides licensed support for the broad idea of anti-minotaur Alessian violence.

VIGILANT becomes much more specific: Belharza says the Order stripped him of power, mutilated him, and created a puppet from his horn. Those mechanisms and autobiographical details are **VIGILANT-specific** unless later source work finds a licensed parallel.

### Morihaus as an unstable accumulation of stories/spirits

**Classification: vigilant-extension.**

Licensed traditions used in this pass portray Morihaus as a demigod, son of Kyne, winged bull/man-bull, military leader, and Alessia's lover. They do not establish VIGILANT-Belharza's theory that Morihaus is an unstable vessel accumulated from anecdotes and spirits.

That claim belongs to VIGILANT's larger motif of unstable identity and should remain attributed to Belharza.

## Pelinal Whitestrake

### Pelinal's extreme violence and Madness

**Classification: established-tradition.**

*The Song of Pelinal* already describes Pelinal's devastating campaigns, villages and forts washed in blood, slaughter of Ayleid captives, and recurrent "Madness" capable of consuming whole swaths of land until Alessia/the gods intervene.

VIGILANT therefore builds on an existing violent and unstable Pelinal tradition rather than inventing that characterization.

Licensed sources:
- *The Song of Pelinal, Volume 4: On His Deeds*
- *The Song of Pelinal, Volume 6: On His Madness*

### Pelinal and Morihaus as family-like companions

**Classification: established-tradition.**

*The Song of Pelinal, Volume 5* calls their family-like relationship famous and says Pelinal loved Morihaus and called him nephew.

VIGILANT's linked Pelinal/Morihaus memory cycle is therefore compatible with the licensed relationship.

### VIGILANT's exact remorse sequence, pregnant captive, and Final Night

**Classification: vigilant-extension / reframing.**

The compared licensed Pelinal sources support violence, Madness, Morihaus, Alessia, Umaril, and an unstable mythic record. They do **not** establish the exact VIGILANT scenes involving the pregnant captive, the particular remorse/domestic vision, or the *Final Night* encounter.

Those are VIGILANT additions, presented inside a memory system that itself warns the player about alteration, forgetting, and temporal intrusion.

## Marukh and the Alessian tradition

### Marukh's vision/contact with Alessia and the Seventy-Seven doctrines

**Classification: established-tradition, with in-universe uncertainty.**

Licensed sources present Marukh as a prophet whose teachings led to the Alessian Order and associate his doctrines with a vision or posthumous manifestation of Alessia. *The Illusion of Death* describes the shade of Al-Esh instructing Marukh while he records the Seventy-Seven Inflexible Doctrines.

VIGILANT's use of Alessia-memory → Marukh is therefore grounded in existing religious/mythic tradition.

Licensed sources:
- *The Illusion of Death* — ESO.
- *The Exclusionary Mandates* — ESO.
- later histories of the Alessian Order.

### Exclusionary/anti-Aldmeri theology

**Classification: established-tradition.**

*The Exclusionary Mandates* explicitly frames Proper-Life around expunging the "Aldmeri Taint," a unitary Akatosh, and sacred expungement. VIGILANT's harsh Alessian ideology therefore has a direct licensed doctrinal basis.

This does not validate every act attributed to VIGILANT's inquisitors; it validates the ideological source material they are drawing on.

### Malada and Alessian holy violence

**Classification: established-tradition used as setting substrate.**

*The Cleansing of the Fane* is an Alessian chronicle describing Abbot Cosmas and the Brothers of Marukh attacking **Malada** with holy fire and destroying relics/books there. VIGILANT prominently uses Malada, Abbot Cosmas, Marukhite institutions, inquisitors, and related geography.

This is a particularly strong example of VIGILANT constructing playable spaces around obscure licensed lore rather than creating the names from nothing.

### The restored/fake Adabal, soul-filled Stone, seventy-seven rituals, Dulsa/unborn-child sacrifice, and Molag Bal theft

**Classification: vigilant-extension / reframing.**

The compared licensed Marukh texts establish Alessia's shade, Seventy-Seven doctrines, Proper-Life, and Marukhite extremism. They do not establish VIGILANT's specific plot in which a Stone/Adabal is restored and filled through souls, ritual sacrifice, war/plague, and Molag Bal's intervention.

VIGILANT deliberately embeds licensed Marukhite books beside this plot, but those reprinted texts are **context**, not independent confirmation of VIGILANT's added Stone narrative.

## Lamae Bal

### Molag Bal, Lamae, and the origin of vampirism

**Classification: established-tradition.**

*Opusculus Lamae Bal ta Mezzamortie* describes Molag Bal assaulting the Nedic Lamae Beolfag as part of his contest with Arkay; she dies, rises from the funeral pyre, and becomes the Blood-Matron from whom vampire abominations spread.

VIGILANT's identification of Lamae as the Blood Matron cursed through Molag Bal is therefore grounded directly in licensed Elder Scrolls tradition.

### Fighting Lamae and freeing her from Molag Bal's curse

**Classification: vigilant-extension.**

The VIGILANT quest sequence *Empty Cells → Remnants → The Blood Matron* and the objective to free Lamae from Molag Bal's curse are a mod-specific storyline. They should not be retroactively treated as an event in official TES chronology.


## Coldharbour as a realm of recurrence

### Realm shaped by Molag Bal

**Classification: established-tradition as substrate.**

ESO's *On the Nature of Coldharbour* describes the plane as a cold, largely lifeless realm of fear where lost souls are tormented, and presents its form as reflecting Molag Bal's nature and purposes. VIGILANT's use of Coldharbour as a landscape organized around domination, suffering, captured souls, and Molag Bal's authorship therefore has strong licensed substrate.

### Re-forming bodies and repeated existence

**Classification: compatible substrate + vigilant-reframing.**

ESO's *Chaotic Creatia: The Azure Plasm* explains that Daedric vestiges rebuild bodies from Coldharbour's creatia and that Soul Shriven can repeatedly reform counterfeit bodies. This provides a licensed mechanism for recurring embodiments in Coldharbour.

VIGILANT's much broader system of repeated historical roles, replayed memories, dream recursion, modified recollection, and the player entering Alessian-era scenes is **not** established by that source. Lorekeeper should distinguish the official reconstitution mechanism from VIGILANT's mod-specific memory/dream architecture.

Licensed sources:
- *On the Nature of Coldharbour* — ESO.
- *Chaotic Creatia: The Azure Plasm* — ESO.
- *On Soul Shriven* and related ESO material are useful follow-up sources for the soul/vestige layer.

## How VIGILANT uses official lore

The emerging pattern is not simple contradiction. VIGILANT frequently:

1. selects obscure or disputed licensed texts;
2. turns textual history into explorable spaces and characters;
3. literalizes metaphors or theological concepts into quest mechanics;
4. places mutually incompatible testimonies beside one another;
5. then destabilizes the entire historical layer through dreams, altered memories, repeating roles, and uncertain identities.

For Lorekeeper, a question such as **"Was Belharza really the son of Alessia and Morihaus?"** should return the VIGILANT testimony, the licensed source supporting that tradition, the licensed source disputing it, and no forced single verdict.
