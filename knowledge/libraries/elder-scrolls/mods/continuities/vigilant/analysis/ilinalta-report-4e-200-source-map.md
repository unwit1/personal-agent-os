## Direct observations recorded by the report

The report identifies the recovered body as **Julia Bruiant**, using her ring for identification.

It records:
- cause of death: **severe burns**;
- the corpse was still **burning from within** when recovered;
- a curse remained on the corpse;
- Julia's blood had become **acidic and poisonous**;
- contact with the blood killed surrounding life;
- the curse was dispelled with unusually little resistance;
- the remains were purified with the **light of Stendarr**;
- the ashes were scattered.

These details independently corroborate Marcus Bruiant's later documentary timeline in which Julia disappears and Jacob returns with her ring.

## Observation versus inference

One sentence needs an explicit reliability split.

The report says the curse's lack of resistance **suggested** that its caster wanted or allowed the curse to be lifted.

That is the investigator's inference.

Direct:
- the curse was unusually easy to dispel.

Inferred by the report writer:
- the caster intentionally allowed the dispelling because they planned a later ritual.

Lorekeeper should not convert that hypothesis into an established action or motive.

## What the report does not resolve

The document does not identify:
- who cursed Julia;
- who inflicted the fatal burns;
- whether curse and fatal injury came from the same actor;
- what later ritual, if any, was intended;
- whether the later **Julia the Crimson** quest form is Julia resurrected, reconstructed, copied, or manifested by another mechanism.

Those questions belong to the wider Bruiant Mansion / Child of Oblivion reconstruction.

## Environmental context

The report opens by saying bodies floating in Lake Ilinalta had become common since the civil war began, while Julia's corpse was unusual enough to justify increased patrols.

This gives the document a grounded investigative frame: Julia's supernatural condition stands out against an already violent regional background.
