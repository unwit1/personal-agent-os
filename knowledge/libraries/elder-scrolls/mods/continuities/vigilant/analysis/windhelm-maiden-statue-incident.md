# Windhelm Maiden Statue incident — reconstruction

Continuity: `tes.mod.vigilant`

Primary sources:
- `BOOK 02038AAE` — Windhelm Report 4E 180
- `BOOK 0201C7F7` — Rahel's Journal
- `BOOK 02038AAC` — Jacob's warning note
- `BOOK 02038AAD` — Aredhel's Note
- `INFO 0200EA82` / **Old Regrets** — Jacob's Molag Bal bargain testimony

## Why this incident matters

The Windhelm Maiden Statue event is a major bridge connecting:
- Jacob;
- Rahel;
- Aredhel;
- the Blood Matron;
- Molag Bal;
- Altano's later Vigilant history;
- the *Old Regrets* bargain;
- VIGILANT's recurring death/revival ambiguity.

The surviving sources disagree in important ways. The disagreement should be preserved as evidence rather than “fixed.”

## Timeline

### 8 Second Seed 4E 180

The Windhelm report says:
- all prisoners in the jail disappeared;
- the guards were missing.

This establishes the crisis before the Vigilants arrive.

### 9 Second Seed

A vampire is captured in the dungeons.

Windhelm authorities:
- suffer casualties;
- order cremation of the dead;
- request assistance from the Vigilants of Stendarr.

### 10 Second Seed

The dead are cremated.

No vampirism is detected among those bodies.

### 11 Second Seed

The Vigilants arrive.

Their investigation concludes that the **Maiden Statue** is the source/path of the vampire attacks.

Authorities begin interrogating the captured vampire about the hidden door behind the statue.

### 12 Second Seed

The vampire reveals that the statue reacts to vampire blood.

The prisoner dies during interrogation.

The authorities open the statue with vampire blood and send:
- seven Vigilants;
- thirteen guards

into the hidden route.

Rahel's journal, dated the same day, says Jacob has been gone for two days hunting vampires beneath Windhelm and she fears for his safety.

Later that day:
- screaming comes from the statue;
- it seals itself;
- vampire blood no longer opens it;
- the expedition's status becomes unknown.

### 13 Second Seed

Jacob is found unconscious outside the city walls.

He tells authorities:
- the investigation team died in a tunnel collapse;
- the collapse destroyed the vampires' route.

The official investigation is terminated.

## Jacob's warning note

Jacob's separate note is much more alarming than the official report.

He warns that if the Maiden Statue ever opens, **“her awakening”** has occurred.

He describes the threat as:
- defiler of Arkay;
- mistake of Stendarr;
- dangerous to see, hear, or approach;
- capable of dragging victims to Blood Mist Castle;
- connected to the “Lord of Corruption.”

This implies Jacob left the expedition with knowledge of a much larger supernatural structure than the official Windhelm report records.

The note does not name “her.”

## Aredhel's version

Aredhel's note says:
- **Jacob was killed in battle**;
- Joshua fled;
- the remaining Vigilants surrendered to the **Blood Matron**;
- the Blood Matron shared her blood with them;
- Aredhel alone accepted willingly;
- the others continued resisting vampiric bloodthirst.

This is the strongest source conflict.

### Official account
Jacob survives and reports a tunnel collapse.

### Aredhel account
Jacob dies in battle and the survivors fall into the Blood Matron's power.

Both accounts are preserved by VIGILANT.

## Joshua's own account

Joshua's final note provides a fourth in-plugin perspective on the failed expedition.

`BOOK 02031119` / **Joshua's Final Note** says:
- a door sealed behind him;
- he abandoned his friends and tried to escape;
- despite believing he could face any vampire, he could not stand before “her” and ran;
- he turns his anger toward Stendarr;
- he hears a lullaby and believes the woman is approaching.

This directly corroborates Aredhel's claim that **Joshua fled**.

It also sharpens the disaster topology:

- **Jacob** — death/near-death → Molag Bal bargain;
- **Aredhel** — surrender → willingly accepts Blood Matron blood;
- **Joshua** — flight → isolation → apparent final pursuit;
- **other Vigilants** — surrender but resist bloodthirst, according to Aredhel.

The note itself never names “her,” so the Blood Matron/Lamae identification remains contextual rather than literal text.

## Jacob's Old Regrets testimony

Jacob later says:
- he was defeated and dying;
- Molag Bal offered to revive him;
- the price was his wife's soul;
- Jacob accepted.

This provides a plausible mechanism by which:
- Aredhel could truthfully remember Jacob dying;
- Jacob could later appear alive in the official/public aftermath.

However, the source does **not** explicitly say:
- the bargain happened during the Maiden Statue expedition;
- Jacob was already fully dead rather than dying;
- the Windhelm report was intentionally falsified;
- Molag Bal physically transported Jacob outside the city.

Therefore the link is **strongly suggestive but not fully explicit**.

## Supplementary intended-background resolution

The a123999 VIGILANT background article explicitly describes the intended sequence as:

**Jacob and his fellows die encountering half-sleeping Lamae → Jacob sacrifices Rahel's soul to Molag Bal → Jacob receives a new body → Jacob continues as a Vigilant.**

Source:
- `sources/supplementary/a123999-vigilant-backgrounds.md`

This is not a direct ESM record, so the contradictory in-plugin source layers remain preserved.

It does, however, raise **Model A — literal death and revival** from a mere reconstruction to a strongly supported supplementary interpretation.

## Rahel as the price

Rahel's journal resumes much later.

By 8 Frostfall 4E 182:
- she wakes in their ruined house;
- Jacob is absent;
- she believes he betrayed her.

Two days later:
- a follower of Molag Bal gives her a mace;
- she receives magical power/knowledge;
- she is ordered to spread discord.

Jacob's statement that he traded his wife's soul makes this sequence especially significant.

The exact metaphysical meaning of “Rahel's soul” remains unresolved:
- soul ownership;
- corruption;
- spiritual claim;
- transformation;
- eventual BalGhost role;
- Stone relationship.

The journal records consequences, not the mechanism.

## Blood Matron identity boundary

Aredhel explicitly names the **Blood Matron** as the power to whom the surviving Vigilants surrendered.

VIGILANT's Blood Matron arc centers on **Lamae Bal** and culminates in freeing her from Molag Bal's curse.

This makes Lamae the strongest current identity anchor for “Blood Matron.”

However, Jacob's Maiden Statue note does not directly name Lamae.

Therefore:
- **Aredhel's Blood Matron → Lamae cluster: strong**
- **Maiden Statue's unnamed “her” → exactly Lamae: plausible but not independently explicit in the note**

Keep that distinction.

## Aredhel's transformation

Aredhel's early VIGILANT form is:
- a captured/corrupted Vigilant;
- part of **Remnants**;
- associated with Aredhel's Note.

Her note says she willingly accepted the Blood Matron's blood.

This creates a high-confidence origin for her Blood Matron corruption.

Her much later Coldharbour/Diagna-associated manifestation should remain a separate later layer.

## Jacob after 4E 180

Whatever happened in Windhelm, Jacob later appears in multiple dated contexts:

### 4E 197
Altano says Jacob rescues him from a snowfield and gives him the name Altano.

### 4E 200
Marcus Bruiant says a Vigilant named Jacob brings Julia's ring and reports on her burned remains near Lake Ilinalta.

This means the post-Windhelm Jacob persists for at least two decades.

That persistence strongly raises the importance of Jacob's revival bargain.

## Possible reconstruction classes

Lorekeeper should preserve several models.

### Model A — literal death and revival
Aredhel sees Jacob die → Molag Bal revives him for Rahel's soul → Jacob is found outside Windhelm and gives the collapse story.

Supported by:
- Aredhel's death statement;
- Jacob's revival bargain;
- later survival.

Not explicitly confirmed as one continuous event.

### Model B — near-death described as death
Aredhel believes Jacob dead → Jacob is actually dying → Molag Bal intervenes → official report follows.

Also compatible.

### Model C — unreliable/partial accounts
The Windhelm report, Aredhel, and Jacob each preserve incomplete or distorted versions.

VIGILANT frequently uses unreliable testimony, but no source explicitly labels this incident a memory distortion.

### Current best practice

Do **not** collapse the event into one definitive chronology.

Store:
- official report;
- Aredhel testimony;
- Jacob testimony;
- Rahel chronology;
- inferred bridge with confidence.
