# Coldharbour memory sequence — reliability map

Continuity: `tes.mod.vigilant`

The 13 `zzzCHMemoryQuest##` records are best treated as a **memory/dream narrative**, not a neutral chronological archive. The ESM itself supplies the reason: characters speak of memories being modified, waking souls forgetting, repeating dreams, unstable identities, false or reconstructed Adabal material, and visitors who are explicitly "not of this time and place."

## Sequence

1. **The Grand Inquisitor** (`0212C4F4`) — Inquisitor Pepe confronts a figure he alternately identifies with Mara/Alessia. His monologue links the Stone, hunger, salvation, Alessian authority, rewritten teachings, the denial of free will, and a promised Tower. This is polemical testimony from an inquisitor inside a memory.
2. **The Mad King** (`0213712B`) — a short Dro'Zel memory centered on a bard's tragic tale of Polydor and Eloisa. Its placement helps establish the recurring bard/story mechanism but the currently resolved dialogue does not justify a larger historical reconstruction.
3. **Knight of Hounds** (`0213965A`) — Emperor Belharza orders expansion toward Malada and explicitly demands the killing of Ayleids without distinction of age or sex. Varla's compassion and departure provide the counterpoint. Later Man-Bull Belharza testimony must be kept separate rather than silently harmonized with this memory.
4. **Johan the Fool** (`02140225`) — after Martha's death, a bard calling himself Bal offers Johan a mace and a program of collecting sinners' souls. Later dialogue grants Johan a final wish to burn everything he built and consigns him to Oblivion. The sequence strongly parallels VIGILANT's larger Molag Bal / mace / soul economy, but the unresolved bard identity remains provenance-tagged rather than assumed solely from the name.
5. **Adabal** (`0205AE03`) — Marukh says seventy-seven rituals will restore the lost Stone of Al-Esh and proposes completing them with Dulsa's blood and that of her unborn child. He says a vision in the Stone justifies the sacrifice and calls Adabal "bread for the starving."
6. **Remains of the Miracle** (`0206A23B`) — an older Inquisitor Pepe says the Stone was filled through wars and plagues, calls it a fake Adabal, and says Molag Bal stole it from the Tower. This is a retrospective account by a participant, not a neutral narrator.
7. **Temptation of Marukh** (`0206F53C`) — a dying wanderer encounters the soul-consuming Stone; a Molag Bal-linked form then tells the player that escape requires submission and filling the Stone with souls, explicitly offering to modify memory. A separate Alessia memory orders Marukh to restore and fill the Adabal. This quest is direct evidence that apparent memories in the sequence can be manipulated.
8. **The Nameless Bard** (`02080E91`) — the memory-tail narrator recounts Lamae material, speaks of eternally repeating dreams, and eventually identifies himself as Molag Bal, Prince of domination and enslavement. The quest blends confession, narration, role changes, and self-identification; it should not be flattened into a literal chronological autobiography without further evidence.
9. **Beyond the Shores of Madness** (`022CAE30`) — identity collapses further. Unresolved narration asks who the speaker was; a Sheogorath-linked memory calls Molag Bal a beast furthest from Shezarr and tells the waking soul it will forget everything, after which all will seem a dream. This is among the strongest reliability warnings in the ESM.
10. **Pelinal the Bloody** (`022A532E`) — a bard confronts Pelinal with the aftermath of Umaril and a pregnant captive. Pelinal's resolved monologue acknowledges massacres of villages, women, and children, then turns toward remorse and an image of domestic peace. Separate Pelinal forms and narration are retained as distinct evidence layers.
11. **After the Storm** (`022B9BAB`) — Morihaus first mourns Pelinal and speaks of love, then the same memory corpus contains orders to kill captives and citizens and a declaration of bloodlust. The contradiction is evidence; Lorekeeper does not choose a single harmonized version prematurely.
12. **The Final Night** (`022BC395`) — Pelinal, Alessia, Morihaus, and an obscured **Akatosh-memory form** displayed as `???????` appear in a quiet final sequence. The figure recognizes the visitor as out of time and place and warns that too much involvement will prevent departure, explicitly framing the episode as temporal/memory intrusion rather than ordinary presence. The structural role is ESM-backed; a123999 supplementary commentary further describes this figure as an **avatar of Akatosh** whose memory was lost by people but retained by “the Lizard.” See `entities/characters/akatosh-memory.md`.
13. **Paravania the Man-Bull** (`0251C038`) — the QUST record exists as the thirteenth memory step, but the current INFO resolver finds no directly owned dialogue. Its narrative content should therefore be reconstructed from scenes, aliases, placed actors, and transitions rather than guessed from the title.

## Reliability classes

### A — structural fact
Record relationships such as quest ownership, scene actor aliases, explicit speaker fields, worldspace/cell names, and quest objective text.

### B — present-tense character testimony
A named/resolved character makes a claim in ordinary dialogue. Strong evidence for what that character says or believes, not automatically for objective history.

### C — memory testimony
A resolved speaker makes a claim inside one of the memory quests. Preserve the speaker, quest, and form variant with the claim.

### D — manipulated/unstable memory
Material in sequences that explicitly involve memory alteration, dream recursion, identity loss, or out-of-time visitors. Treat causal/metaphysical claims as especially provisional.

### E — unresolved voice / symbolic narration
Text whose speaker cannot yet be structurally identified or whose scene function is symbolic. Do not assign it to a character from wording alone.

## Core implication

VIGILANT does not present its Alessian-era material as a clean replacement history. The ESM repeatedly foregrounds **how history is remembered, rewritten, weaponized, dreamed, and consumed**. Lorekeeper should therefore answer questions such as "what happened to Marukh?" with source-separated accounts instead of manufacturing one canonical VIGILANT timeline.
