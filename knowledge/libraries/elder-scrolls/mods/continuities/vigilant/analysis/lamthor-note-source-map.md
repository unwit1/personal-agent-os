Signed by:

## Direct observation

Lamthor reports that after the arrival of the **Wild Elf**, Julius has been acting increasingly strangely.

He says he personally saw Julius:
- playing with the dogs in the courtyard;
- muttering strange incantations to them.

These are Lamthor's direct observations.

## Accusation against Bal

Lamthor concludes that the Wild Elf must have taught Julius the incantations.

That is an accusation/inference.

Within the broader mansion chronology, Marcus identifies the newly hired Wild Elf butler as **Bal**, making Bal the clear referent for Lamthor's “Wild Elf.”

However, the note does not demonstrate the teaching event directly.

Correct evidence split:

Direct:
- Bal/Wild Elf had arrived;
- Julius behaved strangely;
- Julius muttered incantations to the dogs.

Lamthor's inference:
- the Wild Elf taught Julius those incantations.

## Concern about Julius

Lamthor frames the situation primarily as a threat to **young master Julius's future**.

He asks Marcus to dismiss the Wild Elf.

This shows that at least one servant recognized the Julius/Bal relationship as dangerous before the later mansion crisis fully escalated.

## Relationship to Lamthor's death

Marcus later records Lamthor being eaten by his own dogs after they enter a frenzied state.

Bartholo says he detected magic around the later deaths.

The juxtaposition is significant because Lamthor had earlier seen Julius addressing dogs with incantations.

But this note does not prove:
- Julius caused Lamthor's death;
- Bal caused it;
- the earlier dogs are the exact same animals;
- the incantations were frenzy magic.

Keep the later death as a strong narrative echo, not a direct causal statement from this document.
