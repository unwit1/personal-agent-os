## Core position

Ja'zhan rejects what the Alessians call **harmony** if that harmony requires or excuses suffering by innocent children.

He invokes three child-suffering images:
- a child trapped in a cold dark latrine;
- a child lost in searing red mist;
- a child struggling against a rope.

The prayers are directed respectively toward:
- Alkosh;
- S'rendarr;
- Lorkhaj.

## Relationship to the trilogy

The cold-latrine image strongly recalls *What Khajiit Saw*.

The other two images expand the argument beyond that one case.

Do not assume all three images describe:
- the same child;
- events from the same family;
- one chronological sequence.

## Atonement argument

Ja'zhan argues that suffering cannot be absorbed into an abstract doctrine of harmony while the victims' tears remain unanswered and unatoned.

This is his explicit moral/theological stance.

It provides a direct countervoice to:
- Alessian institutional order;
- Amicus's coercive heaven;
- doctrines that subordinate individuals to historical or metaphysical projects.

## Chopping-block language

Ja'zhan says he would maintain this rejection even on a chopping block.

That establishes resolve and opposition, but does not by itself prove a later execution scene.
