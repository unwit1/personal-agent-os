# Petition to House Tharn — ideology sequence

Continuity: `tes.mod.vigilant`

Primary sources:
- `BOOK 0251D5CB` — **Petition to House Tharn, Vol. 11**
- `BOOK 0251D5CC` — **Petition to House Tharn, Vol. 23**
- `BOOK 0251D5CD` — **Petition to House Tharn, Vol. 68**
- `BOOK 0251D5CE` — **Petition to House Tharn, Vol. 77**

All four are credited to **Unknown** and preserve dialogue between **Pelan** and **Amicus**.

This page treats them as a single ideological sequence while preserving speaker attribution.

## Vol. 11 — history as something that can be manufactured

The immediate dispute is **Belharza**.

Pelan argues that:
- Belharza's birth does not invalidate him;
- the **Amulet of Kings** chose him;
- that choice should matter more than Alessian prejudice.

Amicus rejects Belharza's accepted identity and proposes a replacement.

His claims include:
- belief determines divine form;
- memories can be fabricated;
- history can be fabricated;
- an acceptable Belharza can be manufactured;
- inconvenient Ayleid witnesses can be eliminated because they remember older forms of Alessia, Morihaus, and Pelinal;
- the Order can make a god aligned with its preferred truth.

This volume establishes **historical revision as an intentional program**, not merely later accidental forgetting.

## Vol. 23 — exclusion becomes cosmological program

The dispute expands from Belharza to citizenship and species.

Pelan argues for Alessia's empire as a multi-racial project built through cooperation.

Amicus instead supports:
- removal of rights from elves, giants, minotaurs, and other non-humans;
- a human-only empire;
- separating **Auriel** from **Akatosh**;
- erasing elven history;
- destroying non-human peoples;
- reforging the **Heart of Shezarr**;
- remaking the Aurbis with **artificial Flowers**;
- creating a human-only heaven beyond divine control;
- orienting that future toward the **Blue Star**.

These are Amicus's ideological and metaphysical claims. The text does not independently prove that his proposed cosmology is achievable.

## Vol. 68 — the Prisoner becomes the enemy of constructed reality

The focus shifts to anomalous prisoners whom the Order executes despite uncertain identity.

Amicus describes the **Prisoner** as able to:
- follow prophecies in the Books of Stars and Frost;
- resist imposed fate;
- create the world/future they choose in each moment.

Pelan interprets this kind of figure as a potential Shezarrine/savior.

Amicus rejects that salvation specifically because it would destroy what the Order has built.

This produces the central conflict:

**Prisoner freedom → threatens → Alessian constructed order**

Amicus then argues that:
- law and morality are forms of verbal violence;
- the Order needs neither forgiveness nor punishment;
- its rule is illuminated by the **Stone of Fire**.

The volume therefore connects political authority, metaphysical determinism, and Stone imagery.

## Vol. 77 — doctrine dissolves into memory failure and Stone selection

The final volume destabilizes the foundation of the earlier ideology.

Amicus and Pelan realize they cannot actually remember the **Seventy-Seven Doctrines** they repeatedly invoked.

Pelan recalls:
- Marukh was already dead at the cliff;
- the cliff was covered in blood;
- other Imga traced blood stains and produced more than seventy-seven interpretations of a single image.

Amicus reframes this as:
- no single remembered truth;
- surviving records without stable memory;
- interpretation replacing doctrine.

The dialogue then introduces a striking memory contradiction:
- Marukh is said to have been dead;
- Pelan nevertheless remembers returning to the Imperial City with him;
- Marukh says **“Auriel is not worthy”**;
- he vomits up a burning Stone and becomes a mangled corpse again.

Amicus and Pelan then speak of the Stone as having **chosen** them.

Amicus ends by presenting domination as kindness for those unable to bear freedom and tells Pelan to trust the warmth of **Stone-Fire** because “we must be burned twice.”

## Sequence model

Across the four volumes:

1. **Vol. 11:** rewrite people, memory, history, and gods.
2. **Vol. 23:** turn revisionism into exclusionary cosmology and human-only salvation.
3. **Vol. 68:** identify Prisoner freedom as an existential threat to the constructed order.
4. **Vol. 77:** reveal that the Order's own doctrine and memory are unstable and increasingly subordinate to the Stone.

This is one of VIGILANT's clearest documentary progressions from institutional ideology to memory/metaphysical collapse.

## Speaker and reliability boundaries

The volumes are **dialogue transcripts by an unknown recorder**.

Treat separately:
- **Amicus's program** — direct evidence for what Amicus argues;
- **Pelan's objections/memories** — direct evidence for Pelan's counter-position and recollection;
- **metaphysical statements** — in-universe claims, not automatically objective cosmology;
- **memory contradictions in Vol. 77** — evidence of unstable or manipulated memory, not a chronology to be silently repaired.

Do not turn:
- Amicus's claim that belief reshapes gods;
- his Prisoner theory;
- his Blue Star/artificial Flowers program;
- the Stone choosing the Order

into universal facts without corroborating evidence.
