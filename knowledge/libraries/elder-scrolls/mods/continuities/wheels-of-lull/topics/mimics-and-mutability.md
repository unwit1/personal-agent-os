# Mimics and artificial mutability

Continuity: `tes.mod.wheels-of-lull`

BOOK `05371593`, *De Rerum Mutabilitatis* by **Quintus Sextus Septus VIII**, presents a short natural-history account of creatures called **Mimics**.

## Claims made by the treatise

The author says:

- the last known Mimic had recently been killed by an adventurer;
- Mimics could voluntarily change form and remain motionless for long periods;
- they were artificially created by a "mad Chimeri wizard" during the first two centuries of the First Era;
- their bodies combined flesh and metal;
- because they were created rather than naturally reproducing, their total population was finite;
- over centuries they spread across northern Tamriel, with a stated range from Morrowind to High Rock;
- they commonly adopted forms attractive to people searching old rooms or treasure spaces;
- they did not require food but nevertheless preyed on intruders.

## Lore significance

Within Wheels, Mimics are an example of early artificial life that blurs biological and mechanical categories. That places them thematically beside fabricants, Trademarks, and other engineered organisms, although the book does not claim a common creator or technical lineage.

The title emphasizes mutability, but the surviving text is primarily a creature-history account rather than a general metaphysical theory.
