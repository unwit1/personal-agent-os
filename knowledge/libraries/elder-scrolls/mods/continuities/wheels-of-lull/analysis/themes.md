# Themes and structural interpretation

Continuity: `tes.mod.wheels-of-lull`

## Maintenance versus apocalypse

The story repeatedly juxtaposes mundane repair work—gates, mines, smithing, tram operation, mechanism slots—with world-scale failure. Chronographers describe themselves less as heroes than as maintainers who fix a fault and move on.

## Identity mediated by machinery

Fabricant bodies, pneuma, Rewound bodies, the Stoker in Avarri's mind, Ganaran's proxy FRF, Wailway soul-engines, Archeron's self-modification, and Memory all blur the boundary between person and mechanism.

## Unreliable certainty

The continuity repeatedly makes certainty itself a problem:
- Llavados performs madness.
- Numinar makes a reasoned but wrong accusation.
- Archeron wears Fyr's identity.
- Hammar speaks confidently where Yagrum explicitly remains uncertain.
- scholarly books retain rumor markers.
- forged and satirical texts coexist with technical manuals.

The provenance model therefore mirrors the story: identity and truth should be reconstructed from multiple evidence layers rather than accepted from a single surface.

## Memory and persistence

Memory is both a character and a repeated motif. Memodermis reacts to memories; Memory is designed for survival after catastrophe; Wailway victims retain painful identity inside machines; Rewound bodies retain behavior after pneuma departure; Chronographers preserve temporal integrity.

## Repairing the future

Chronography's "integrity preservation," Tower maintenance, Snow-Throat's crisis, and Memory's future purpose all frame the present as maintenance work performed so a future remains possible.
