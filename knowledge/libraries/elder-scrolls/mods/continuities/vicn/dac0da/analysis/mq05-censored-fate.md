# Censored Fate — quest reconstruction

**Quest:** `zDcdMq05`  
**Title:** Censored Fate  
**Source:** `DAc0da.esm:00CA1E`  
**Continuity:** `tes.mod.vicn.dac0da`

## Direct quest structure

The final numbered main quest has three explicit objectives:

1. Go to Solitude Lighthouse.
2. Talk to `Monk`.
3. Talk to `MonkAlt`.

The alias table resolves these roles to two distinct NPC records:

- `Monk` -> `DAc0da.esm:000D77`, **Rolls-On-Roads**
- `MonkAlt` -> `DAc0da.esm:00CA1B`, **Load Error**

The records should remain distinct. The second form functions as an alternate/censored quest-state actor and should not be silently merged with Rolls-On-Roads without condition/scene evidence.

## The crisis has been "censored"

At the lighthouse, temporal distortion is now diminishing on its own and Numidium fragments have stopped reacting/resonating. Evidence: `DAc0da.esm:00CA26`, `00CA28`, `00CA2B`, `00CA2D`.

Rolls-On-Roads says he has been sent by the Psijic Order to survey the site because Numidium fragments in the Order's possession had recently been drawn toward the lighthouse. By the time of the conversation the reaction has vanished. Evidence: `DAc0da.esm:00CA3B`.

Most importantly, when the player says that they and Rolls-On-Roads banished Numidium together, he insists they have never met and rejects the story as impossible. Evidence: `DAc0da.esm:00CA3F`.

This is the direct narrative payoff to MQ04's statement that the Jills would "censor" the event and reassign participants to appropriate coordinates.

## Jill censorship, Dragon Breaks, and memory

When asked whether Jill censorship affects memory, Rolls-On-Roads answers through comparison with Dragon Break reports.

He says memory retention varies by person and that powerful individuals or people near the center of events can retain memories without necessarily understanding them. Evidence: `DAc0da.esm:00CA54`.

An alternate response says memory loss during a Dragon Break can function as a psychological defense mechanism; even those who remember may later forget without understanding what occurred. Evidence: `DAc0da.esm:00CA55`.

He then says memory loss is the best-case outcome of correction: people can suffer burned-out eyes, lost limbs, or death, and corrected Dragon Breaks can therefore be accompanied by abnormally high numbers of missing people. Evidence: `DAc0da.esm:00CA57`.

These are direct DAc0da cosmological claims attributed to Rolls-On-Roads.

## Rewritten mundane history

The player can ask about the ships that disappeared during the opening crisis.

Rolls-On-Roads says the Psijics investigated and concluded that the Imperial, Thalmor, and Solitude naval forces had been jointly hunting a stray Sload pod and were lost in an ordinary storm in the Sea of Ghosts. Evidence: `DAc0da.esm:00CA59`.

This sharply contrasts with MQ00's original crisis account involving the offshore Numidium and supports the idea that censorship replaces the impossible causal history with a self-consistent mundane one.

## Romion continuity bridge

Rolls-On-Roads says the lighthouse makes him nostalgic for an old friend named **Romion**, whom he describes as:

- an Altmer;
- a Dwemer scholar;
- someone who loved tinkering with machines;
- his former colleague;
- an apprentice contemporary;
- an admirer, together with Rolls-On-Roads, of stories about the Vanos siblings exploring Dwemer ruins.

Evidence: `DAc0da.esm:00CA49`, `00CA4A`, `00CA4C`, `00CA4E`.

GLENMORIL independently contains a named Altmer/Dwemer scholar **Romion**, making this a direct named cross-Vicn continuity bridge. DAc0da's statements remain attributable to Rolls-On-Roads and should not overwrite GLENMORIL-specific biography.

## Ending state

Rolls-On-Roads intends to take time away from Psijic work and sightsee in Solitude. He says he has an unexplained feeling that he has accomplished something great despite no longer remembering the Numidium mission. Evidence: `DAc0da.esm:00CA43`, `00CA50`.

That residual feeling is consistent with DAc0da's broader model in which corrected events can lose coherent memory while leaving traces in people, artifacts, and causal history.
