# Combat perk guardrails

Continuity: tes.mod.vicn.unslaad

Some custom perk/spell names look lore-heavy but are used broadly enough that they should be treated as technical scaffolding.

## Dragonskin

Custom perk:
- zzzCrbcrResistNPC50
- display: **Dragonskin**

It appears on many unrelated actors, including:
- Austella;
- Khev;
- Jill bosses;
- Pelinaalilargus;
- Dov-Ah-Kiin;
- Atmoran trial bosses;
- Clone of Ayrenn;
- others.

Therefore:

**having this perk is not evidence that an actor is biologically a dragon, Dragonborn, or part of one bloodline.**

It is best classified as a resistance/boss-balance perk.

## Boss Difficulty / Damage Difficulty

These custom spells/perks occur across a very large number of bosses and clearly serve scaling/difficulty functions.

They should never be promoted into character lore.

## Increase Detection

A custom detection perk is shared by many bosses.

It is encounter AI scaffolding, not heightened metaphysical perception unless corroborated by another source.

## Damage-by-player multiplier perks

Some actors use custom x5/x10 player-damage perks.

These are combat tuning and should not become durability or immortality lore.

## Fall-damage cancel

Hushed and Sinak forms often share a fall-damage-cancel perk.

That is useful locomotion/body implementation context, but it does not establish a shared ancestry by itself.

## General rule

When a custom perk:
- appears across many unrelated actors;
- has obvious difficulty/AI utility;
- lacks player-facing narrative text;

classify it as **technical**.

Only promote it to lore when:
- the effect is unique or narrowly shared;
- the names align with other direct evidence;
- placement/dialogue/quest context independently supports the interpretation.
