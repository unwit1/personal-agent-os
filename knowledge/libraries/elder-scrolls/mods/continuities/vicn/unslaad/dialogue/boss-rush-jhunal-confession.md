## Speaker attribution

The speaking quest alias is OwlTA.

That alias's VTCK/source actor points to NPC 0309D3E6 — **Jhunal the Gray**.

This is the same Gray-Owl/Jhunal representation family used elsewhere in UNSLAAD. Because OwlTA is a talking-activator style alias rather than a fixed body, retain the distinction between **speaker representation** and literal body identity.

## Failed experiments

The speaker says:
- Bard;
- Laza;
- Ulliss

were all failures, but that he has finally reached "the real thing."

This directly adds **Laza** to the Gray Owl's experimental history and creates an especially important VIGILANT retrieval link, where Jhunal the Owl independently says he used Laza to harvest dragons.

## Jhunal-name confession

The speaker says he has been using the name **Jhunal for thousands of years**.

He then says people can no longer distinguish:
- their god;
- himself.

He attributes this to the disappearance of anyone who still remembers the difference.

This is the clearest UNSLAAD evidence that the Gray Owl figure is **not simply identical to the original/deific Jhunal by default**. It directly supports the Hermit's separate statement that a man "pretended to be Jhunal" when influencing Austella.

## Forgetting

The speaker says:
- everyone forgot him;
- isolation nearly drove him mad;
- one dream kept him sane.

This tightly connects identity appropriation with UNSLAAD's recurring memory/forgetting mechanism.

## Dragon dream

His surviving dream is to:
- become a dragon;
- fly through the sky;
- feel wind in his wings.

He calls it a childish dream and says it is the last thing he still possesses.

This makes the later Black Owl distinction between the Gray Owl's **dream** and **wish** more intelligible: becoming a dragon is explicitly the dream he refuses to abandon.

## Identity model

Current high-confidence model:

1. the active Gray Owl/Jhunal representation has used the name **Jhunal** for millennia;
2. he admits there was originally a meaningful distinction between himself and "their god";
3. collective forgetting has erased that distinction socially;
4. he is still structurally and narratively entangled with the wider Owl/Jhunal network.

Lorekeeper should therefore avoid the two extremes:
- **do not** treat Gray Owl as merely unrelated to Jhunal;
- **do not** treat every statement about original/deific Jhunal as automatically biography of this impersonating/appropriating figure.
