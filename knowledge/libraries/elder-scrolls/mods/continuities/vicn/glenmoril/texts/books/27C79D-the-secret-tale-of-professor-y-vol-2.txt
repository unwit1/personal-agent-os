The Secret Tale, Vol 2: The Seed

Assistant: Is this giant sphere the Egg Ja'bal was talking about? Looking at it makes me feel strangely nostalgic as if I should be familiar with it ...

Professor Y: No, this is not the Egg. It's a ship. A living ship, of all things. This must be the true identity of the round cat the Khajiit of Anequina once named Ja'bal.

Assistant: Well, it's round, that's for sure. But where are the remains of Ja'bal, then?

Professor Y: It's this ship's terminal. The reason why we feel so familiar with this ship is that we fed on the blood and flesh of its terminal. We've been assimilated and integrated into its network.

Assistant: Then ... is this Ja'bal's main body?

Professor Y: No, only a part of it, but it's not exactly appropriate to call it the main body. Ja'bal is not a specific person with merely one body. It's a mental entity. Most of it was trapped in Oblivion because of the sacrifice of the King of Anequina, but a small part of it remains within us. And it's whispering, waiting to be released.

Assistant: No wonder we can't sleep anymore. Can we extract any information from this shell? I wonder at the theories of the unknown that could be hidden within it.

Professor Y: It seems someone already beat us to it, unfortunately. Music of Apocrypha is echoing inside. It would be impossible to observe most of the information still left on the ship even if we could neutralize it.

Assistant: Judging by the decay of the music, the ship must have been entered as soon as it crashed in Anequina.

Professor Y: Your observation is correct. The catastrophe that occurred in Anequina was caused by that crash. Any organs that could have been used for communication were carefully destroyed. And this is where Ja'bal's madness began.

Assistant: Do you think Ja'bal was capable of remote communication like we Dwemer? Is it possible that another Dwemer clan could have built it?

Professor Y: I already told you this was a living ship. Building living ships is not a Dwemer hobby. Now then, when I examined the build and structure of this ship, I found something very interesting. Do you know, what?

Assistant: Don't act like this, just tell me.

Professor Y: The structure of internal organs and fluids matches that of an Argonian. The outer shell of the ship also corresponds to the bones and scales of an Argonian. Judging by this, we can already tell who, or rather what, built this ship, can't we?

Assistant: The Hist. There's no other explanation.
