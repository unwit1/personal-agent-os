The Secret Tale, Vol. 5: The Transference

Assistant: I never thought Azura would be willing to deal with the Dwemer like us.

Professor Y: She's vain, not stupid. She must have been interested in what lurks within us. Whatever the reason, it solved the problem we had and got us out of that unfortunate situation.

Assistant: No, no, it didn't! While we were being transferred to Oblivion, the eastern entertainment district was destroyed and filled with a forest. I suppose all this was too much for the city to handle.

Professor Y: Perhaps the magic released by the Alessian Order interfered, and there was not enough Magicka left for the transfer. This is why magic that bets only on appearances shouldn't be trusted.

Assistant: It's still better than failing and having the whole city razed to the ground. But we were supposed to be transferred to Moonshadow ... where are we? Black water is flooding everything. Lower parts of the city sank in the blink of an eye.

Professor Y: I don't know. But I heard the tones of Apocrypha coming from the underground during the transference. They must have been hiding in the sewers.

Assistant: And they must have changed the course to this incomprehensible place. Is this what they wanted? Did Azura know this would happen all along?

Professor Y: She presides over destiny and prophecy, she would not have made a mistake in her foretelling. There are remains of her Winged Twilights strewn around the sewers. She was actively trying to fulfill her prophecy.

Assistant: So the tide has turned, then?

Professor Y: It was changed long ago. Repeated minimal interferences, hidden in the larger flow. This millennia-old current created by the forces of Apocrypha cannot be altered with small changes anymore.

Assistant: So we can only resign to be swept along?

Professor Y: We've already transmitted our knowledge and technology to the other Dwemer facilities before this transfer. The seeds have been planted.

Assistant: So now we must wait and wait and wait for hundreds, maybe thousands of years ...

Professor Y: There is no other way to change the flow.
