## Contract / choice branch

Prompt: **...I was set up, right?**

Jhunal says:
- the player agreed, so **the contract** proceeded;
- the player could have stepped away and still can back away;
- “the only one who turns the pages of this book is you”;
- they should follow the flow because only “the life of a little louse” is at stake.

Prompt: **And what was I supposed to do instead?**

Jhunal says:
- the player could have let **the orphan** die and remained free;
- alternatively the player could have killed themself and ended everything;
- the player is here because they agreed “to me and to yourself.”

Within this branch, “the orphan” strongly points to Lalanoah from the immediately preceding topic context, but the response itself does not name her.

Prompt: **I had to. All this feels like a deception.**

Jhunal says:
- even if it is deception, judgment has already been passed;
- the player “weathered it with flying colors”;
- a **Divines' seal** was given;
- the arrangement is justified, sanctioned by the gods, and **“this covenant will be fulfilled.”**

Prompt: **No. This contract is off.**

Jhunal says:
- it cannot simply be undone because it was an irreversible free-will choice;
- the contract is not completely perfect;
- the player also has power in the contract and could break it from their own side.

Prompt: **Are you truly on my side?**

Jhunal answers that he always is and is always ready to help.

## Evidence discipline

This is the strongest direct contract/covenant language currently resolved for Jhunal the Black. It proves that he **frames** the player's progression as voluntary contractual consent while also engineering the surrounding circumstances.

It does not establish that his account of free choice, divine sanction, or fairness is objectively true.
