## Sc01 — 14 rows

Ulrik asks whether they should flush a baby down the river. Ja'zel says the river runs through the Cradle Woods in the Reach and on beneath Falkreath. He expects **Anem** to find the baby, and says her kindness will compel her to save it even at the cost of her own life.

Ja'zel denies that the Eyes of Hinnom supply this knowledge. He instead links his understanding to **Yelem's legacy** and to being guided by **the Owl**. He says the Owl normally only offers guidance because excessive interference would alter the flow itself.

Ja'zel says an old Nedic god's statue rests beneath Falkreath. His plan is for Anem to wash the baby so the river carries the baby's curse downstream and **defiles the statue**, which Ulrik calls a disaster for Falkreath.

## Sc02 — 8 rows

Ulrik worries that Ja'zel's plan depends too much on chance. Ja'zel distinguishes chance from **fate**, quoting the Fourth Sermon of Vivec.

The two discuss Vivec, pretenders, belief, and a recurring river/fish metaphor. Ja'zel says a pretender can become real if he believes he is, and compares larger minds to fish in the river while Ja'zel and Ulrik are too small to understand them.

## Sc03 — 10 rows

Ulrik reports that **Felix appears dead**. Ja'zel says the plan can still work and requests **Yelem's mutagen activator**.

Ja'zel intends Felix to become a **sacrifice for the new Chick**. Ulrik predicts the Owl will interfere. Ja'zel confirms that provoking direct intervention is the point: he wants the Owl side to send **avatars**, because their true form would be impossible to defeat, whereas some avatars might be killed.

## Sc04 — 8 rows

Ulrik asks about the curse placed on them. Ja'zel identifies **Laza, the shepherd trapped in the past**, and defines the curse's essence as longing for something that cannot truly exist anymore.

Ja'zel says Laza was not supposed to survive because the nomads attacked by **Lamae** all died. When Ulrik interprets the Laza scheme as an attempt to escape death, Ja'zel shifts to the problem of entities that **mimic an owl**. He says imitation is as easy as imitating a voice, and claims “they” previously failed with a hare and learn from mistakes better than mortals.

## Sc05 — 10 rows

Ulrik reports that Falkreath has **six chicks**. Ja'zel calls six a good sign and deliberately plans to arrive late.

His stated reason is that another will appear and replace one of the chicks; that replacement will be the **real Chick the Chick Trader seeks**.

Ulrik explicitly references the Egg → Chick → Chicken story. Ja'zel says the story was spun by fish that **Sheggorath** brought from another river or downstream; he says the story shows people what they want and destroys them.

Ulrik asks whether **Ja'bal of Anequina** came into this world for a purpose like Pelinal. Ja'zel rejects that model and says Ja'bal simply wants to return to where he came from.

## Sc06 — 10 rows

Ja'zel senses someone who seems as though he should be present but cannot be found. He wonders whose dream contains Ja'zel and who dreams of the dreaming gods.

He runs backward through a chain of figures/events: **Ja'zel, Ja'zeri, hidden survivors of Saarthal, Orlando of Atmora, Ja'bal the Cat, and Laza the Shepherd**, then decides the origin is earlier still.

He treats **Lamae's death as a fixed point** but questions how Laza survived alone, who was watching, and who transmitted the knowledge. He wonders whether Molag Bal was truly the only presence and concludes that something unnatural is interfering.

Ja'zel then describes **someone without a name** who is not Sheggorath but feels as if they were present all along. He asks whether the name Yffre gave it was taken away or whether people were simply made to forget it.

Using the river metaphor again, Ja'zel says the **Blue Star is visible now**, but that this is not his role. He says only Ja'zel can weave Ja'zel's tale, that his tale ends here, and that what follows belongs to the one watching him—his dear little Chick.

## Evidence boundary

All metaphysical and historical claims above are Ja'zel/Ulrik dialogue claims inside the Lucid Dream sequence. The SCEN structure establishes speaker and order, not objective truth of Ja'zel's cosmology.
