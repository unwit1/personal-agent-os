At Morahame the people looked upon the bloodied marble and came to know that Mercy long-hoped was coming on hoof and wing.

The Saliache were caught off-guard by the descent of Morihaus from the heights, and three-score were dead before he took his perch on the hated tower. The first men to see him fled, but he bellowed black thunder and all knelt.

"I am Morihaus, son of Kynar-Et, spoken into the world by Sky-mother and Winged Queen. I am her command-made-flesh and the first act of covenant. Listen and be known and be free."

The words from his mouth were solid and wrought of white diamond, and so none were capable of refusal. When asked why he had come now, Morihaus said:

"It is the will of Kynar-Et that a prayer be answered. Howling remorse is the prize for those who stand between us and Sard."

And though he was but one bull, his wings stretches across the Niben and bore the folk of Morahame to Sard. Al-Esh was there, robed in scarlet that was the solemn wellspring of the first-fallen King.

"Morihaus, Breath-of-Kyne, how I have prayed for your company! How the wretched hosts of man have yearned for your kindness."

"You are a heart to man, beloved Al-Esh. Do not think that I have not espied you from heaven and wished that you would pray for me to come."

"I have found the seed of White-and-Gold, beloved Mor. It is not far from here, in the clutches of the Salt-Queen. Willt thou aid me in its gathering?"

Morihaus snorted and pawed the earth. "It is why I am here, Al-Esh. Mount my back and we shall bring them life's wants and comforts."

Al-Esh flew on the back of Morihaus, who wore the armour of Kynar-Et herself. Her gathered host of warriors followed on the ground, eager to shed the blood of the Saliache now that they knew gods walked in their midst.

Before them fell the strength of Vindasel, whose wheel-torments were shattered and its slaves flocked to the banner of the Bull and the Queen. But the Salt-queen still stood against them, and so Al-Esh pronounced in her presence the circle unbroken and the triangle shattered. And thus weakened, Morihaus tore her on his horns.

Al-Esh raised from the corpse of the queen the Chim-El Adabal. "Lo, Morihaus. So far from White-Gold! It is the font of Kyne's tears."

At the mention of his mother-speaker, Morihaus bellowed with triumph. "We are victorious in Sardavar and Vindasel! Long reign to the death of elves! Now, have you the wisdom to know its mystery?"
"She will show me if I ask; Sky Mother Kynar-Et! By the name of the winds and the birds and the wilds, I beseech thee; what knowledge may I come to know if I turn the angles so?"

And Kyne replied with these things: She showed Al-Esh the image of a dragon swallowing the egg from which it hatched. She showed her the last star of the evening sky making bed-talk with the blackness of the night. The stillness of Lake Rumare broken by the stone hurled with love. Lastly she showed her the warrior who had fought in every war, and said: Make Him of the Stone.
Al-Esh wrought the red diamond then into a man, whose mane was silver as the wind and whose eyes burned with the fighting-hate of battles yet to come. She started with the boots, and unwound the stone in every direction until those white tresses fell over his armoured shoulders. And all who looked upon him knew that Al-Esh had conjured the Pelin-El.

"Gather, hosts of Men," she cried, "And let all the realms know this: I am Queen Ut-Cyrod and the servant of servants, none are beneath my care, for with the ministrations of Kynar-Et I have made covenant with Aka-Tosh, and with her son I have made the promise of a home for man where the tail of the dragon may ever reach his mouth. On this I promise you freedom and the wealth of forgotten memories rejoined in eternity."
