<p align="center">

The Saga of Nafni

as recorded by Huldr Bright-Rune

</p>

[pagebreak]
<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/I_letter.png'>t was in Varmann's time when the dread elfen mage of the ice, Azaran the Cruel, who was son of Calmathil the White Death, did come to Hallenrume Redoubt in the Lost Valley. He had learned from the dark god of magick, Syrabane, how to taint the earth and thus snare the souls of those numbered dead that they might not know their deserved rest in Sovngarde, but be doomed to wander in their old bodies on Nirn under the thrall of the mage himself.

Under cover of night did the necromancer guise himself, and found his way to the halls of Orkey's vigil. There in secrecy did he begin his foul rites. But among the clever dead was Rognvald, who in life had been keeper of the Whalebone Shield, and though his mortal remains had been burnt to ash, his spirit kept eternal watch in those catacombs.

And that night, the ghost of Rognvald did appear to his son Nafni, who lived in the city of Hjenkedde, which is now known as the Ironbind Barrow. He bade him come to the Lost Valley to stop the acts of the foul Azaran, since the necromancer's wards meant he could not rouse the dwellers of his own city.

And so Nafni rode out from Eisenkedde to the temple of Ustengrav, where the great scholars and priests of that time made their home. He gave his plea to the wise priests of Alduin the Dragon, who agreed without fault that he should be aided. Thus was he gifted the great mare Skjaermalde, who was daughter of the grey horses of the tundra, and also they gave to him the Dawn Breaker, which was a sword made by Meredja the Fire-Star, who was feared as a demon by the elves of old, and was bane to the undead and their masters.

Then did Nafni ride south, but he did not know that the necromancer had heard his father's plea, and had already sent out his warriors and thralls to slay him. Nafni was camping by the river Karth when it happened that he was set upon by the foe.

First came the thralls, who were the rotting drowned dead of once-great warriors. He drew the Dawn Breaker from its sheath, and even as he did so it shone a light so great that the husks recoiled in fear. He showed the undead the mercy of Sovngarde that day, and the sword cut cleanly through them and thus released their spirits to their appointed peace.
And then came at him Elebir son of Ascalande, and Thalissa daughter of Lembe, who were the snow-elf servants of Azaran. They did wound the brave Nafni but he was strong in his sides and did resist his blows such that he sent the elves to their own appointed fate.

Come the morn, he rode Skjaermelde to the Lost Valley. He was halted by spear-carls there, who demanded to know his purpose. When he told them that there was a foul necromancer in their tombs, they laughed and asked how he could have known that. He told them that he had been counselled by the ghost of his father, and they laughed further still, and told him he could not enter the city for he was clearly a troublemaker.

So Nafni rode Skjaermelde into the hills, of which she was well capable because the herd from which she came was strong. Here he got to a place where he could lower himself down the cliffs of the Lost Valley into the temples of Hallenrume.

The temple was well-guarded by his countrymen, who did not know of his presence. He hid himself well as he crept along the edges of the great fane, but before he could enter the crypts there he was confounded by a guard-thrall that Azaran had raised to guard his doings. Nafni quickly slew the undead, but the Dawn Breaker shone out as he did so, and the spear-carls across the city did happen to notice, and began to come towards the crypt.

Knowing he had not long, Nafni rushed inside the tomb. Azaran's dark rite was not yet complete, but he had already commanded some of the torc-draugr to guard him. Nafni thus found himself in battle with Sygnir the Elf-Slayer and Galma Bone-Bleeder, who were two heroes of renown. Had he borne a blade other than Dawn Breaker, they would have been his bane. But the sword forged by the Red Star was true to its purpose, and he suffered no wound that he could not abide.

Next he fought the great Jorgald the Fierce, who was father to Rognvald and grandfather to Nafni. He had passed down the sword-learning that Nafni still used, and so they were well matched strike for strike and block for block, and the battle lasted longer than Nafni wanted. But the dead man was not well practiced as his grandson, and so Nafni did strike his ancestor's heart and bade him travel to Shor's mighty hall.

By now the spear-carls were entering the tomb with shouts, and Nafni had to hurry. He headed into the deep crypt, and the carls caught up with him there - but not before they saw the dread necromancer defiling the dead with great clouds of purple light. And so with Nafni, they thwarted the necromancer and slew him, and cut off his head.

Nafni was thus hailed for his great heroism, but also recognised for his cleverness in evading the spear-carls in the face of a great threat. A feast was held in his honour in Hallenrume.

In the coming years, three great walls were erected to commemorate this happening. One was raised at the grave of the necromancer Azaran, another was raised by Nafni to salute his father, and another was raised in the name of great Shor by those who rememebered Nafni, encouraging all Nords to embrace those same virtues. One of these was at the Lost Valley, another at Ironbind Barrow, and the last at Ustengrav itself.
