<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/A_letter.png'>s it was so written in the Green Tablet Steps of Julianos in ancient times, 'As Above, So Below'. To gaze upon the heavens is to see the map of time lain out as Father Akatosh has so decreed; the old Ayleids of the heartlands of yesteryear were so perspicacious as to turn the map heavenwards, and so command portions of the firmament with their own image of the celestial net. The scholar industrious is therefore tasked, being keen diviner and recorder of the nature of things to come, with ascertaining the future of things by tracing their patterns in the sky.

Stars are rents in the very substance of Oblivion through which the inexorable flow of varliance descends from Aetherius. The largest clusters are the constellations we use for divination. The constellations of the firmament are fixed; naught can be learned by looking at them alone, for they are always exact in where they appear given any moment in the year. Rather, they are the canvas upon which the moons and the planets tell their plans of what is yet to come. However, Nirn itself does appear to rotate as a lesser wheel within the greater, so that one constellation will habitually have greater dominance over the others. It is by this rubric that we know the months of the year, for there are twelve constellations and indeed twelve months. Neglect not the itinerant constellation known as the Serpent, which serves as a frustrating reminder that all rules have their exception. It has neither stasis nor a month, and according to some seers of yore may not even have true stars. It is associated with forces of great upheaval and shall be mentioned later.

The Planets, sometimes called Planes, are naught but the very substance of the Divines themselves. They are infinite in size, surrounded by the further indefinition of Oblivion which renders them conceptually as spheres. Three of these planets - Akatosh, Arkay and Julianos - each form a 'star' in their own constellations.
Around Julianos, Stendarr orbits, but the other four planets do not remain within constellations. We shall refer to them as the Starless planes.

To portend the future, it falls to the diviner firstly to calculate the current month as this ascertains which constellation is in primacy. One must witness the moons and the four starless planes and determine which thirds and quarters they fall into, and likewise adjust for the position of the moons. The rogue plane, Baan Dar, is not usually used for divination although its presence may portend things if it enters a close tangent.

The RITUAL (charge of the Mage) is the constellation of Morning Star. It can portend many things; if Masser is in primacy, then it may signal good health and harvest the destruction of the undead, and those born under the sign in this time may find themselves blessed with this power. If Secunda is in primacy, it might signify the creation of undead, the failing of health and the inimity of animals.

The LOVER (charge of the Thief) is the constellation of Sun's Dawn. It signifies union, vengeance and pleasure. With Masser in ascendance, it may predict the stillbirth of a child, a lustful encounter, an enemy disguised as a lover or an unexpected drowning. If Dibella is in her zenith, it may denote the great wisdom that comes with fascination of the world, or great fertility.

The LORD (charge of the Warior) is the constellation of First Seed. It signifies strength and skill, but also inflexibility. Those born under the sign of the Lord are often vulnerable to suffer by fire, unless Stendarr is obscured by Julianos in which case they may be resistant to all forms of damage, magickal and physickal.

The MAGE is the constellation of Rain's Hand. He is the eye of the constellation as it is usually drawn. It represents brilliance, arrogance and talent. For those born under the Mage, it always signifies greater magickal ability - this is a constant - but depending on the position of Zenithar's orbits, it may cause the mage to be born a reclusive genius (Mara asc. and Dibella desc.), a great tutor (Mara and Dibella asc.), an imaginative if inattentive dreamer and creator of extraordinary illusions (Mara desc. Dibella asc.), or a cruel and ambitious hoarder of knowledge (Mara and Dibella desc.).

The SHADOW (charge of the Thief) is the constellation of Second Seed. Though it has the obvious association with thievery and obfuscation, it is often associated with the Daedra Nocturnal and the dread void beyond the stars. It always portends hidden things, unseen threats, unknowable wisdom. Those born under it may turn invisible - this power seems to be common to every person born thus.

The STEED (charge of the Warrior), constellation of Midyear, usually comes to mean haste and urgency. If Kynareth is in transit through the constellation, it may also mean allegiance and co-operation. Those born under it are fleeter of foot, and may bear greater burdens. The Doom-Driven born under the Steed hasten events of great import to the wildest extremities of possibility.

The APPRENTICE (charge of the Mage) is the constellation of Sun's Height and is often associated with elvenkind, given the ascent of Auri-El. It represents the potence and folly that come with youth. If Kynareth is close, then it may be a sign that folly should be tempered. If she is further away, it is a sign that fervour should be embraced. Those born under it are immensely powerful mages, but are far more vulnerable to magicka themselves.

The WARRIOR is the constellation of Last Seed. It portends war. If Zenithar is on the approach, it may represent a great victory against poor odds. If he is on the return, it can mean great calamity. If he is in transit, it means destiny may fall upon the fate of a single warrior. Those born under it have the War-Wyrd, an inherent knowledge of battle and fighting that begins at birth.

The LADY (charge of the Warrior) is the constellation of Hearthfire. It portends great resilience, or the need for such. When Mara is in ascendence, it is the former, and the latter in her descent. If consulted at times of pregnancy, it is often the case that if Mara and Dibella are both in descent it may mean death of either mother or child. As such, mothers often consult with astologers before attempting to conceive. Those born under the sign are extremely resilient in body and mind, and their own children nearly always come to term unharmed.

The TOWER (charge of the Thief) is the constellation of Frostfall. Oftentimes its ascent merely signifies strength or security, but once in a while - when Zenithar is at his furthest distance, or when the eye of the Ritual is obscured or distracted by the movements of other planets, it may portend freedom, escape and the beginning of a changing chapter in history. Those born underneath it are adept at getting in and out of locked rooms.

The ATRONACH (charge of the Mage) is the constellation of Sun's Dusk. It represents wealth of knowledge, but also a struggle to learn new things. It often portends that a great notable may be revealed a plagiarist, thief or impostor. Staves and enchantments created under the Atronach are more potent that normal. Those born underneath it struggle to recharge their reserves of magicka, and in many cases are completely unable without potions or a strange knack to absorb magick spells cast at them.

The THIEF is the constellation of Evening Star, and represents chance and fate as well as the more traditional meaning of theft. If Zenithar and Stendarr are far from the eye of the Thief, which is Arkay, then it often portends the loss of something precious into the hands of those who would misuse it. Those born under it have uncanny luck as well as being talented in criminal pursuits.

The SERPENT has no hour and no month and no time of its own. The stars that make it up are not truly stars, rather they are un-stars. It is strongly associated with the dead god, Lorkhan, and the un-stars, which are believed by some to be the carcasses of dead Magna-ge, emit no varliance. When it comes within contact with another constellation, predictions based on it tend to waver towards the worst possible - though the Warrior and his charges tend to keep the serpent's ill-fortune at bay more easily. Those born under it are often known as doom-driven or fate-wracked, and bring with them ill-tidings on the few times when it should eclipse the constellation under which a person would normally be born. They have been reported to have the power to project a poisonous bite at distance.

The SUN is a beacon of such varliance that is makes divination impossible, because the amount of exactitude it emits makes interpretation impossible. Thus using the sun to divine on its own is impossible. But, observations made in the intermediate times of Dawn and Dusk, when the Daedric prince Azura holds sway, may grant unusual wisdom. Subtleties and gentle interpretations are more likely to hold sway over precise and exact ones in these hours.

MASSER and SECUNDA construct the lunar lattice, which is mostly used by the Khajiit. They prefer to use the moons for divination rather than the stars and planets, and have developed a system that requires its own volume. However, the movements of Jone and Jode (as the Khajiit call them) are of some importance to any diviner. Generally, if Masser eclipses a constellation, is empowers the constellations on either side. If Secunda eclipses a constellation, it is best to read instead the constellation's opposite - which is usually obscured by Nirn.
