And worse still when a thing cannot be agreed upon, it has no recourse but to be a different thing to every person witnessing it. And the light that falls upon it from the great firmament above does nothing to assuage the contention. And thus conflict arises over things from the most trivial to the most profound, and all the while there is cast by that great light inexplicable a strange shadow whose penumbra is the nature of our very being. 

Light, Shadow, Penumbra. Is, Is Not, Maybe. A mind cannot be sure of the excesses of its own reclusions, and when those become evident to the eyes of others then the shadow is cast within the self. And for all that we observe the self, the only way to comprehend it is to chisel a statue of it from an idea of permanence and rigidity that is ill-suited to the occasion.

The inflexibility of these statues is mirrored by their fragility, for even a simple observation can render them into concept-dust that shortens the equation to the mere integer "I think".

The lesser mind becomes anguished and fragmented in this moment, whereas the wiser learns to separate the equation more fruitfully. "I am".

When one merely IS, then the facts of what one is cease to be of material importance. One can choose not only what one is, but also what one is not, and with understandings of the thought-pattern of other apparent consciousnesses, one can weave the shadow of things onto the actuality.
