In the Hall of the Hunter

by Shassour-Inibael

</p>
[pagebreak]
<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/I_letter.png'>n the Hall of the Huntmaster, Hircine, master of the Ritual of the Innocent Quarry, presides upon his throne, coated in the furs and skins of a hundred thousand beasts. There he sits, and he remembers. He casts his ancient mind back to his greatest victories, and his most satisfying kills, and his most cunning rivals.

Correct etiquette must be observed when sat at any of the tables in his realm. 

First, you must never doubt the veracity of a hunt-tale told by any hunter sat between you and Hircine - for they are your betters. 

Second, you may doubt the veracity of a hunt-tale by any hunter who sits opposite you, or the same distance as you from Hircine. However, this will automatically initiate the Saviour's Challenge.

Third, you may doubt the veracity of any hunt-tale told by a hunter who sits further away from you than Hircine, and they are expected to either demonstrate their tale better, or they may choose to initiate the Saviour's Challenge.

Fourth, you may only challenge a hunter who sits closer than you to Hircine if they have doubted your hunt-tale. 

Fifth, the Saviour's Challenge must be fought in the space between Hircine's Table and the lower tables. He must preside.

Sixth, the hunter claiming doubt must wear the Cuirass of the Saviour's Hide.

Seventh, the hunter being doubted has one throw of the Spear of Bitter Mercy.

Eighth, the wearer of the Cuirass of the Saviour's Hide may not move both their feet from the spot, but may otherwise: Duck, dodge, swivel, pivot, or they may adjust their stance so that the spear strikes the most well-protected parts; being the chest, the groin and the shoulders. Likewise, they may move one foot from its original position to aid in the above.

Ninth, if the wearer of the Cuirass of the Saviour's Hide should move both feet, or fall over, or be physically wounded by the Spear of Bitter Mercy beyond mere grazes or bruising, then the wearer is considered to have lost the challenge.

Tenth, if the wielder of the Spear of Bitter Mercy does not manage to knock down the Wearer of the Cuirass of the Saviour's Hide from the stance, and fails to physically wound them, then the wielder is considered to have lost the challenge.

Eleventh, if the wearer of the Cuirass of the Saviour's Hide should fall over or move both feet, AND the wielder of the Spear of Bitter Mercy should miss their target, then both are considered to have lost the challenge.

Twelfth, if the wielder of the Spear of Bitter Mercy manages to throw the Spear hard enough that it embeds itself in the Cuirass of the Saviour's Hide, but the  wearer of the Cuirass of the Saviour's Hide is not injured and does not fall over or move both feet, then both parties are to be considered the victor.

Thirteenth, the loser or losers must move further down the table from Hircine by one seat. If this should remove them from the last seat in the hall, they are ejected from the Hall of the Hunter and become prey.

Fourteenth, the winner or winners may move further up the table towards Hircine by one seat. If this should take them to the Throne of Hircine then they are awarded the honour of Posting the Hunt.
