Varieties of Troll

By
Barukha gra-Varga

</p>
[pagebreak]
<p align="left">

<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>rolls have been a perennial problem in Tamriel since its conception. Due to the regenerative properies of their flesh, it has been supposed that, barring natural calamity, warfare, hunting or particularly voracious diseases, they may simply continue living. Particularly ancient trolls have been recorded, some supposedly dating back to the Dawn Era - if such stories can be believed.

Trolls are not limited by a simple morphology, though their regenerativity, third eye and weakness to fire are almost universal. Cyrodiilic readers will be familiar with the forest trolls that lumber across the Nibenean basin when left unchecked, but there are many other kinds too. Let us start with the familiar, however.

The mossy-green Forest Trolls of Cyrodiil are a threat to many who wander the wilds, but they prefer to nest around old ruins and under bridges. They sometimes gather in groups of up to three, but on their own they are not beyond the skills of most warriors to handle. Their mossy coloured fur makes them hard to spot from a distance until they move. They have some rudimentary intellect, and in some cases have been seen to form small clans or even write. One third era account tells of the 'Kalpaklan', who looted and hoarded pairs of callipers.

Cave Trolls are found across the North of Tamriel, especially Skyrim and High Rock. They are brown of fur and rather more fierce than their Cyrodiilic cousins, but fire will drive them off just as easily. They tend not to wander the wilds of Skyrim, and the lowlands around Whiterun Hold, Falkreath and the Rift tend to be safe from trolls in general, provided one stays above ground.

Frost Trolls are very similar in apperance to Cave Trolls, but white of fur. They live in the highlands and mountains of Skyrim, and are not limited to dwelling in caves - indeed, they are occasionally a problem to hikers and hunters in the snowy peaks. They are harder to kill, being stronger of muscle and tougher of hide, but they are often hunted for their pelts, which are very valuable and are often traded outside of Skyrim for a high price.

Grahl exist only in the most far northern reaches. They have only been definitively recorded in Solstheim, but tales of their presence elsewhere do exist. They have some fur around their shoulders sometimes, but otherwise they are more leathery. Their skin is ice-blue, and they are sometimes called Ice Trolls. Their eyes are blood-red, and their third eye is often used in magickal or alchymical preparations by the Skaal. 

River Trolls are most common around the South of Tamriel, especially Elsweyr and Black Marsh, but have been sighted in Morrowind and Cyrodiil on occasion. They have a tangled mane of hair resembling swamp weeds that allows them to remain hidden until they need to strike. They feed on mudcrabs, knowing instinctively where to find them and digging them out of the sand. They have even been known to use this ability in combat, pulling crabs out of the ground and hurling them at their enemies.

Then there are the trolls of legend, which have not been confirmed to exist at all. There are the Lava Trolls of ancient Resdayn, which supposedly could resist fire and indeed thrived in the volcanic ashlands of modern-day Morrowind. There is the hoary Udyrfrykte, a lumbering black-furred troll with just two, bright green eyes that was reported to have attacked a hunter's lodge in Solstheim in the late third era. And then there are the ancient trolls of yore, who have avoided death by predation or hunting, and have supposedly grown in such stature since the dawn of time they they are now great colossal beasts lurking in the darkest reaches of the world.

Remember, all trolls are dangerous - never be caught unawares. They are more intelligent than they seem, so regardless of their type, be ready for a fight.
