They cannot be blamed for this shortcoming; even the travails of worshipping Bal are a relatively straightforward matter compared to the impossible vagaries of divine logic. More still flock to the banners of the Black Worm, hoping that the maggot-moon will shine upon their meagre endeavours. It is true that Mannimarco was a master of the art in his life and in his unlife, but many have become disillusioned with the worm-god now. What secrets has he to show? What wisdom will he grant?

To unravel the true mysteries of the dead, one must unravel thus the entire of creation. Turn away from the sprightly gods of pacts and promises and power exchanged for will. There are forces more ancient that know the weft of existentia.

HERMAEUS MORA, the Wisdom of the Sea, is the keeper of the threads of knowledge. At his pleasure he gathers great cults who hunger for even a glimpse of cosmic wisdom, and he disperses among them such morsels as to tempt any mage. Do not be deceived by his offerings; there is far more that can be shown and far more that can be answered.
Make instead the offerings of life to him, and of secrets hidden for eternities. Abate the boredom of the eternal watcher and his rewards will be far greater than those gleaned by witless obeisance.

XERO-LYG, the Flesh Star, is a rogue scion of the source of all magicka. She can barely be seen among the un-stars of the Serpent constellation, yet her movement can be traced by its position. Remember: Just as earth and fire and water and light and wind were used in the process of creation, so too were the carcasses of dead spirits of the last order. Flesh is an element. Perhaps you think this is untrue because you have rendered down flesh into a powder, or produced a liquid from it. Herein lies the truth - you have split the primordial element into lesser elements. Xero-Lyg shines lightlessly, a dark-star, robbed of light by vengeful sisters. She remembers what they do not: The world has weight. Flesh has weight. Weight has power. She may un-shine the dark blessings of the most ancient of all magicks onto your proceedings if you but know the rites and possess the instruments, and have ready knowledge of the passage of the stars. 

SITHIS, the Empty Watcher, is the non-existence of reality, the black canvas onto which we are but a flick of paint almost invisible. Assassin cults revere him and depict him as a skull, but his form - if he can be said to have one - is far more ancient than bones. 
To understand Sithis enough to ensure that he may empower your necromancy, you must unravel mathematicka to the point where zero does not equal zero. For that is what he is - nothing - yet one must divine the sequence by which a number greater may be involved in the impossible equations. This may have terrible consequences; many a mage has reduced themself to dust through such miscalculations.

PADOMAEY is harder yet to conceive, for he is a dream of a mirror. He is held culpable for a crime he did not exist to commit. He is the projection of the dream's self-hate. He is the absolution of the dream's guilt. He is nothingness given life. He is the father of Sithis, who is father of Lorkhan, who is father of all mortals. Within each of us is that hatred that the dream holds for us. 
And thus we may absolve ourself of that crime by uttering the accusation against the dream itself. And when this is known, there is no more equation. The mathematicka becomes an illusion, and the constraints on our power are no more. Life is a dream, and death is the sleep between dreams. Time exists to limit the passage of one state back to the last, and once the obscurity is pierced, the wheel is null. Immortality exists as a shard within the spirit of every mortal, for we are ourselves shards of the divine. With this malign knowledge, life and death are but breaths in a moment, and the one may flow into the other with ease. Life in death, death in life, sleep in dream, tears from lidless eyes.
