De Salibus Animae

</p>

[pagebreak]
<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>he moste attentive student of the matters of arcane philosophie may, without invoking the moste foule daimons of Oblivioun nor calling upon the naymes of any dark masteres, draw the very aspects of the mortal soule into pieces of inert matter so form'd from the very material of the utter realms. Such morpholithick substance may, with the right and most exacte strikinge, be reduced down to their moste essential saltes - and with such reductioun, become a substance from which a greater piece may be so fashion'd.

Morpholiths range most greatlie in capacitie from the most insignificant to the enormously grande. The least of such stones may but harbour the soul of the merest of Kynareth's creatiouns, be they the ratte or the scribbe, though the grandest may yet form a damnable prisoun for the soules of far greater beings, even so they be the keenest intellects of the hordes of Oblivioun.

Moste blasphemous of all is the stone so mark'd by its darkness, for such stones may holde within their fractals and facets the very souls of mortal men and mer. And with such a soul grieviously bounde, the necromancer may indulge themself in all manner of moste powerful enchantings.

Yet it may come to be that such a sorceror mighte instead, having neede of such thynges, reduce the stone. The salts so gathered from a morpholith will, it shall astonish none, differe greatlie dependinge on the size of such. However, a greater amount of this salte may be yielded from the stone thus filled than from the stone that has lain emptie.

Thus, with a hoste of the leaste of such stones, a craftsman of abominable intent may entrap the souls of a gathering of small creatures, and havinge done so may grind the stones to their most basic corpuscular nature. With these saltes the sorcerer may then fashion a wholly greater stone into which the essence of a much larger being may be so ensnared.
