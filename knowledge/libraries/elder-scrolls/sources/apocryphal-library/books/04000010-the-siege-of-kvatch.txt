<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/I_letter.png'>n the last year of the Third Era, the machinations of a cult known as the Mythic Dawn led to the assassination of Emperor Uriel Septim VII and the failing of the Dragonfire wards that kept the wards between Oblivon and Mundus from waning. The cult, who worshipped Mehrunes Dagon, sought to bring the Prince of Destruction into the waking world.

Before the Storm

Following the quenching of the Dragonfires, the first and most immediate act of the Daedric forces beyond the veil was to open three great gates before the city of Kvatch. To understand the reasons for this, one must understand that before the Emperor had even been hurried to the believed safety of the Imperial Palace's subterranes, his heirs had already been murdered. The only blood-heir to the Septims now was a man called Martin, whom we now know as Saint Martin Septim. He was the illegitimate son of Uriel VII through an unknown lover. He was also a priest in the service of Akatosh in Kvatch.

It is assumed that the Mythic Dawn were able to determine the other heirs with relative ease, but Martin had presumably posed a greater challenge, since he was unaware of his own bloodline, as was everyone else. But the forces of Oblivion must have known that the heir was in the city somehow. With a whole city of people, any one of whom could have been the heir, the most pertinent course of action in the eyes of the Lord of Destruction was, quite simply, that.

The City Falls

The date of the attack is hard to place but sources usually centre around Last Seed, 3E 433. The surviving witnesses said that there had been no unusual activity in the surrounding area at all, and in fact the night had been a calm one until a storm seemed to whip up out of nowhere. With such freak storms being unusual in their own right, people came out of their homes to look up at the sky which, to their shock, suddenly began to turn red.

It was the guards on the walls who first saw the three red gates open before the city gates. A host of lesser Daedra, backed by Dremorae, began assaulting the gates at once, scurrying from the portals. Shortly after, those within the city were horrified to see something mounting the wall.

This was the Siege Crawler, and it was the Daedra's most powerful weapon in the siege. Without it, it is possible that the city may have endured a good deal better. But this weapon was fashioned like an enormous centipede, far greater in size than even the Great Chapel of Akatosh.
Siege Crawlers are mechanical, and not formed of organic components. They do not seem to be made from creatia in the same way that Daedric creatures are, but rather it seems to be forged for purpose in the realm of Oblivion where Dagon lurks, the Deadlands. And so a giant mechanical caterpillar reared its head over the walls and spat columns of flame and magma onto the unsuspecting city.

With the city mostly aflame, the Crawler had done its work. The walls were mounted by Daedra who swarmed in and overwhelmed most of the guard already. Count Ormellius Goldwine was barricaded into Castle Kvatch with his personal guard, and a few others managed to defend what remained of the Great Chapel. Around this time, the Siege Crawler withdrew from the walls, and two of the portals closed, leaving one directly in front of the city's gate. A few of the city's guard had managed to fight their way out of the city, and were now attempting to hold the Daedric hordes back and prevent them from attacking the Gold Road. The acting Captain was Savlian Matius, who held the line until unexpected relief arrived

The Fall of the Portal

The individual who turned up was but one person, and is now understood to have been a prisoner in the Imperial Dungeon mere days previous. This prisoner had been one of the last witnesses to the Emperor's assassination, and had been tasked by the Blades with delivering the Amulet of Kings to safety. Following this, they had been sent to Kvatch to find Martin Septim, only to discover that they were almost too late.

The arrival of a single extra warrior was enough for Savlian, who decided that with one additional sword at their side, and with their mission now taking a different timbre, the gate could be attacked. And so a small number of guards and the former prisoner assaulted the gate. This involved advancing directly into the Deadlands.

The other side of the portal was, by all accounts, a land wrought of fire, iron and spikes beneath a bleeding sky. The brunt of the Daedric attack had already been spent, and so the small band of soldiers were able to fight against a few scamps and clannfears until they found a survivor from the previous mission into the gate. He warned them that the tallest tower in view was their objective, and that the gate was held open by a sigil stone. 

Sigil stones are a type of morpholith (shaping-stone) common to Oblivion that can be inscribed with a certain sigil that allows them to function in their purpose. This can be keeping a portal open, or it can be used for imbuing a powerful weapon. In the former case, they had to be kept at the summit of a great tower in order to penetrate the veil between Mundus and Oblivion. They are the stone that allows the tower to function, much like the binding towers of the mortal realm.

The scaling of the tower took some great effort for the interlopers, and at this point they came into contact with Oblivion's true soldiers, the Dremora, or Kyn. They formed a defence of the tower, attempting to keep the Kvatchi soldiers at bay, but had been caught unawares and were pushed back to the Sigil sanctum, where they made a last stand against the Imperial contingent. The former prisoner was able to steal the Sigil Stone, collapsing the portal and transporting the remaining troops back to the city gate.

The Recapture of Kvatch

The soldiers forced their way through the gate, and battled their way to the Chapel. It was here that the strategy for the rest of the battle was decided. Martin was found to be alive and well, and had been looking after surviving citizens. They were taken outside the city to safety and the next phase began. It was to be a dangerous push forward, but the plumes of smoke above Kvatch had drawn a small batallion of Imperial Guard to the gates. They pledged to aid Matius' retaking of the city, and formed the much-needed relief that would ensure success.

The gatehouse that separated the castle from the city proper had been raised, and Daedra were attacking with fire and arrows from atop the castle's walls. A direct attack seemed catastrophic, so a detachment of guards kept their arrows on the Daedra while the others went through the chapel's undercroft. It is understood that the prisoner actually led this attack through the catacombs and up to the inside of the gatehouse.

With the gate down, the main force attacked the castle walls. The doors were breached, and the inside of the castle was found to be in ruins. Count Goldwine had already been killed.

Aftermath

The city had been retaken, but the damage had already been done. There was little shelter to be found anywhere, and the fires seemed to burn for weeks afterwards. However, the story of the prisoner's rescue of the city and its survivors soon became a rallying call against the oncoming Daedric tide. The prisoner came to be known as the Hero of Kvatch, and their battles alongside Martin Septim and the Blades remain the stuff of legends.

Savian Matius became the de jure Captain of the Guard in Kvatch, and is remembered as having sent soldiers to the defence of Bruma later in the crisis, when the Siege Crawler was destroyed.

The Oblivion Crisis came to an end with the heroic sacrifice of Martin Septim, who embodied the Time-god Akatosh in order to defeat Mehrunes Dagon in the mortal plane.

The city of Kvatch remained a ruin into the early Fourth Era, when it was eventually rebuilt.
