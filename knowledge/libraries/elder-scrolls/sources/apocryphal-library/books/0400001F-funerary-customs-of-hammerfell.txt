Funerary Customs of Hammerfell

by Thomas Holvin 

</p>

[pagebreak]
<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/W_letter.png'>hile most of the races of Men tend to view Shor, Shezarr or Lorkhan as a benevolent creator, the Redguards of Hammerfell and Yokuda believe, like the Mer, that this was a trick by the god, whom they call Sep. Sep, the serpent, complicates the return of Redguard souls to their appointed afterlife, the Far Shores.

Through the rites of the death-god Tu'Whacca, the mortal souls might find their way to the Far Shores. This god's rites fulfil the same role as those of Arkay - including protection against necromancy.

The dead are buried in great necropolises, usually deep in the desert. They are afforded the rites of Tu'Whacca (or sometimes Arkay, among the Forbears) and are a place of burial for all, from peasants to princes.

Burial is common, though some undergo mummification. This practice, which involves the removal of the organs and the wrapping of the body in tight wrappings, is occasionally practiced in High Rock and Cyrodiil, and mirrors practices among the ancient Nords and the Velothi Dunmer, but the practice is well associated with Hammerfell where it preserves the body against the heat of the desert.

To strike the dead is considered extremely taboo among the Redguard. This makes protecting oneself against the Undead a challenge. A whole caste of Redguards form a tribe called the Ash'abah, who take upon themselves the duty of fighting the undead. For this, they are gratefully shunned by the rest of Hammerfell society. They have a series of methods for slaying the undead that require the uttering of apologies.

Great wards are placed over the sacred cities of the dead to enforce Tu'Whacca's will. His priests, Throne Keepers, tend to the dead and ensure their correct burial, and can call the god's power in the form a great breath that can drive the dead to return to their slumber.
