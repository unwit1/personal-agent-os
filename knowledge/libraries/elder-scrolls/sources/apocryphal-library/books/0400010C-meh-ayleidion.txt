A Traditional Bosmer Poem

<p align="left">
Foreword by the Translator:

The Meh Ayleidion is by far the most well-known of the early Bosmeri epics, and it is a common occurrence to see its remaining known fragments performed in Valenwood. It is not known exactly how much of the epic survives, for the 'Thousand Benefits of Hiding' are not numbered down the page; rather, the poem is an exercise of the reader to derive meaning from them. Those fragments and verses that remain have been assembled into a narrative to the best of my ability.

It has been translated many times before, but I hope that this humble offering will afford a new understanding of the Bosmeri attitude to their magnificent forest to the reader unaccustomed to their lands.

- Malden Gryphonspear

<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/F_letter.png'>rom the Lips of Jephre:

"O look, my child, where the river turns wild,
Where the salmon-poke and catcher sits
The shadow but moves blade-by-inch
On the sun-clad day the eagle favours
But lo, the walk-dark spectre looms
Whereon the catcher trills his tune.

Like bone-ash coughed from the pan
The knife-tooth cat drags hurling-snarl
The catcher, gone in light of day
Dragged to fester on the leopard's branch
Better to feed the forest floor
And the patient hunter."

"O remember, my child
Hiding is not the property of the hunter
It is the birthright of the forest-dweller
And should never be forgotten
Except by the brazen baiter
And the river-spinner.

For the leopard forgets not the shadow
Nor the whooping-crow nor the ever-tadpole
Nor the shell-crab nor the whistle-frog
Nor the shy calling-gecko nor his wife
Cleave to the shadow, my child
And the shadows will cleave to you."

<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>he Instructions to the Bosmer from the Forest Magician, Hermaeus Mora:

It is time for the lesson.
Attend to Old Mora.
Great is my wisdom.
Ignore at your peril.
For mine is the scripture,
that teaches the art-form
where ink matches brush-stroke
in hazy dark dance-move.

Lesson One:

There is but one tree
In the whole of the wood
For though they seem many
All came from the One;
The Acorn of Maybe,
Each tree could have been,
So it was, so they are.
But each tree is the same tree
For One was the acorn.

As each tree casts a shadow
So too is there One;
One shadow between them
And the wisest among you
Have solved the next riddle:
If there is but one shadow
Can I just hide in one?

And then move from another?

Yes, clever sap-elves.
Your minds please Old Mora!
In the shadow of one tree,
You stand in them all
From the all-shade emerge
And the quarry is thine.

But now Mora must caution:
Do not tarry in all-shade!
Be as one always moving 
And wait not on the slowest.
Fear things in the dark-tree
That I Will Not Name.

<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/F_letter.png'>rom the Mouth of Baan Dar:

O, you could have it all!
The fruits of the mine and the silkworm
All yours for the feasting, all for the taking!
The yolk of the goldmine, glistening!
The stolen wood of foreign lands
The emerald, suiting lover's-eye
The coin of grassland Men.

The guardian of great wealth
Only guards 'gainst what she sees.
The sentry of the mound
Only cries at what he hears.

And so the orchard is the gold mine
And the silkworm brings the diamonds
All for the clever forester
Who steps not on dry twig,
Who catches not the lanternlight.
