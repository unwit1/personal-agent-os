THE TAXONOMY ONEIROSOPHICAL

Being a treatise on the many and varied dream-gifts of blessed mother Vaermina

by Husticus of Bruma

</p>

<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/R_letter.png'>emember, devout dreamer, that all dreams and interludes of the liminal state between sleep and waking are not mere fancies of the eager and energetic mind as it struggles with the inertia of immaterial thought; rather, they are unto themselves planes and worlds and times that may yet come to be, or may have long since ceased to be. All portents are dreams and thus the reverse is true. By the same rubric, those persons that we may dream might be dreaming us in turn, so wonder not too deeply that our deaths might simply not be the waking of another.

All too common is the nightmare born of simple inadequacy. Of shame, of embarrassment do we find ourselves sky-clad among our very clothed peers. We fail where they succeed, and see even our imbecilic enemies outshine us in every deed. They are insidious but usually bring relief upon waking.

Mother Vaermina often allows her brothers and sisters to come into our dreams. Thus does the Lord of Madness come to taunt us with world that cannot be, of spiral-existence made into line-thought that bends across an infinite plane. Waking shall bring relief after a time, but it is not immediate.

Namira and Peryite shine dark into the portents with visions of flesh and insects, of plagues and devoured persons reduced to naught but the simple ingredients of their being. Slugs and spiders and all manner of creature that form such plagues upon Nirn are the audience for our midnight shrieks.

Where there is a good dream of being a hunter, another has a nightmare of being hunted. Chase-nightmares are common, and usually end when the dreamer, being the quarry, is caught - though it is common to witness ones fellows, in their own dreamlike form, be picked off one by one first.

Mephala and Boethiah tempt us with that which we want and yet spirit it away at the last moment, to reveal to us the black truth of what we now no longer have. Loss and betrayal are common in such dreams.

The other princes may also amuse themselves in our minds, but Vaermina will always return with the Presence.

The Presence cannot be denied. It has no name, and none wise to the nature of portents has ever seen it or given form to it. It is ghastly and unknowable, it is a haunter of all. None survive their whole life without experiencing the Presence. The feeling of being hunted by it pervades the liminal barrier, and it can take hours after waking to feel free of the Presence's malingering sensation.

But worse still is the Absence. Vaermina sometimes deems us worthy of the Absence in our lives, and it is no mere loss of life or love. It is an echo of the emptiness of things. It is the lack of nature present in everything. It is to make a gap hollow. A chasm that cannot be bridged in thought or concept. It is vast, and endless, and miniscule. After dreaming of the Absence, it may be days before the dreamer feels safe enough to sleep again.
