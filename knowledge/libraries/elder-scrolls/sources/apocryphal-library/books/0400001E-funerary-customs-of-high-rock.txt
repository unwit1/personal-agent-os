Funerary Customs of High Rock

by Thomas Holvin 

</p>

[pagebreak]
<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/A_letter.png'>s in Cyrodiil and Skyrim, the Bretons of High Rock worship Arkay, though in ancient times would have made prayer to Xarxes or Orkey depending on whether they were in a region occupied by the Direnni or by the Nords.

Arkay is seen as the great wheel by the Bretons, whereby one dies and is reborn constantly, and that this allows the Mundus to continue. The practice of Arkay worship in modern-day High Rock differs little from mainstream worship of the Divines, with funeral customs resembling Colovian ones.

In ancient times, burial practice was usually cremation. This seems to have developed both from the traditions of nomadic riders, for whom permanent grave sites were of little use, and also the Direnni who were culturally Altmeri. As the Imperial faith took root in High Rock, so too did the practice of cremation fall to the wayside until the second era. Nowadays, Bretons are buried in simple graves or elaborate tombs, as in Colovia. 

Upon the body are places the final two consecrations. The first consecration is Arkay's Grace, which a Breton receives at birth, which protects children until they come of age. 

The consecration of Arkay's Blessing is places upon a dying Breton, which eases their passing into the great wheel, and prevents their soul being trapped or misused.

Finally, after death the mortal remains are imbued with consecration of Arkay's Law, which prevents the remains from being raised by criminal necromancy.

Among the druids of the various wyrds and covens of the uplands of High Rock, Arkay is venerated less and the elven god Y'ffre is more common. Like Arkay, Y'ffre is associated with cycles of renewal. Druidic customs may involve burial mounds, which though different in style are functionally much like crypts. There is some amount of crossover between the Druidic and the Imperial faiths to this day.

In the East of High Rock dwell the Reachfolk. The practices of the Witchmen are not like those of the Bretons, nor is their faith. They do not venerate the Divines, but rather give themselves over to the worship of the Daedric princes Hircine, Peryite and Namira, as well as the dark god Lorkh which is their name for Lorkhan.

Namira fulfils the role of death god among the Witchfolk. She is seen as the beginning and end of all things, a great primal darkness. Hircine is the teacher of harsh truths, and together they prepare the souls of the Reachfolk in life for what they may experience in death. Constant improvement is seen as necessary to overcome the trials of the spirit world, and has been compared to the Psijiic Endeavour of the Dunmer by some scholars.

Reachfolk bury their dead. They believe that necromancy may prevent their souls from joining Hircine's great hunt at the end of time, and so they do not practice it among themselves, but Gravesingers do exist in some clans who may perform it on other bodies. 
They tend to build cairns to house their dead, although in the region of Markarth in Skyrim they will often favour the use of old Nordic crypts.
