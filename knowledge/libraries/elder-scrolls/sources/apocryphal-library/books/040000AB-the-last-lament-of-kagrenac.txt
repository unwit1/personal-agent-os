<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/A_letter.png'>s he struck the heart with Sunder, Kagrenac saw Anumidum for the first time. 

Of course, he had smoothed his hands over every brass panel and every wightbone fragment. He had checked every gauge a hundred and eight times, and personally overseen the fixing of every rivet grander than three inches in diameter. No piston nor gyre had been installed withour his having inspected it thricefold.

He had looked upon the brass titan's emerald eyes on occasions innumerable, and sung solemnly to each capacitor as it was harmonised. He had tuned the frequencies and tones of every circuit and centrifuge. Most importantly, he had - through the safe embrace of the Wraith-guard - touched the very Heart of the World itself, feeling its rhythmic plea for release.

But as he struck the heart with Sunder, he saw Anumidum for the first time.

Here was everything he had worked for, and everything he had sought. Here was the pinnacle of his achievement and the very culmination of all his sleepless moments and frantic calculations. All had come to this, and to his success, his victory, his moment of absolute triumph and the logical vanquishment of all his doubters and detractors.

He saw Anumidum for the first time.

And he saw the Numidium.

He saw that he was replaced.

All the other dreams of his people now amounted to naught. All their hopes for the future were absorbed into this one obsession. A being comprised of the weight of his whole people's desire for something else.

And it was going to replace them. It had thoughts and ideas that the Dwemer could never have. It calculated patterns of being and unbeing in an instant that the Dwemer had spent generations trying to perfect. 

He learned of machine-forms of angst, decay and obviation, of loop-heraldries and pattern-signatures of heaven and earth that had been the most dear discoveries of his race, dreamt up and corrected in an instant by a being that had no capacity to feel joy in its discoveries. What then, wondered Kagrenac, might this titan feel something for? He begged the machine he had birthed to answer. He submitted himself in humiliation to the majesty of his own creation.

"O Anumidum, unshackle of the seventeenth tone, child of Logic and Reason, will you accept the Dwemer as your children in reversal of your own investiture?"

And then Anumidum replied,

NO.
