The Fate of Vivec

by Mevena Nereli 
College of Whispers
</p>
<p align="left">
[pagebreak]

<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/V_letter.png'>ivec has not been seen since the Third Era ended. Various groups have tried to explain this in various ways, but none to any sense of conclusion. What is known is that without his presence, or with his separation from the source of his power, the prison moon Baar Dau eventually collapsed into his eponymous city, wiping out the population. It is believed that he had already vanished by the time that this had happened, otherwise the popular view would be that he died in the catastrophe.

Scattered reports arrived at the onset of the Oblivion Crisis that Vivec had been abducted by Daedra when gates to the Deadlands opened around the city. The fact that the gates were able to open so close indicates that Vivec's power had already substantially weakened. For want of other explanations, many go to this one - it is prosaic and bleak, but much of the close of the Third Era was. He had already disappeared from public view sometime before this event, so it is unknown if the Daedra were actually responsible.

A curious narrative is that of his 'Trial'. There are no official records of a trial having taken place at Hogithum Hall, and in fact there are none who can say with certainty that the hall exists. Occasionally someone claims to have uncovered some official records of this, but they are usually determined to be either forgeries or apocryphal, or otherwise irreplicable. That said, there are a number who do still hold firm that the events of the case did occur.
The narrative claims that Vivec was put on trial for his part in the murder of Lord Nerevar Indoril, after the events of the Nerevarine prophecy had been fulfilled, and some old information had come to light from the archives of the Apographa. His defence seems to have been that perhaps Vivec the mortal had done these things, but Vivec the god had not. At some point, the Daedric Prince Azura enters, and Vivec banishes her using Muatra in a fashion that is particularly characteristic of Vivec's boastful takes of sexual violence. He then ascends to Aetherius.
The tale is usually regarded as false - there is simply no backing in any official set of documents to support the narrative. Unusually though, the tale is supported by some priests who were very much opposed to the Tribunal's rule - and that makes the story more compelling.

Various other tales describe how he reached a state of New Dreaming, in which he used the ancient symbol of Royalty to comprehend the Aurbis in totality. The various Guilds Theosophical who have studied this practice believe that it is indeed possible to reach this state, though here opinion swiftly divides on whether or not Vivec actually reached it. Some claim that his power was sustained purely by the Heart of Lorkhan, and others state that while this may be true, it does not preclude the possibility that he attained CHIM by using the heart's power.
A group within the latter camp suggests this: CHIM is a state that is very difficult to maintain. To remain separate from creation while observing it in completeness requires constant focus. When that slips, the separation goes with it, and so the mind of the individual becomes indistinguishable from the mind of the Godhead. Thus they claim, Vivec did achieve CHIM, but following the death of the Sharmat and the vanishing of the Heart, he lost his ability to focus and erased himself. 
The notion of the Godhead, the 'Zero-Sum' and CHIM are themselves hypothetical - while many scholars believe they exist, very few reports exist that give them any solid standing in common academic circles. It is possible they exist in the records of more secretive institutions like the Moth Priests, the Marukhati Selectives and the Psijiic Order.

The most likely, however, is that tale foreseen in prophecy. The Velothi of old Morrowind believed that the Nerevarine would topple the false gods of the Tribunal, and many now believe this did indeed happen.
Almalexia and Sotha Sil are understood to have perished at the end of the blade Trueflame, now. The reports from the time are very poorly understood, but this is more or less what is suggested. And there are reports from the desk of the Ministry of Justice in Vivec City, rescued from the city in the moments before it collapsed (thanks to an unusual turn of events), that indicate the Nerevarine was seen entering the Palace of Vivec with intent to slay him. One set of reports claims that the Nerevarine did this once Dagoth Ur had been defeated, and another set claims it was done to retrieve one of the Tools of Kagrenac so that the Sharmat might thus be slain.

None of these tales can be relied on with absolute certainty, and none can be dismissed as complete fancy. If Vivec has truly ascended to Aetherius, then he has yet to show himself in any capacity of new godhood. If he was merely slain, then his body has not been recorded either.
