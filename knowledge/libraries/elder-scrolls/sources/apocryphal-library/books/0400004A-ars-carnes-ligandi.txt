<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/t_letter.png'>he mortal form is no mere effigy of forgotten Ada, nor an unadorned and rough-hewn vessel for the immortal soul to maintain it until our allotted hours are counted to nil. Indeed not, for the very substance of our forms is the promise of oft-maligned Sheor whose watchful eye trails the night sky in never-faltering vigil. Our flesh, our skin and our sinew and indeed our bones give sanctuary not just to our souls but also to a most celestial and sacred magick far older than the very hills.

It is little wonder that Arkay seeks inveterately to turn the wheel of time on the cadavers of the deceased, debasing them to the substance of the earth before the far older sorceries can be called forth. Many are the simple necromancers who rejoice at this inexhaustible reservoir of earth-salt and rotten material, and indeed at the hastiness with which it reveals the bones they crave. And they are not impoverished of wit to seek these bones, for they do harbour a great and powerful potence - but in neglecting the stuff of flesh, lesser necromancers become frustrated with a lack of progress. And yet even when presented with a carcass affording ample remaining flesh, they persisted in animating the bones merely to support the rest of the body.

The wisdom of the flesh was shown to me by a tutor who shall remain kindly nameless, and in those experiments I witnessed very old and strange things that the world had not known since the harrowing of the Wild Elves by the slave-queen Alessia and her Bull-Consort. For those ancient Ayleids had practiced something in their slave gardens very similar to what I now witnessed on that day in Anticlere.

Before a room of us so gathered, she placed upon the table a human arm, resting on a brass support, which she explained came from an individual no more than three days dead. She had carefully removed the skin of the arm so that all could witness the musculature of the appendage and the bones beneath. She gave us all a chance to confirm to our satisfaction that the appendage was indeed detached, and then she bade us sit back as she raised her staff. She demonstrated the power of this implement, which we saw was a simple staff of sparks as could be fashioned by any meagre apprentice of the enchanting arts. But what happened next astounded even the most inventive of our number. 

For she touched the tip of that staff to the stump of that gruesome arm, and while releasing only the barest amount of its power, she caused the whole arm to spasm - wildly at first, but as her control improved, she was able to manipulate the fingers of the hand to open and close, as if it were indeed alive. 

This, she claimed, was the least of the power of which the elves of old Vindasel and the most secretive of Witchmen could conjure. And indeed, she went on, those flesh-crafters too had encountered limits. But among their legends, even into the present day in the case of the Reachfolk, there are rogue accounts of sorceries of the blackest nature, in which the same protocols of magick that we had just witnessed had given rise to living beings from the flesh of the dead.

The secrets of this she did not reveal to us, for it was clear that these mysteries were unknown even to she. Nonetheless we were shown the rites of preparation for the flesh, and the most satisfactory cantrips of fulment charge that could shock life into the flesh, and the ancient symbols and sigils with which to adorn skin. We were shown the tools of the forgotten Dwemer which could be diverted from their intended purpose to the practicalities we desired. We were tutored in the use of soul gems to imbue auras of static without the need for spells. These arts I will now explain.

Flesh used for the purpose may be preserved indefinitely provided it is not allowed to necrotise. Once rot sets in, the muscles gradually lose their ability to contract. Mort flesh is only of use to traditional necromancers.
To preserve the flesh and tendons, they should be removed as soon after death as possible. Once removed they must be preserved in a suitable preparation. If there is nothing of superior use to hand, ordinary salt will stay the touch of Peryite for a short time, but cannot be used indefinitely. Frost salts, while expensive, will preserve a body for a long time, especially if the flesh is packed with ice. Fire salts should be avoided as they will quicken the flesh. Void salts are the best single substance to use for preservation, as they can keep the flesh in a revivifiable state almost indefinitely. 
Other cheaper solutions include physician's aether or alcohol, preparations of disease resistance and the venom of the Frostbite Spider of Skyrim. A mixture of all of these with void salts will produce a material that can sustain flesh in perpetuity.

A skeleton of any kind may be used provided that it has not been thrice-tainted by Arkay. Lengths of flesh and sinew may then be lain along the long-bones as according to known anatomy, and pinned into place using any suitable means. The bones must be connected according to their proper motion and joins, ideally with wire and sinew, and the whole effigy will finally be covered in skin, stitched into position. One must be careful to seek good quality parts for the creation of the flesh-golem, for it is not a rite easily performed.

A soul gem, of lesser to greater magnitude and furnished with the soul of such manner of beast or imp to fill it, should be charged with the use of the shock spell, and this will enable the construct to move. A second soul gem of similar potence should be placed in the cranial cavity. This will afford the flesh-atronach with a semblance of intellect.

The flesh atronach will have no blood of its own, nor does it possess a living body's natural healing processes. In order for the material of the being to refrain from perishing, it should be used frequently so that the fulmenic energies of the shock spells will keep the flesh in a fit state. This may also involve regular replacement of the soul gems. When not in use, a golem should be kept ideally in an ice-cold environment. Other magicians have found creative ways to bypass the process of rot, including the use of daedra hearts and dwemer mechanisms.

The atronach must be thus awoken, at midnight when the Serpent constellation can be seen, and the forbidden rites of XERO-LYG must be invoked. This will call to your workings the oversight of the never-star, the point of absolute sky-flesh, for flesh is one of the very elements of creation, as are darkness and light, and the wind and the earth themselves. This fell knowledge is oft forgotten by the magicians of the present.

If your symbols have been drawn true, your morpholiths duly imbued and your workings of the flesh performed to the standards necessary, the atronach will indeed awaken. Our tutor claims that she regularly performs these mysteries in the court of the Daedric prince Sheogorath, and that she makes her home in that incomprehensible realm of his. Of her claims I have no reason to doubt, for the very act of calling forth such a behemoth is surely an act of the greatest insanity.
