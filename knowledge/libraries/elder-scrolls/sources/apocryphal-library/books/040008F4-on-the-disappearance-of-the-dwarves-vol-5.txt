Our third contribution on the Disappearance itself comes from the late Borgusilus Malier, who published this paper back in the third era. It details a fanciful but intriguing interpretation of the incident, featuring a grand 'journey' that the Dwemer undertook away from Mundus, together.


- Maura Calvaine

<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/F_letter.png'>ellow Scholars, I bring you my latest lecture on the Dwemer, this time on their enigmatic disappearance from our very corporeal plane.

In the Sixth-Hundred and Sixty-Eighth Year of the First Era of our Lord Akatosh's reckoning, there did occur the now widely-debated event of the Disappearance of the Deep Elves, the Dwarves, anon the 'Dwemer' of old. The debate has roared on many a side, with many learned and otherwise peaceful theologians, mages and collegiates physically coming to blows over the affair. No longer, for I, Master Malier of the Arcane University, aim to settle this contentious matter for good! And so, under the sagacious gaze of Julianos, we turn our solemn attention to this weighty affair.

One of the most puzzling peculiarities of the matter, one that has caused every beard from Hegathe to High Hrothgar to be stroked in equal consternation, is where this ancient race of scholars disappeared to. Some place the time of their disappearance around the time of the Battle of Red Mountain, but the ease of their defeat by the Dunmer forces proves a troublesome detail. The Dunmer were arrayed largely in armour of chitin and leather, and though great in numbers and valour, they were confronting a foe armed with the wisdom of Tonal Magic, and whose very legions constituted Men of Brass, whose construction repelled simple means of violence and whose loss meant little to a race who could simply manufacture more of these grim-countenanced simulacra.

While it is possible that a force of Dwarves did remain at Red Mountain, it may simply be that they had already left entirely - and that the Dunmer were merely fighting these Brass guardians towards the end, who had been employed by a rabble of Nordic and Orc bandits, whom the Elves took to be 'Mercenaries' of their Dwarven foes.

So, to where did the former inhabitants of the Red Mountain, and all the other strange and now-desolate tomb cities of the Dwarves, leave? The end-matter of that question cannot be answered, but what we can extrapolate is this.

The Dwarves were linked by what we know as 'The Calling'. This allowed them not only to express themselves to each other in thought alone, without the need to raise their voices, and across far greater a distance, but also to share their ideals and ambitions with the greater number of their race.

We also know that their chief spellwright, Kagrenak, had been developing a new way of thinking. Dissatisfied with the world in which they lived, he had proposed that they seek a new life elsewhere, a great journey on which they would find a better life in each others' good company. This philosophy was called the New Medium, a new pattern of existence for their whole race. 

And so, it was not with slaughter that the Dwarves of old left the world, but rather with peace. They lay down their tools and their weapons and found in each other a sanctity of common purpose from which those of us left behind on Nirn could learn a great deal.
