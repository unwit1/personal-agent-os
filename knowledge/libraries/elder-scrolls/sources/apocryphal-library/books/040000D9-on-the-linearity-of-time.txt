A simplified explanation of time and creation

<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>ime is, by its nature, very complicated. It is attributed to the will of Akatosh, but this too is one of many ways to simplify a complicated matter. This volume, then, seeks only to expand on the nature of time, the Dawn Era and the time-shattering events called 'Dragon Breaks' - which, the author assures the reader, do exist - in a manner that is accessible. It is not a perfect and accurate model of time. That would require a much lengthier volume.

To understand time, one must understand its beginning. The beginning of time is not exact - before that moment, things must have happened in a confusing order devoid of meaning. It is impossible to truly imagine.

As existence comes into being and the first spirits are born, time begins. Some attribute this to the meeting of Anu and Padomay, and others attribute it to the birth of Anui-El, Satakal or other cosmological forces of Everything.

At first, Time is the ordering of events so that one must precede another. It is the syntactic ordering of the Aurbis, just as words are arranged on a page so that they might be read. However, it is not impossible to read a sentence backwards, or to read the sentence in any order one chooses. So too was this the case for time.

Eventually a spirit emerges who is the god of time. This god we shall call Auri-El, as was the ancestor spirit of the Aldmer, and we shall not get to the matter of 'Akatosh' until later.

Auri-El is, in this Elven-influenced model of time, the chief of the gods. It is he who gives his approval to Lorkhan when the latter suggests the creation of Mundus.

Lorkhan, it is noted in Aldmeri creation myths, is a limiting force. It is only when the limitations of life in the Aurbis are realised that his trickery is unveiled and the Dawn War begins. He has duped the gods, so it is told, into creating a world with finite life. His motivations for doing so may be variously complex.

Eventually, he is thwarted by Trinimac and the war comes to an end. His heart is thrown into the East by Auri-El, and time becomes linear, marking the beginning of the Merethic Era.

The question now is; why does time become linear? How does Auri-El cease to manage non-linearity within time?

The simple answer that must suffice for this volume is that Lorkhan did not truly die. His heart, which is the heart of the world, ensures that he can never truly pass away without the world itself coming to an end. And so in order to keep him prone, Auri-El has to keep him like that. The act of keeping him prone limits Auri-El so that time can only flow in one direction.

In order to fathom how this makes Akatosh, picture Auri-El as a large eagle or other bird (as the Altmer often portray their gods), and Lorkhan as a snake (as he is among the Redguard, as 'Sep'). 

These two animals have been fighting. The bird is stronger and with a powerful beak and talons, but the snake has a deadly venom. Eventually, the bird pins the head of the snake to the ground so that it cannot writhe free, but in doing so it cannot fly away. To do so would be to release the serpent.

Thus, Akatosh - the god of linear time - is Auri-El, but limited by the troublesome nature of Lorkhan. Therefore, one might argue, Akatosh as a concept must contain a measure of Lorkhan. This explains, perhaps, the unusual nature of the Amulet of Kings, sacred relic of Akatosh made from a drop of Lorkhan's blood.

Now let us consider the great seers who receive visions of the gods. If one of them views Akatosh, what do they see? Eagle or snake? Perhaps they somehow view both at once, and describe a being winged like a bird but scaled like a snake - a dragon.

Finally, we get to the matter of Dragon Breaks. These are events when time ceases to flow in a linear manner, when for a period the chaotic nature of the Dawn Era returns to Nirn.

By now you have probably already solved it; a Dragon Break is any event that causes Akatosh to lift his claw from Lorkhan, literally 'breaking the dragon' back into bird and snake. 

Take for example the Middle Dawn, where the Marukhati attempted to split Auri-El from Akatosh. They were successful, it would appear - and Lorkhan notoriously wandered the world in human form for much of that un-time. Why does the Numidium break time? Because it is - or was - powered by the Heart of Lorkhan (or its facsimile), which is enough to make Lorkhan 'alive' again, just for a moment. Free of Auri-El's grasp, just for a moment.

Perhaps this simplified model of time will make it easier to unravel the true complexities of the Dragon God, the greatest of which remain a mystery to even the most seasoned scholars.
