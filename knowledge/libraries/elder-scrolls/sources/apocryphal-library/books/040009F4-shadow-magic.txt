<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/W_letter.png'>hen one looks upon a shadow, it is rational to assume that, due to an object's presence between the light and the shadow, that the shadow is merely the absence of the light that shines upon the object. This is not only rational, but also true. However, light is not simply a projection of radiance from a source - it is one of the foundations of magicka.

The magickal constituent of light is called varliance, hence the Ayleidoon 'varla' (star). Starlight and sunlight are the most pure forms of magicka, and the only ones ideal for spellcasting, but all light has some inherent magickal property. Varliance is not simply magick from light - it is also a representation of exactitude. When light shines upon something, generally people will see the same thing. It is deterministic.

Darkness, therefore, is indeterministic. The Daedric Prince Nocturnal is the least well understood of all her peers, and is often equated with a primordial darkness from which all other things of all possibilities emerged. 

From this, we derive the chronocular model of possibility. Time flow is linear, and attempts to disrupt that flow without the use of extremely potent magicka will only have minimal effects. Chronocule flow is very hard to reverse, but given that we can only see one chrono-event when witnessed, it gives rise to the notion that in darkness, other events happen simultaneously, and only when the area becomes lit do these possibilities collapse into the one with the highest probability.

Now, after an event has already occurred, it is not possible to simply shed darkness on a scenario and expect another event from a previous split-point to happen instead. Nor is it is possible - at current - to change a prior event my magickal means, other than by the dangerous methods of chronomancy described above, which would normally involve short-distance time travel - something any mage should be keen to avoid.

However, consider that a shadow is also a conflict of light and dark. To put it in better terms, it is a conflict between exactitude and inexactitude. Order and chaos, in a microcosm of the creation of existence.

From one shadow, there comes the echo of things that could have been. The other possibilities that we, in one 'branch' of existence, collectively did not witness. Therefore a mage with the correct understanding of varliance, chronocules and time itself could - and indeed can - invoke the shadows of things that could have happened. 

Metaphysically, this can be scaled up - greater things than light and dark cause shadows. All things that are in conflict, which is to say where there are multiple outcomes, will create a 'shadow', a pool of unwitnessed possibilities. Shadows are caused during times of war, strife and bloodshed. They are caused by debates and even healthy competition. If one can access the power of these shadows, one could call forth magickal effects of the could-have-been. 

As an experimental proof of concept, an injured fox was treated with a shadow spell, tapping into an existence where it did not lose the fight that hurt it. The wound was indeed healed, but the fox was hightly distorted and misshapen afterwards, and had to be dealt a mercy strike.

It was determined that the root cause of this was the amount of light used in the experiment - and so it was repeated with an injured wolf in comparative darkness. The wolf was healed, and with only minor distortion around the wound site. It was concluded that varliance interrupts shadow magick by enforcing exactitude, and the resulting disfigurement was a result of the two forces in conflict.

Therefore such experiments should be conducted in darkness, except perhaps where one might wish to disfigure the subject. Shrivelling spells become a possibility, where a minor change could be affected upon an individual in stark light, causing them to wither under the conflicting forces.

This would be, perhaps, an irresponsible use of this power. Ideally all new forms of magick should be used for the betterment of all. In conclusion, it should be stated that this is still a very dangerous form of magick that exists only in its nascence - contributions to the research of this new branch of understanding should not be taken lightly.
