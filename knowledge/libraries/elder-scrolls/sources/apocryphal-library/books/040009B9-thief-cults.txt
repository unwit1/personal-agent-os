<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>hieves are, in general, an impious folk - the Divines do not look favourably upon lawbreakers. The Daedra might ask any number of things of their worshippers, including theft, but it is unusual for thieves to commit themselves to them. Not unheard of, however.
Thief Cults are those groups of thieves, bandits and brigands whose activities fulfil some grander ritual purpose, whether directly related to a god or not. The orders of assassins are not included in this work, because the activities performed in the honour of Mephala and the Night Mother are more well-known.

The Baandari are a group of merchants from Elsweyr, who honour the thief-god Baan Dar. Little is known about this god - his worship is prevalent in Elsweyr and Valenwood, but almost unheard of in the rest of Tamriel. Occasional references to his being honoured by members of the Thieves Guild do exist, however.

Also known as the Avenger and the God with a Thousand Faces, Baan Dar is something of an irregularity in Tamrielic worship. Supposed by some to be connected to the Thief constellation, this has never been proven.
The Baandari who follow his word are ill-regarded by most people they encounter, but they are renowned for the quality of their wares. They are reported to be thieves and smugglers, using their god's favour to assist them in their crimes. The following creed has been linked to this group:

Do not slander another child of the Baandari.
Remain mindful in all dealings.
A gift must be returned in kind.
A fair trade need not be fair only in gold.
Truth and cleverness need not be enemies.
Find what is lost, trade what is found, and leave what has no purpose.

Much of this god's work has been recorded in a series of documents called the 'Scrolls of Baan Dar', whose translator, Arkan the Gifted, believed that Baan Dar was not a single god but rather a series of spirits, entities or simple urges to use trickery against injustice. The use of cleverness in the face of tyranny is a common theme in his worship. Certain elements of the Thieves Guild have adhered to this philosophy, such as the notorious Gray Fox of the late Third Era.

Another such group well known for their supposed 'honour' as thieves was the Bal Molagmer. They were notorious in Morrowind throughout the Second Era, before fading into obscurity not long after the absorption of that province into Tiber Septim's new Empire.
Their name stems from the Ehlnofex-Dunmeris, meaning 'Stone Fire-Men', after their habit of taking lava stone from the top of Red Mountain, with their special gloves that prevented their hands being damaged. The exact purpose of this ritual is still not known, but it may be linked to notions of 'stealing fire from the gods' that are prevalent in Ashlander folk tradition.

The manifesto of the Bal Molagmer was to take what had been wrongfully seized by authority, and return it to those who had been wronged. They were known on sight by their scorched-palm gloves, which were nevertheless exquisitely made.

Their disappearance in the early Third Era was noted by many of Vvardenfell's temple Ordinator records, but curiously so was their 'return'. In the years around the cleansing of the Blight and the discontinuation of the Ghostfence, a series of thefts were carried out by an unknown person or persons. Individuals around Vvardenfell claimed that they had been returned seized or stolen goods by an individual or uncertain aspect wearing the gloves of legend.

Whatever the case, they have not been heard from in the fourth era yet. Whether or not their curious credo is linked to an actual god is unknown - they remain another of Tamriel's mysteries.

Strangest of all is the tale of the Nightingales. They have never been confirmed to exist, but the idea of a cult of thieves dedicated to Nocturnal is too captivating for many to disbelieve. Supposedly hailing from Skyrim, they are known only by the shape of their crest - a bird holding a black moon in its wings - and their name. 

Many doubt their existence. There have been two confirmed appearances of this symbol, and one piece of graffiti in a prison cell linking the name of the group to Nocturnal, but nothing else in Skyrim. However, It has been noted that Queen Barenziah of Wayrest was once visited by a thief using the masquerade-name of 'The Nightingale', in order to elicit from her the location of the Staff of Chaos. That thief was none other than Jagar Tharn, the nefarious battlemage of Uriel Septim VII, who caused the Imperial Simulacrum incident.

Whether or not Jagar Tharn was the head of this group is unknown - little has been heard from them in some time, if they are even anything other than an elaborate distraction from the Thieves Guild present in The Rift. 

The Khajiit god known as Rajhin is still worshipped to this day. Supposedly born a mortal Khajiit in the slums of Black Kiergo in Senchal, his thefts of many great treasures eventually granted him the title of Thief God. He supposedly stole a ring from Mephala herself, which became known as the Ring of the Khajiit, which allowed the wearer to become as swift as the wind. He is also written to have stolen the Oghma Infinium of the Hermaeus Mora, and even the Empress Kintyra's tattoo.

Eventually the ring slipped from his finger when he was surrounded by foes, and his mortal life came to an end. Since then he has become the Thief God, though whether or not his worship overlaps or has been conflated with that of Baan Dar is unclear. 

On the 13th of Frostfall when the Witches' Festival is celebrated, it is not uncommon for cat-masks to be worn in honour of Rajhin. This is in part due to the day's association with Mephala. At the New Life festival, Khajiit will perform the Trial of Five-Clawed Guile. This involves stealing coins or sweets from locked boxes as a test of wit and perserverance.

Little about Rajhin's church is known. It is not confirmed that he has one, which is of course hardly confirmation that he doesn't have one. That is the nature of the cults of thieves - bonds of secrecy that keep the waking world distracted from their own pockets.
