The Ayleid practice of flesh sculpting survives only in rudimentary forms today, such as the distinguished chirurgeons of Cloudrest, a practice which dates back to the influx of Ayleid refugees in the First Era. While this practice is benign and has afforded long health to its clientele, the dark art from which it arose was anything but.

It was this foul craft that first inspired the old humans of Cyrod to overthrow their Daedra worshipping masters under the command of Queen Alessia. Slaves were sought who could be altered into living works of art, experiencing unbelievable agony for the amusement of their elven overlords. 

This practice was, accordingly, the result of the Ayleid's Daedra veneration. It does not seem to have originated as a particular expression of magic, rather it seems to have been something they created in league with the Princes of Oblivion. Exactly which princes lent their skill to the perfection of the art is unknown, save for Molag Bal whose assistance is well known. Namira, Mephala and Vaermina are suspected also.

The art was hard to master - too much pressure or alteration in the wrong place could cause the unfortunate victim to haemorrhage prematurely, which was most certainly a mercy. Skin was warped, bones were altered, until what was once a man now resembled a flowering plant, a wheel, a vessel or some other ornament.

Morihaus, the winged bull, oversaw the destruction of these gardens. It is not known if any survived being saved from their torment, indeed it is hard to imagine that if any could, that they would have retained their sanity. Merciful blows brought an end to the miserable lives of the sculptures of living flesh.
