The Shezarrine



by Prioress Marciana


Gottlesfont Priory

<p align="left">
[pagebreak]

<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/S_letter.png'>hezarr, brother to the Divines, creator of the world, and the missing god. Few pray to him, but yet through his works are we granted the power to fulfil our destiny. Thus is Shezarr remembered for his act of sacrifice, that being creation.

On the bones of this god have we built our Empire, and his mortal remains are the very ground we walk upon. But upon this same holy ground, every so often once an era may have turned, there walks a stranger with familiar aspect. This wanderer, never content to rest upon a throne for long, or to occupy himself with the mundanities of everyday life, is the aspect of Shezarr - and his passage can bring change both wonderful and terrible.

Sundered from his heart at the beginning of time in a battle with Auriel, the God of the Elves, he is considered Dead - but his spirit arrives upon the land in times of great need, in mortal form. The names given of the Shezarrine in 'Before the Ages of Man' are; Pelinal Whitestrake, Ysmir, Hans the Fox and Harrald Hairy-Breeks. The infamous Lich Mannimarco, King of Worms and thrice cursed be his name, assigned the names Arnand the Fox and Arctus to this list, when he was questioned on the matter of the Middle Dawn. 
[pagebreak]
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/P_letter.png'>elinal Whitestrake

Pelinal is the most well-known appearance of the Shezarrine, though to refer to him as such was often a death sentence, for he despised those who spoke 'God-Logic'. He was invoked by the first Dragonborn, Alessia, and arrived in Cyrodiil clad in strange armour unknown to the period. The legends claim that he was revealed to have within his chest a gaping cavity, in which a red diamond shone. This is one of the two common explanations for the origin of the Amulet of Kings - the other being that it was a drop of Shezarr's blood, which landed in an Ayleid well as his heart soared across the skies to form Red Mountain.

A great many strange deeds are attributed to Pelinal. While Alessia's forces fought out her battles under her guidance and command, Pelinal seems to have sought out enemy kings and champions, tearing into them with unmatched ferocity. In the darkest bouts of his battle madness, he is claimed to have erased whole areas from existence. 

Alongside his greatest ally, Morihaus Breath-of-Kyne, he helped Alessia achieve her victory. He died in the process, being severed into eight pieces by the sorceror-king Umaril, but returned to Alessia on her death-bed and bestowed upon her the Amulet of Kings. 

The symbolism of Shezarr placing his 'heart' with the first Dragonborn, a scion of Akatosh himself, cannot be ignored. While the true heart of Shezarr beat under Red Mountain, the drop of blood that he held within himself ended up being the Heart that kept the Covenant of Akatosh together from Alessia's death up until the end of the Oblivion Crisis.

He is sometimes known as 'Pelinal the Third', because he was believed to have manifested twice before.
[pagebreak]
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/Y_letter.png'>smir Wulfharth

The title 'Ysmir' confers such weight that it is almost impossible to subtract it from that of blessed Talos himself. Supposedly a title for Nordic kings, it is seldom ever used in honorifics aside from Wulfharth or Tiber Septim.

Wulfharth reigned in the first era, his date of birth unknown but the place is recorded as Atmora. He is remembered for his power as a Tongue, whose voice was so powerful that he could not swear in his oaths verbally. He is known thus as Shor's Tongue (for Shor is how Shezarr is known among the Nords), and is remembered for extraordinary deeds, including helping Shor's ghost fight the ghost of Alduin the World-Eater, and reversing the curse that Orkey placed upon the Nords. 

The Songs of King Wulfharth state that he was often in the presence of Shor's spirit. The tales are old and cannot be confirmed one way or the other, so if they are true we still cannot be sure if this is to be taken literally, or whether it demonstrates the fact that he was himself Shezarr reborn. 

Even after his death, he returns with Shor's aid, reborn as the Ash King. His presence at the Battle of Red Mountain is attested in the Songs, and it is even claimed that he was able to reach the Heart of Shezarr itself, and for a while the missing god had his heart back. He is killed before he can completely renew himself, though.

He appears again at the time of Tiber Septim, according to a confusing tract known as the Arcturian Heresy, authored by 'Ysmir Kingmaker, the Underking'. If this tract is to be believed, the Ash King appeared to young Tiber Septim and granted him his early victories in Cuhlecain's time.  He is later slain by Zurin Arctus during the creation of the Mantella, the artefact that Tiber used to power the Numidium in his final conquests. 

Given that Zurin Arctus is also supposedly the Underking that haunted the Third Era, and that the Mantella was supposed to be used in place of Shezarr's heart when powering the Numidium, it's clear how the Arcturian Heresy became so popular. But remember always this simple truth; Talos became a Divine through the grace of the gods. He is Aedra, and the journals of the late Martin Septim prove this.

Whether or not Wulfharth himself was the Shezarrine, or whether Ysmir (sometimes rendered as 'Dragon of the North') is the manifestation and it possessed the king, is unknown.
[pagebreak]
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/H_letter.png'>ans the Fox

Of Hans, even less is known. Only very scattered legends refer to him at all - most of the sources are secondary, such as 'Before the Ages of Men'. Of primary sources, the only one that affords a reliable connection is a work often considered apocryphal at best - the 'Five Hundred, or Thereabouts, Companions of Ysgramor'. 

The work is often dismissed since it outright changes the names of some well-attested historical figures, such as Yngol and Ylgar whom it claims are the "...ashen-amalgamation of his sons that had survived Sarthaal only to die in the freeze-rains of the returning, named Tsunaltir and Stuhnalmir when alive and now called the Grit-Prince Tstunal..."
Regardless, the work is somewhat ancient and is afforded more than a casual dismissal. The last paragraph is especially confusing, for it seems to mirror the first, but the characters all have names believed to be Dragon-names. 
The character 'Hans the Fox' appears in the first paragraph, and in its place in the last, is 'Pelinaalilargus the Pragmatist'. Pragmatism is often considered a characteristic of Shor the Fox in very old Nordic folklore, and the similarity between 'Pelinaalilargus' and Pelinal is obvious.

Beyond that, there is little that can be said about Hans the Fox, other than him bearing the same soubriquet as Arnand the Fox, who we shall discuss later. But with what we have, it seems there was certainly a tradition of believing that Shezarr, as Shor, was wandering in his mortal form at the time of Ysgramor's return.
[pagebreak]
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/H_letter.png'>arrald Hairy-Breeks

The only time the soubriquet 'Hairy-Breeks' is found is in reference to the other names of Pelinal, though not in the Song. As such, his exact identity is unknown, though some commentators point to King Harald, thirteenth of Ysgramor's line, and the one who relinquished Skyrim's holdings in Atmora. 

The evidence is intriguing, though leaves much to be desired for scholars. Nevertheless, their arguments are presented thus.

King Harald is said to have died at the age of One-Hundred and Eight, the exact same age that Tiber Septim died at - both considered unusually old for Men. Though Tiber Septim is not usually associated directly with Shezarr, many theologists believe that there is an inherent link between the dragon blood and the missing god.

His forswearing of his titles in Atmora is seen as a desire for a new beginning, of leaving old things in the past and setting eyes on somethign new. This is similar to the Shezarrine's wandering aspect, never content to stay in the same place for long. 

His rule is associated with the inspiration of humanity to push forwards and expland across Tamriel, and ever to push aside the yoke of elven slavers. This is very in keeping with aspects such as Pelinal whose focus on human emancipation is well known. 

Few supernatural feats are ascribed to him, though he is known for crafting the Jagged Crown from the bones and teeth of ancient dragons. This ancient crown has been lost since the time of King Borgas, but for many years it was the symbol of Skyrim's rule.
[pagebreak]
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/A_letter.png'>rnand the Fox

Though the name Arnand is rarely spoken, it is usually equated with Zurin Arctus. The origin of either name is unclear - Zurin, famously the battlemage of Tiber Septim, was supposedly of old Imperial lineage. He may have been remembered as Arnand in the province of High Rock.

The Fox apellation clearly ties him to Hans, and to the fox-totem of Shor. He is conflated with other instances of the Shezarrine by the King of Worms. But what is most interesting about him is that he is able to construct the Mantella from his own heart. 

The Mantella was a soul gem that was to act as a replacement for the Heart of Lorkhan, built by the Dwarves. Tiber Septim, in his wisdom, used this device to conquer Alinor. The Arcturian Heresy claims that Tiber betrayed Zurin to create it, but the truth is that Zurin died defending the Emperor from the Ash King. With the Ash King's soul in Zurin's heart, Tiber uses it to power the brass golem.

For years after, the Underking haunted Tamriel, hungry to reclaim his heart. Only when the Warp in the West finished was the Underking reuinited with his divine heart, and then he blessed by Talos himself with everlasting rest.

Since then, the Shezarrine has not been known to walk the lands. Perhaps he will return again heal the realms or to bring great war to the land. Or perhaps now, with the Oblivion Crisis over, the world can finally be at peace everlasting.
