<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/F_letter.png'>or those outside of Hammerfell, the mythology of our people can be somewhat puzzling. The gods of our ancient myths do not have the same names as the Aedra or the Divines. The way that they act, interact and create is not the same. So it is the duty of this Forebear scholar to elucidate the peoples of the Empire in the nature of the Redguard faith.

In the beginning, there is only Satak. He is a great serpent, the First Serpent no less, but he is alone in the void. And he is light and order, he is everything. Therefore in order to exist, his opposite had to exist - and that was Akel, who is the hunger of Satak, and nothingness, and disorder.

Satak, you might notice, is similar to Anu in the Monomyth. Therefore his counterpart is much like Padomay, who is darkness. It may be helpful to consider them as such in order to understand their nature.

Satak's hunger drives him to eat - but there is only one thing he can eat, which is himself. And so he consumes himself, leaving room within the space he occupied for the first time. Things that exist in this space lead brief, chaotic lives - compare this with the Twelve Worlds of Creation mythology.

Eventually, he consumes his own heart, and dies. But the hunger does not die, and so the First Serpent sheds his skin in order to begin eating again. Thus is born Satakal, who is made of both Satak and Akel. Satakal devours himself again and again, each time leaving behind is old skin.

Satakal is hard to syncretise into the other Anuic faiths, because he is not exactly the Aurbis and not exactly Akatosh. He is comparable in some ways to Alduin, the Nordic aspect of Akatosh that devours the world in each age. He is like Anui-El and Sithis, both at once.

The 'skins' that he leaves behind are the kalpas. Each one is the dead remnant of a previous age - each age being marked by its beginning in the Dawn Era and end in the Dusk Era.

The constant destruction of the world was of concern to those who lived there, however, and so they learned how to avoid it by moving from one skin to the next. The strongest of these spirits was Ruptga, called Tall Papa because he was the largest of the spirits, able to arrange the stars so that they could lead the way to other skins. He arranged the stars to point the way for the spirits to reach a place called the Far Shores, where they could await the next skin while avoiding the devouring.

Ruptga is more similar to Auri-El and Akatosh here - he is the chief of the wandering spirits, and he is able to shape the stars (thus he is also like Magnus) in order to make their afterlife, the far shores, where they can await the next kalpa.

But after several iterations, Ruptga had far too many spirits to guide. And so he made Sep, a new guide, from the remnants of several skins. Sep was crafty and clever but also had Akel's hunger within him, and he even ate some of the spirits he was supposed to lead.

One can easily draw parallels with Lorkhan and Shor in other myths here - and this becomes all the more clear when he announces to Ruptga that he is tired of this constant skin-hopping, and suggests making a new world instead.

Many spirits join him - and the idea has much merit, it seems, because in this new world it is not required to jump from skin to skin constantly. But they were far from Satakal now, and they were no longer immortal. They began to die, and those who lived pleaded with Ruptga to take them back.

This is clearly the foundation of Mundus, as Lorkhan is supposed to have done in the myths around the Dawn Era. Ruptga's subsequent refusal - or perhaps inability - to take the spirits back is a detail not found in other cosmologies, however.

He instead tells the spirits that they must find other ways to follow the stars, and that they may have to live on through their children. And so they procreate, and thus begin the mortal races.

Ruptga then kills Sep, and the hunger falls from his mouth. The mortal races may strive for immortality by trying to reach the Far Shores, but Sep may only slink around Nirn in dead skins, or swim among the stars in the sky, trying to eat them.

The hunger, of course, is similar to myths of the Heart of Lorkhan. His wandering around in dead skin is not unlike the myths of Shezarr-who-Wanders, a dead god in mortal form aiding or troubling mortal kind, and the hunger in the sky is comparable to dark myths of the Void Ghost or Serpent Constellation to which astronomers attribute much grief and doom.
