Cults of Tamriel

The Dark Brotherhood

by Gwendoline Denwick
</p>
<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>he Dark Brotherhood are not often thought of as a religious order, since they make their living as cruel killers who take lives in exchange for the lustre of gold.

According to conventional history, they formed in the Second Era following turmoil surrounding the death of the last Potentate of the second empire, when they broke away from the Morag Tong and appeared to cease their veneration of Mephala. The Morag Tong are covered in another volume and will not be discussed in depth here, but their status as a warrior-cult is well documented. The Dark Brotherhood, it was believed for a time, were not.

Another tale of their foundation states that they were not in fact formerly those of the Morag Tong, but errant members of the Thieves Guild who found their heists went more easily if they murdered the occupants of the houses from which they burgled. The leader of this new offshoot of murderers, the 'Night Mother', accepted renegades from the Morag Tong when the order was illegalised following Savirien-Chorak's death.

Either way, the two guilds have a traditional rivalry and very different modes of operation. The Brotherhood does not stand by any ancient honourable customs, nor does it demand any kind of transparency with its contracts. When the coin is paid, the deed is done.

The cult aspect of the Brotherhood is therefore easy to overlook - but it is there. There is a deep and hidden secret among the assassins that remains unknown to the world, but the manner in which they are contacted is the most obvious clue to this. It is not something they take particular pains to conceal either - a book which shall not be named here has made the rounds, describing in detail the vile and hideous ritual required to contact the Brotherhood. 

In this tome, there are made mention of the 'Night Mother', who is believed to be able to 'hear' the casting of the ritual and dispense an assassin to arrange the task.

The other name is 'Sithis', an apocryphal figure of Aldmeri legend related to notions of the void beyond the stars. In reality, this figure is probably an aspect of Namira or Sheor.

How the Brotherhood are able to overhear the casting of these rituals is unknown, as is the nature of the worship in which they engage. But their identity as a religious sect cannot be ignored. Those who seek to dispense with a foe for the mere cost of gold should be wary of what manner of cost should be incurred upon their soul.
