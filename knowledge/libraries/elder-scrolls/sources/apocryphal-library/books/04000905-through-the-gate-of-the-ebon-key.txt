By Sethurvaine

<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/I_letter.png'>n a sombre room hung with faded tapestries showing the long-forgotten victories of Old Cyrod, I and three others stood, our palms planted on the varnish-oak table, its legs bedecked with carvings too termite-riddled to tell their ancient woodwright's name.

Our business, such was the reason for our gathering in that room of withered antiquities, was the strange silver box that my business partner, Master Jule Merrance, had recently acquired in the last will and testament of his old Aunt, Lady Herstwaine, a secretive but well-humoured hermitess during her twilight years. The box, ornate with the filigree of hand-woven Daedric sigils, was tightly locked, and would require an adept hand to open. It was to this purpose that I was to offer my aid to the gathered. If only we had stopped to ponder the meaning of those sigils that glistened a silent warning round the hem of that box; though would knowledge of their meaning really have stayed our hand?

The lock was of enormous complexity; though a key most certainly existed that could open the argent casket, the lock confounded all of my initial attempts to prize open. Eventually, taking my finest pick, I peered down the keyhole, letting the day's remaining, fading light shine off the intricate inner workings. I tutted to myself, and twisted the dials on my best pick - until I was certain that I was holding a device exactly able to open the box before me. And open indeed it did, after what I took to be an hour or so's labours.

When I was granted the honour of opening the box by good Master Jule, I was astounded by what I saw within. 
It was a key! Of no ordinary kind, but what seemed to be bronze teeth jutted out in manifold directions. It was large, and the head of the key was not simple wrought metal, but what looked to my astonished eye for all the world like jade embedded in ebony.

"By Sheor!", exclaimed Jule, and grabbed for a book, its leather cover the hue of birch-moss, and opened it to a certain page, and there, painted elegantly onto the page was that very same key. And there, above it in plain print; THE SKELETON KEY OF NOCTURNAL.

"By Sheor", the rest of us echoed. How had this man's old Aunt come into the property of one of the ancient Daedric artefacts of forbidden legend? And furthermore, what mischief was Master Jule liable to begin when he had time to consider the implications of his most unusual acquisition?

That night, having been invited to stay, we drank fine Betony wine and joked about all the heists we would manage now that we had the Key. Of course, we were no thieves - I had put that part of my life behind me long ago - but we enacted humorous gambits where we might rob the wealth of the greatest and richest magnates of our time.

And so we eventually bid each other a fair night's sleep, and settled down to bed. My dreams were full of fancy that night, as they rarely are these days; I put it down to the excitement of the discovery, my old skills seeing some use, and the strong, rather heady wine of which we'd partaken. But in the steady hum of my dreams, I sensed a rather insistent repetition:

AE UR DRA KARAN GUME PADHOME

AE UR DRA GARLAS HAL PADHOME

DAE UR DRA ALTADOON GUME PADHOME

[pagebreak]
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>he next morning, we awoke to find that Master Jule had not yet arisen. When we enquired with the servant, we were informed that indeed he had not yet left his room. We knocked, eager to start the day's business, only to be met with the first of many long silences that will forever haunt my waking dreams.

Trying the door, we opened it to reveal that he was not present: His bed was tousled, as if he had clearly been there, but his window was firmly bolted from within and the servant swore that he had not opened his door that night.

We assumed at first that he must have stolen away to use the Skeleton Key on some unsuspecting victim, but the malevolent artefact sat right there, on his bedside armoire. 

After some hours of deliberation, it was agreed; my friends would attempt to find Master Jule, and I would take custody of the Skeleton Key until  his return. I was confident that I would be able to keep the artefact safe, but just to be sure I took with me the strange silver box in which it had come to us, as well as the book of artefacts.

I spent the evening at home, drinking Dunmeri flin and musing upon what fate could have befallen my old colleague and friend. I opened the strange book with its moss-green cover, and leafed through the pages. Here were painted many of the strange and forgotten weapons, arms and tools passed down into myth. Here was described the eldritch tome, the Mysterium Xarxes, written by the demon-prince Mehrunes Dagon, whose words contained the key to enlightenment. Here was depicted the Coffer of King Orghnum, ruler of the Sea Elves, whose contents never depleted no matter how much the owner spent. The Dragon Bone Armour of the ancient wizard Zurin Arctus, and more besides, before I finally found the page with the Key.

The Skeleton Key, I learned, can open any lock. No lock can be made for a door which it cannot open. Simple application of natural philosophy would make such a thing impossible, as any scholar could tell you - and even magic spells, such as the 'Open Door' spell of the Dunmer sorceror Rels Ondusi, cannot open every lock in Tamriel. But the primeval nature of the Skeleton Key, wrought in unfathomable aeons before the first ancestors of Man clawed their way out of the ice of Atmora, can open any lock - be they physical, or of the mind, or of the spirit, or of the very bones of the earth.
I then translated the sigils on the Silver Box - my translation of those gleaming, eldritch runes was inexact but I managed:

"The first giver is a shield against the start of all true houses, but also a passage to it, and not a weapon against it."

That night, having allowed myself to drift to sleep in my chair, my dreams bore stranger fruit yet. I walked through an ebony mere, the lapping of silent waves beating against a sightless shore. I wandered in a great towering darkness. Around me fluttered what felt like bats or crows or ravens. Eventually, I espied a figure in the darkness, around whom glowed an aura darker than all the other night around. I called out, hoping it might be Jule - but my voice fell dead in that cyclopean void, my words just deafening nothingness that somehow caught the attention of the figure. The woman, for such she was, drifted over to me and looked down with a countenance that was a void within a void.

She gestured for me to follow her, and she led me to a great gate of basalt, the strange green light drifting from nowhere barely illuminating those signs and runes on its surface, that even had I been able to see I would not have been able to read.

Against all my will and understanding, I placed the Skeleton Key to where there was no lock, and turned. The great basalt doors opened just a crack, just enough for me to peer within.

What I saw within almost drove me mad, in fact I cannot say for certain now, in my waking hours, that it did not. For what I looked upon was the very void of the dawn times, from which all reality sprang in spite of itself, whose size is both innumerable and nothing at all. The incomprehensible vastness of the void before me, even half-glimpsed, filled me with a terror of the night and all its shadows, where the souls of godless wretches go to be forgotten, from where the most indescribable monstrosities and phantasms of Oblivion crawled in the ages before mortals. I looked up.

I woke up screaming, and did not stop until my neighbours knocked on my door, troubled by my cacophony. I explained that I had been dreaming badly, and that was not untrue - but I did not tell them the last thing I saw before I screamed and shut the gate tight, which was two enormous voids-within-voids hanging in the nothingness, as if two dead existences lay before me, their creation spent, facing each other, like two huge sockets in a skull larger than the infinite.

I will never claim to understand Nocturnal, the holder of the Key, who I believe has now removed it from my custody as I can no longer find it anywhere in my house. Of Master Jule, there has still been no word. My dreams are still haunted by the void of all voids, and I pray that I locked the door when I still had the key. Whenever I dream that I am there, I always wake up before I find out.
