The Myth of Dragons

by Artely Deschuur

</p>
[pagebreak]
<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/D_letter.png'>ragons. The word conjures up the image of a great winged reptile, crowned with horns, breathing terrible fire upon its foes. Yet this conjuration is, in fact, mere illusion.

The Dragon God of Time, Lord Akatosh, is very real. His divine image has appeared in the dreams of many, and indeed he was witnessed above the Temple of the One in all his glory by many eyewitnesses just a decade ago. But while Akatosh is the substance of fact, the notion of an intelligent race that once bore his image is mere fiction.

The earliest tales of dragons go back to the Merethic era, when the refugees from Atmora brought with them the faith of that distant land. After some time, the narrative claims that they began a 'Dragon War' against these creatures, who had supposedly become as cruel tyrants.
Of course, the likely true explanation from this lies in their folklore. As they came into contact with the Snow Elves, the Atmorans began to re-evaluate their relationship with the divine. At first, Alduin - their version of Akatosh - had been a kindly god, but on coming into contact with the Elves, they witnessed their version - Auri-El - who they saw as a tyrant coming down to strike Men from existence. Thus, the reality of the 'Dragon War' was that it was simply another word for the strife that existed between the Atmorans and the ancient Falmer, and the 'Dragon Priests' whom the elves fought under were simply those sorceror-kings of Auri-El. 

There are indeed bones found around Skyrim that do seem to be in the shape of what we call dragons, but they are similar to those of the 'Dragonlings' still seen in High Rock today - therefore they are simply likely to be a larger version of those creatures.
Dragonlings do indeed exist, though even the Chantry of Akatosh itself has on occasion had some difficulty distinguishing these reptiles from the dragons of folklore.

There have been some who have claimed to have seen dragons since, that cannot simply be dismissed as vivid imagination or outright insanity. Those who looked upon Tiber Septim often could not do so without seeing a dragon. Of course, this is due to his status as Dragonborn. His soul was tied to the nature of Akatosh, whose radiance shone through at these astonished witnesses.

The last recorded 'Dragon sighting' took place at the end of the Third Era, when at Stros M'kai the infamous Naarfilalargus, the Jewel of the Empire, was said to have guarded the palace of the Governor.

This can be ascribed to one of two things.

One is that Admiral Richton had managed to conjure a Mantikora, a strange reptilian beast made in the second era by alchemists and conjurors. They have a particular draconic quality to them, as afforded by the blood of Wamasu-lizards. Wamasu are a real, wild creature native to Black Marsh.

The other is that this 'Jewel of the Empire' was simply the Amulet of Kings, which Tiber Septim had brought with him to Stros M'kai in order to put down the rebellion of the Restless League. Given what Saint Martin was able to do with the Amulet, it is likely that Tiber Septim himself was capable of something similar. What the witnesses of Naarfilalargus in fact saw was the form of Akatosh himself - a far more impressive narrative than some mere creature of flesh and blood.

Ultimately these tales of dragons only serve to mislead the scholar and imbue the casual reader with a sense of mythic grandeur that almost always has a more realistic interpretation.
