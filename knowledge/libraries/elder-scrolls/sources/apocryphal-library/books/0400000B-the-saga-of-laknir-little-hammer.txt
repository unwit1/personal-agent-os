As recorded by Horic the Deaf

[pagebreak]
<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/I_letter.png'>t was at the height of summer in the reign of Jarl Ovarn Bleakstern of Whiterun. Laknir Little-Hammer was a Blacksmith from Angarvunde, and he was the son of Haevar Little-Hammer who had made twelve swords for King Aetheric. He had no wife and no lands or titles of his own, for his forge was sworn to the service of Angarvunde, though he had an apprentice and servant who was called Vakeeza of Lilmoth-Hist, and she was daughter of the distant land of Argonmarch.

In that Summer, Jarl Ovarn arrived in Angarvunde so that he might have his horse Sarvirra re-shod. He left the steed with Laknir along with payment of thirty drakr for the work, with the bond-promise that if this steed would fare well in battle, there would would be more work forthcoming. Laknir, keen that his work should bring more, scorned the iron he would oft use for the task and in its stead took the blade of the broken sword of his uncle Grimr Red-Arm, who had taken the sword from his foe Rhuac Strong-Shield, Dwarf-Champion of the city of Alftand. The blade was of golden brass, a metal that the Dwarves made from the sweat of Kyne and the marrow of Tsun, and he melted it into ingots. He had soon made four shoes that fit the hooves of Sarvirra well.

And so Jarl Ovarn, who was son of Jarl Eivende Bleakstern, who had slain the Wistful Maiden who had haunted all the hold in the years before, did take Sarvirra to battle. Sarvirra was a powerful stallion of mottled grey, and already one of the boldest steeds in all the holds. He defeated Calavaunt the Black and Ungalno Half-Elf in his battles, and it was soon most clear to him that the shoes of Dwarf-brass were indeed a great boon upon his steed.

And so when he returned to Angarvunde, he brought with him a Dwarf-shield that he had won in battle from Ntchgard the Illogical, and paid the wright to make him not only more shoes, but also to ride with him that he would always have his able smith to hand. And so Laknir left the forge and headed off with Vakeeza.

Whiterun had been beset by the dread she-wolf, Ulfeidr Silent-Moon, who was known by day as a woman of flaxen hair most beautiful, and by night as a beast of the dread god Hircyne, who was the dark twin of the Hawk-Mother herself. Tales tell that in her youth she had been a true wolf, and had become as woman by the Hunt-King's breath. Her host of warriors were the Crimson Pack, and they were both wolves and wolf-men of her own blood. 
Against them stood Jarl Ovarn, whose shield-thane was Ysfride of the Blue Wind, who was harbinger of the Companions of Jorrvaskr, who was daughter of Eise Shallow-Shore the Mystic. At her beck and call were the other Companions, among whose hearty host were Beric Greenstride, Oghmar Twice-Read, Hemmick Eivarsson, Hesta Fallow-Glance, Enderis the Nede, Burgha Shadow-Hammer and her brother Borthu Mild-Storm, Horgan the Unsteady, Bevic the Dwarf and Hoona Sweet-Cradle. Also there was Laknir-Little Hammer the Smith of Angarvunde, and a fyrd from Whiterun of bold warriors and clan-fighters, and Vakeeza daughter of Lilmoth-Hist.

The two hosts clashed in battle in the early hours of night, when the Wolf-kin brought on their own dread aspect. The Crimson Pack numbered fewer but they fought with the rage of the Hunt-King himself. Many died in that battle, and their names are carved on the walls of Ysgramor's tomb.

When Dawn came, the Crimson Pack retreated to the South past Lake Eilenaltr, for they did once more take on the forms of Men. And half the Companions lay dead and the pyre burned high the following eve. 

Jarl Ovarn summoned his surviving warriors one by one to seek counsel. And when it came that he summoned Laknir and Vakeeza, he asked them what weapons might make their next battle against the Crimson Pack go in their favour.

Vakeeza, who had fought so bravely that Ovarn accepted her oath to the Kings of Skyrim, told that in her travels to Whiterun from the lands to the South she had heard that such beasts are weakened by silver, and that blades forged from it are often kept as proof against the hordes of Hircyne. Upon hearing this, Laknir told the Jarl that he could make spear-tips of this if he had enough, but also horse-shoes so that the Jarl's riders could trample their foe.

And so Jarl Ovarn commanded the citizens of Whiterun to hand over all their silver, be it their pitchers or their jewellery or their plates, and he set Laknir to work at the mighty Skyforge making these things. 

Ulfeidr had drawn her pack back to Ancient's Ascent in the mountains between Falcreath and Cyrod. There she waited, with her pack licking their wounds as they prepared to descend on Whiterun Hold once again. But word arrived to her that the Jarl's men were coming instead to her, and so she made sure that her host would not sally forth until the sun had set.

And so that Autumn evening, her Crimson Pack came down into the valley to bring doom to the Companions and the Jarl of Whiterun. Upon seeing the horde descend, Jarl Ovarn blew his horn and his army charged the dread pack. And true to the forging of Laknir Little-Hammer, the silver shoes did indeed bring grave wounds to the wolf-folk.

Ormulf Greyfur slew Horgan the Unsteady, and was slain by Enderis the Nede. Enderis was killed by the Hound of Kreath, who was a dog raised by the wolves. Hemmick Eivarsson's great silver axe cleft the heads of both Bjorn Karg and the Thrice-Howler of Asklunde. Sarvirra herself did bring to death with her hooves three-score wolves and Wynja of the Red Stare, but not before Wynja had slain five banner-thanes of Whiterun and Bevic the Dwarf. Vakeeza daughter of the Lilmoth-Hist struck down Klemmen the Wailing Death, but she was felled by Ulfeidr herself. Jarl Ovarn was struck by a blow from Keinar the Moon-Blessed and would have died from another of his arrows had Sarvirra not reared up to protect him. That great steed took seven arrows before perishing.

At the end of the battle, Ulfeidr realised she had few of her pack left. Let it never be said that she was not as bold as any other child of Atmora, for she commanded her pack to retreat but stayed to prevent them being followed. And she leapt at her foes once more, and before she was lain low from a blow by young Laknir Little-Hammer, she had already slain Burgha Shadow-Hammer. The she-wolf died some time later at the place where she was lain to rest.

Laknir was rewarded well for his service, and he came to join the Companions and he did beat the bellows of the Skyforge. After the battle was done, three stones were raised to remember Vakeeza the Servant, Sarvirra the Steed and Ulfeidr the Pack-Queen. One alongside the companions in Ysgramor's Tomb, one in Angarvunde and one at the site of the battle near Ancient's Rest.
