Varlatropism in Naturally Occuring Meteoric Glass Formations


by Lithnilian of Cloudrest



Gwilym University Press
</p>
[pagebreak]
<p align="left">
A survey of various deep-stratigraphy sites in County Bravil in southern Nibenay revealed that Welkynd stones are indeed, as previously thought impossible, able to coagulate outside of Ayleid ruins. While the orthagonal geometricals of Ayleid architecture are the most germane surfaces for the crystals to accrue, the corpuscules of the welkynd stone had adapted, likely over considerable time, to the heterogene surfaces of caves and abandoned mines. 

Given that the crystalline structures found in Ayleid ruins will, over time, direct themselves towards sources of starlight, or for want of such would grow upwards in search of same (varlatropism), the question remained as to whether those structures detected in natural environments would behave in the same fashion.

A study of seven sites across southern Nibenay revealed that while the stone formations do indeed attempt to grow towards sources of starlight, the effect is moderated somewhat by distance from Ayleid wells. Nevertheless, even those furthest from a well demonstrated crystalline structures in keeping with standard varlatropism. 

It must be concluded therefore that some element within an Ayleid well - either the varliance flow or the meteoric iron nidus around it - are responsible for the increase of varlatropism in Ayleid ruins. But it cannot be overlooked that the process continues to occur in absence of the architecture of the wells, presumably as an innate patterning of the crystalline substance.
