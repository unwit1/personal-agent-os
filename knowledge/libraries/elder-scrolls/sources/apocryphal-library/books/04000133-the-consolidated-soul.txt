As for the Dwarfs of Red Mountain, it can be said that they performed a great ritual to make their entire selves into one larger self using a kind of necromancy lost to the ages. For in attempting to awaken themselves as gods, they gave of their entire corporeal selves in order to awaken a larger self, the Numidium. 

There is nothing in their notes to speak of their experiments, save mostly for warnings against the very state of union they sought to achieve; consequently, attempts to match their intellect have mostly drawn upon incomplete sources. However, the shape of possibility is beginning to mount; there is no need to waste an entire species on the formation of a new god, nor is that particularly desirable an outcome. Rather, on a smaller scale, it must be considered possible to consolidate a sizeable amount of deceased but quickened flesh into a larger, and animate, being with a greater possession of soul residue - and perhaps therefore sentience and reason - than is possessed even by a living human.

Thus with the inherited wisdom of the Dwarfs we may begin to create beings of indeterminable prowess, against whom it will be impossible to argue the virtue of the mortal sciences. How will the priesthoods of the Wheel be able proclaim the value of the thrice-blessing of the dead, when the dead can speak for themselves, and with far greater eloquence? And will they be able to argue against the truth they have fought for so long; that the mortal souls are merely foodstuff for the star-gods, and that necromancy is the one salvations mortals have against immortal depredations?
