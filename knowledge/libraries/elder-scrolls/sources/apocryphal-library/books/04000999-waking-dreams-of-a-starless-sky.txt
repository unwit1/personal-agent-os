<b>Waking dreams of a Starless Sky</b>

by Bilus Felcrex

The rest is vulgar fiction, attempts to impose order on the consensus mantlings of an uncaring godhead. First, the scholars ruminate on patterns; the next, they significate numbers. All the while, they maintain an old illusion by copying it into a million books that none shall ever read.

Hypnagogia, the threshold of sleep. The simple truth ignored by everyone is that none who sleep all night, dream all night.

A thousand and eight scholars have asked what might happen when the dreamer awakens. I put the question to you; would it be better or worse than when it falls asleep? For that is where we are. Our lives and passions are the attempts to keep the dream from ending. Is it any wonder then that all true power is derived from conflict? From things that keep the pages of history from ever being truly ink-dry? 

Imagine the night sky without a single star. The dream is over, and sleep has come. The children of the architect have shone their last, and darkness falls on the Aurbis. What now for the troubles tyrants, hollow viceroys and forgotten Sharmats whose very ambition was to be the one to dream the dream itself?

At the edge of thought is the starless sky. A darkness before time, to be feared but relished, for it is the wellspring of all tears. Cast your thoughts into the endless night and draw out new ones, because the boundless country at the edge of knowledge is the tide of a sea of ink. The amnesia of creation is the end of all things and a nightly occurrence. 

The lie of the Sharmats is the fear of self-annihilation.

The lie of Royalty is that the dream might no longer need the Dreamer.

The truth is that the knowledge of one dream exists in the next.

You may one day find youself in a place where the walls and columns are histories, and you walk through a library of knowledge kept secret by one who slept while the Dreamer slept. The Library Unending and the Starless Sky are both far older than the dream, perhaps both as old as the Dreamer. Do not be alarmed at the contents of the books you should find. Do not be woe-stricken when the stars will not guide you home.

Some tales are told again and again. Consider a bookmark, but when you open to that page, the story is nearly the same but the characters have changed. Or maybe the characters are the same, but the story is different entirely. Reading it, you remember the last time all of this came together. 

Consider the dreams that come with morning; how much more vivid they are than those on the cusp of sleep. A thousand years of non-existence is the same as one minute's worth. Do not falter at the edge of creation, be ready to hurl your knowledge across it into the world beyond. His library awaits, and nothing will be forgotten.
