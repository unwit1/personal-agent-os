The Battle of Glenumbra Moors

<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>he backdrop for possibly the most famed battle in Tamrielic history is more well-attested than the details of the battle itself. In 1E 478, King Rislav the Righteous defeated Emperor Gorieus at the Siege of Skingrad. This showed promise that other regions might be able to throw off the yoke of Alessian doctrine, the stringencies of which were a source of great chagrin.

Heartened by this triumph, the Direnni clan of High Rock also claimed independence. The Alessians launched several raids against them, all of which were beaten back by Aiden Direnni. 

The High King of Skyrim, Hoag Merkiller, detested the Alessians due to their murder of his predecessor, Kjoric the White. Such was his hatred that he was prepared to ally with the Elves against the Alessians.

Still sore from his defeat, the emperor vowed to press into High Rock, and in 1E 482 the Alessians drew up their battle lines against the Direnni at Glenumbra Moors.

On the Imperial side, the commanders were Emperor Gorieus himself, this time confident that his superior numbers would not be disadvantaged by the enemy. The Arch-Prelate of the Alessian Order, Arexia Calut, had arrayed her battlepriests to the left flank of Gorieus' legions. 
The legions were mostly battle-hardened veterans, though there were many novice troops as well. The battle-priests of Arexia were similarly bolstered by zealots, whose battle-fervour was strong but mostly untested, though she counted several units of skilled battle-knights under her command.
To the right of the unmounted legion, there was a rank of heavy-armoured cataphracts and two ranks of light cavalry.

Facing them were the main ranks of the Direnni. These were under the command of Aiden Direnni, and were largely skilled Elven warriors backed up with ranks of Bretic and Reachman archers and footsoldiers.
To Aiden's left flank were the Nord warriors of Hoag Merkiller, mostly battle-ready and skilled shield-fighters, berserkers and cleverfolk.
To his right flank there was a fortified camp, in which the enchantress Raven Direnni waited. Also there was the Last King of the Ayleids, King Laloriaran Dynar of Nenalata, who was acting in a tactical capacity.

Overall, the Alessians outnumbered the Direnni forces two-to-one. 

The battle began with a charge by the Alessian cavalry. King Dynar had correctly surmised that the horses would be directed to hit the weaker-armoured Bretic footsoldiers, and so he had carefully maneouvred them behind well-concealed marshy ground. This slowed the cavalry advance considerably, allowing the Bretons and Reachfolk, armed mostly with polearms and bows, to close in and wipe out the cavalry in the first blow of the battle.

Not to be perturbed, the Alessians marched forward. The battle-priests hurled magickal fire at the Direnni lines, causing chaos and confusion. Raven Direnni called up a mist from the marshy ground, spreading in front of the battle-priests, and disrupting their aim - this helped the Direnni advance forwards. 

At this point, Hoag Merkiller's Nords charged. The left flank of the legion had been exposed when the cavalry had charged, and the Nords exploited this by running into the side. The left flank buckled momentarily, and battle was joined. 

The legion met the Direnni head-on at this moment, and although the legion was fighting on two sides, they were skilled warriors and veterans. However, they found it hard to break the shields of the elven warriors - most had been enchanted before the battle, a technique known only to Raven Direnni at this time. 

At the pallisaded fortress, King Dynar was concerned that, with enough time, the Imperial forces would chew through the Direnni soldiers. He suggested to Aiden that the Arch-Prelate be slain, one way or another. Aiden agreed, and sent a detachment to challenge the zealots.

The fervent warriors of the Alessians were not well armoured, but they did not shy back from the fight, and would not flee. Many soldiers were lost to their flails and their staves, until an opening was made.

Meanwhile across the battlefield, King Hoag had engaged Emperor Gorieus. He slew seven of the Emperor's personal guard in order to reach the man, who flew at him in a rage. The two were locked in bloody combat until the rest of the personal guard, concerned that Hoag would win, rushed in and cut him down. This enraged the Nords, but it caused the Direnni line to waver for a moment - a great King had just been cut down.

Fortunately on the other side of the battle, a warrior, clad in the shining armour of a holy knight, bore down upon Arexia. Her spells of holy godsfire had frightened off many of her assailants, but the knight charged in and, after a short fight, skewered her with the sword Chrysamere. This deadened the Alessian charge, and they panicked. A charge ordered by Aiden caught them in the rear, and also surrounded the Alessian legions.

The battle was no longer up in the air - it could only result in an Alessian defeat. Gorieus sounded the retreat, and he was one of the few who made it out of High Rock and back to Cyrodiil.

The aftermath of the battle is not well recorded, but it soon became clear that the Direnni had lost a lot of soldiers of their own. The Nords had fared little better. All were regarded as heroes, but it would not be long before the Direnni Hegemony, like the stringent Alessian Order, ebbed away.
