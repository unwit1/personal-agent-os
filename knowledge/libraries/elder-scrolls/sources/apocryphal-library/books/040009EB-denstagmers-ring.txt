Volume IX:
Denstagmer's Ring

by Aurileon of Sunhold

<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/D_letter.png'>enstagmer's Ring is a beautiful relic of an unspecified time. A ruby cut in the Direnni Rectangular style, it affords the wearer powerful protection against the elements.

The rectangular cut is very distinctive of the First Era Direnni and Telvanni styles, but has the slightly offset gemstone associated with the former. The crafstmanship is almost identical to the amethyst Mentor's Ring, which was supposedly made by Archmage Carni Asron, an elf of Direnni heritage, in order to help aid his students. However, Archmage Asron was mostly known for his enchantment prowess - having studied under the legendary Raven Direnni herself - so it is more likely that it was the enchantment he performed rather than the crafting of the item.

The enchantment on Denstagmer's Ring is powerful - certainly consistent with the early work of the Direnni and their students. However, of the name 'Denstagmer' we know nothing. It is not a name associated with any individuals. Some have insisted it is a Nordic name, others have claimed it is Bosmeri.

As such, the myth behind the creation of this powerful and oft-occurring item are mostly unknown. Was Denstagmer the crafter or the enchanter? Was Denstagmer even a person, or a place, or something else? One scholar, Hilde of Neugrad, who has wandered much of Skyrim suggested a theory.

She noted that the '-mer' suffix could refer to the Aldmeris word meaning 'folk' or more commonly 'elves'. What, then, would the 'Denstag-' translate to?

She posited that it may be a mis-hearing of 'Kjenstag-Mer', or the folk or elves of Kjenstag, a first-era holding in Skyrim that his long since been reduced to a roofless ruin. Most of the elves in Skyrim had been wiped out by the time of the first era, so this seemed unlikely, but she did relate a tale of a ghost that sallies out from the ruins every night in search of its stolen treasure.

A rousing tale, but not very compelling as evidence goes. Could there be more to it? She admitted that at this point, the trail runs as cold as the ground around Kjenstag.

Though the mystery of its creation is yet unsolved, its use has been well documented. Its first recorded use was at the Blade-Surrender of Pale Pass, adorning the finger of Reman himself. Since then it has supposedly been used in a number of conflicts including the Sacking of Betony, the Fall of Anequina, the Battle of Hunding Bay, and finally the recent War of the Red Diamond.
