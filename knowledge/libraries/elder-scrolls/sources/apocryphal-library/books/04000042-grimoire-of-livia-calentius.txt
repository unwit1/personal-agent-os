To truly call forth a power from beyond the wheel of the Enemy, the necromancer must be willing to deal with the very matters of the soul itself.

Part I: On the Invocation of the Soul

First, upon the ground one must draw the triangle. This represents the mirror between the worlds of the living and the dead, and also the mortal eye that observes the transmission of the soul from one to the other. Around this must be drawn the pentacle, whose points refer to the schools of magick as they are understood within the current mythic: Destruction to render the veil thin, Illusion to coax back the shadow of the deceased, Alteration to so infuse the mortal remains, Conjuration to draw forth the soul from the hereafter, and Restoration to repair the veil.
Onto this symbol the carcass of the deceased is placed, and the binding circle is drawn around so that the spirit may remain in its confines.

Second, the lighting of the candles at the points of the pentacle.

Third, the invocation must begin. 
AE NAGAIA GLAMORIL, WEND CECEYE MORAGA CE ADA, AURAN CE ANYAMIS ABISIA.
Thus will the soul be bound to the body.

Fourth, the body must be painted with the symbol OHT. The paint must be made from blood and gravedust mixed together in a brass vessel.

Fifth, the final invocation:
AE NAGAIA GLAMORIL, CE ABAVARLOR SILYA BISIA ANYAMMIS.

Thus will the body be awoken in a facsimile of life. This is life of the second sight; one step beyond the mere awakening of zombies, yet far from the true prize that is the dauntless resurrection. Those necromancers who have learned this secret keep it close to their hearts.
