A Children's Tale from Morrowind

Edited by Gorieus Glumm

<p align="left">
Editor's Note:

The very earliest versions of this story appear to be Dunmeri, though the most recent tellings feature an Imperial protagonist. It is believed by some that the story is indeed true, and that such happenings have befallen the people of Ebonheart since before the Armistice.

G.G
[pagebreak]
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>his is a tale of Old Ebonheart, which sits on the coast of Mainland Morrowind, overlooking the Bay of Vivec. 

Plovia Arden was a young Imperial girl who lived there with her family. She would sweep the houses of rich families in return for a little silver, and sometimes she would help her mother sell cheese at the market.

One day she was sweeping the house of a Dark Elf family. She knew them quite well, and was friends with a girl her age from the house called Medretha. On that day, when she was taking a break from her chores, she and Medretha swapped old ghost tales they knew.

Plovia had been telling Medretha the tale of the Crossroad-Queen of Nibenay who liked to drown inattentive wanderers, and when she had finished, Medretha told Plovia a tale that her family knew - that of a stange old witch.

The tale went like this: Many years ago, before the Septims signed the Armistice with Lord Vivec, a strange red star was seen in the sky above Morrowind. The local Dark Elves went to talk to a local witch, who knew much of the stars. 
When they spoke to her, she told them she had many dreams concerning the star. "It will return Morrowind to the way it used to be!", she said.

Five years later, the red star shone above Morrowind again, its thin light resting on the summit of the mountain in the middle of Vvardenfell Island. Again the people went to consult with the old witch.
But when the old Dark Elf witch turned to face them, they saw that she had no eyes. Many ran away, but those who remained heard her words; "The Red Star will reveal the lies of Lord Vivec."

Seven years passed; the red star shone again. When the few townsfolk consulted the witch, they saw that her face was hollow - just a grinning mouth above which there was an empty cavity. All but three fled, and those three heard her say; "The Red Star tells us that the Great Houses will betray Morrowind to a great enemy."

Thirty years passed, and the town of Old Ebonheart was now under Imperial governance. One night, though, the red star shone over Vvardenfell, and those who remembered - and a few of the Imperial citizens who didn't remember - went to see the old witch, despite the warnings of others.
When they encountered her, she indeed had no eyes or face above her mouth, and to make things worse, a terrible tentacle snaked its way out from the cavity, as it trying to smell them out. All fled from the old witch, but not before they heard her cry "It will drive out the Empire from Morrowind!"

Plovia had enjoyed the tale, and Medretha told her that it had been seventy years since the last visiting of the witch.

Five nights later, Plovia and Medretha were sitting up together at night, when they saw a red star in the sky across the Bay of Vivec. They were shocked - could the stories have been true? They asked among the adults, but most had not heard of the tale and those older Dunmer who had, refused to talk about it.

But curiosity got the better of them, so they went to the old cave where the witch had supposedly lived. It was dark and grimy, with strange mushrooms growing all around. Strange illuminated pools lit the cave, and they fancied they could hear the heavy breathing of others. 

At some length they came to a cavern lit with red candles, where stood a hunched-over figure, facing away from them.

"Why is there a red star over Vvardenfell, old witch?" asked Medretha.

The old woman turned to face her, her head no longer anything like a Dunmer's. It was a mass of tentacles, pulsing with evil intent. She blew a strange, eldritch piping sound from them, which formed words in the air.

"You already know, child!"

They both screamed and ran, and dodged between the shambling, twisted figures that now appeared between themselves and the cave's entrance. They did not stop running until they reached Old Ebonheart, where they told the city guards what they had seen.

They had not expected to be taken seriously, but on hearing the description of the witch, the guards looked troubled.

The following day, a boat from Vivec arrived. A group of strangely-armoured Dark Elves disembarked, their cuirasses made from the chitin of large insects. They were followed by a group of Dark Elf knights, their armour gold with crested helmets. They went to the cave, and twelve hours later, they returned, telling the townsfolk that the witch was no more.

A year passed, and then one night above Vvardenfell there glowed the red star.

Plovia went to see Medretha, and found that her friend had not slept well.

"I saw the star in my dreams last night, Plovia. It told me that it would make Morrowind how it used to be... and that it would drive the Empire from the land."
