The Aurbis Viewed Sideways: CHIM, the Tower, and the Zero-Sum

<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/ApocLib/ouroboros.png' height='240' width='240'>
</p>
<p align="left">
by Japhetas Salker
[pagebreak]

Every so often, a talented thinker of philosopher comes upon the notion of The Tower, and with it the idea of CHIM. While the Aurbis viewed on its side would indeed appear similar to a tower, the notion that being able to perceive it as such would somehow imbue that philosopher with grand enlightenment is almost certainly wooly thinking.

Given that reality forms a wheel, it would - as stated - form a linear shape on turning it sideways. This has been referred to as The Tower - a concept of absolute totality, often thought of with regards to 'The Godhead', a being of pure myth that supposedly created or - in stranger versions, is dreaming - the whole of existence. To bear witness to this tower would be to comprehend everything at once, which leads to the question which the early philosophers posited - if you are looking at everything, and you are part of everything, then what becomes of the observer?

This thought-experiment led these thinkers to suggest that there are two possible results of this happening. The first is that the observer, while understanding their place in the universe, is nevertheless able to retain their individuality. This state is known as CHIM, the Aldmeri word for Royalty. It is a concept written of frequently by the warrior-poet of the Chimer-Dunmer, Vivec, and also by the architect of the Oblivion Crisis, Mankar Camoran. 

By accessing this state, they suppose that the observer can gain incredible power, in that by witnessing that they are part of a falsehood - a dream - that they can dream other things into being as well. Vivec claimed to have attained this state, and Mankar Camoran claimed that Tiber Septim had managed the same. 

It should be pointed out that both individuals were renowned propagandists, as was Tiber Septim. Vivec was revealed as a liar around the time of his disappearance, and it was discovered that not only was much of the literature of the Tribunal Temple based on his lies, but that he was actually gaining his power from the Heart of Sheor which had been under Red Mountain. His disappearance after the Nerevarine found and disabled the Heart is no coincidence. 

Mankar Camoran was a megalomaniac who tried - and briefly succeeded - to summon Mehrunes Dagon into Mundus, an act that could have destroyed all reality were it not for the sacrifice of Martin Septim. Martin's distant forbear Tiber Septim, whom Camoran claims attained the state of CHIM, had his own abilities and powers that did not require the reaching of a divine state. Namely, a huge army and the Numidium.

Nevertheless, the idea of CHIM persists. It has even been stated that Sheor himself tried to achieve this, and failed deliberately so that all his 'creation' would know how to walk the path. The supposed consequence of failing to attain the state is sometimes called Zero-Sum.

In Zero-Sum, the observer cannot differentiate themselves from the Aurbis. They are unable to conceive of themselves as separate from everything while they remain in it, so they effectively delete themselves from existence and become part of that everything, no longer possessing their AE, their individuality.

There is no real evidence of this occurring either. Scattered reports of vanishing monks or mages have been recorded by supposedly well-respected institutions, but these are most likely the results of arcane malpratice or death at the hands of jealous colleagues. It is thought by some mages to be the fate of the Dwemer, but their fate can usually be explained by more prosaic means, such as their being wiped out at the Battle of Red Mountain, or their immortality fading away once the source of their power was destroyed.

And if this is indeed what happened to Sheor, then ask yourself; how is it that his heart continued to beat under Red Mountain after he had removed himself from existence? How did his blood become ebony if he had erased himself? This detail is omitted by those who attempt to describe the 'Psijiic Endeavour', that philosophy of the Chimer that attempts to re-create Sheor's effort at super-divinity. 

As with many things, it does the scholar well to be sceptical of claims made by powerful persons. Such tyrants have a vested interest in making themselves appear divinely immortal. The wise scholar puts their faith in Julianos and does not put their trust in the care of those who would misuse it.
