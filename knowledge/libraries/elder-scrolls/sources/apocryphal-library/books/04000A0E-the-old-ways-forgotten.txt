Ask yourself, mages who excelled under the simple system of extreme complexity - do you remember the School of Mysticism at all?

You all know how to trap a soul, this is a given. And now you call yourselves conjurors, and perhaps you are right. Yet what is it that is conjured? 

Those who took the might of others with a whisper, showing them the cosmic balance can be changed with the removal of a feather from the scales. Are you truly Destruction mages now? Was it that you were always so?

Those who close their eyes and see with the second sight; are you now illusionists? Were you always so? Many of you were. But what you see is real, even if the means are not.

Those who raise the veil and see with the Seventh Sight - are you now Alterationists? I dare say you were. Many mages are. But what have you changed? Not your eyes.

But do you remember when all these spells were of one discipline, the school unspoken, the house untutored, where wisdom is learned in the mirror of final misunderstanding?

Mystics were once those who hunted the ash-wastes under the guidance of the Ghost Snake. They were the Clever-folk of Jhunal who could read the tides on the shells of ancient crabs. They were the painted witches of Bjoulsae whose drum-beat could steal the thirst-wisdom of angry men. They were the Moth-singers of Nibenay with a thousand gods unspoken. They were the shamans who left the Xanmeers of Argonia to sink, and the Moon-seers of the dunes whose wisdom is sweeter than wine. They were the crystal-tenders of divine logic among the gardens innumerable, the story-spinners who knew the real reason that flesh becomes literature, and the Forgemaidens who wrought truth from the broken armour long-tarnished. They were the shapers of the first sung sword, the guardians of the Three Sacred Lies of Resdayn, the dream-chafers of the black passage, and more things than can be named even by the word-thirsty.

What will it take for this to be remembered? Do mages weigh their worth by the heft of their spell-tome now? 

Perhaps when Artaeum returns, the Old Ways will be remembered.
