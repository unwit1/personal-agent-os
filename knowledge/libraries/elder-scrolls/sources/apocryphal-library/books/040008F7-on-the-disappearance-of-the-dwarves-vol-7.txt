<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/F_letter.png'>oreword by the Editor

Indara Sul of Blacklight was gracious enough to provide this essay when the call went out, and as such she is one of the contributors who remains alive to answer questions on her work.

Her work focuses on Akulakhan, and uses it as a means to add weight to the Luagar Anulam's 'Final Report to Trebonius'. Her account takes some liberties with the notion of reality as a dream, providing them as fact in the process of her conclusion. She also claims a certain amount of knowledge on the Psijiic Endeavour which aren't necessarily agreen upon. Nevertheless, the parallels she draws between Luagar's work and current knowledge about Akulakhan make her work a very interesting addition.

- Maura Calvaine

[pagebreak]
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>he Final Report to Trebonius, written in the late Third Era, concludes that the Dwemer's efforts in building the Numidium were focused on a simple goal - becoming, as one, the brass skin of their new god. This would allow them to return, the author claims, to their pre-Aurbic god-concept. 

The work has attracted acclaim and criticism in various measures, so in writing this essay I intend to demonstrate my support for the concept. Whether or not the Dwemer was successful in their endeavour is unknowable, but I believe that Luagar's work ably outlines the premise that the Dwemer believed would work. And I intend to support that byt demonstrating that the second Numidium, wrought by the Sharmat in the closing years of the Third Era, was built to the same principles - if not the same design.

The Dwemer were trying to reach Godhood - and we know that Dagoth Ur was trying to do the same. The Tribunal used the Heart of Lorkhan for the same reason, though in a different manner. Godhood is an irresistable concept to those who locate a source of great power.

While the Dwemer intended to become Brass, the intended skin of Numidium, the Sharmat intended another substance. He understood the principles of the Brass Tower, but not its construction. So, in building Akulakhan, he devised a replacement solution. He would have his followers add their flesh manually to the skeleton of Akulakhan.

He used the heart to develop the Corprus disease. It served two purposes; one was to weaken those not of his House. The other was to strengthen those who were of his house, and make them functionally immortal. Their flesh could be removed in bucket loads and always grow back due to the nature of the 'Divine Disease'.

The substance of Akulakhan was described as grey, the same colour as the Corprus flesh that was found in so many of the Sixth House shrines during the Ordinator's purges in the years surrounding. Why else would these cultists remove so much of themselves? Some have suggested it was to spread the Blight and Corprus around the rest of Tamriel, but this also seems to have been the purpose of the strange statuettes that were smuggled out of the province around that time. No evidence was found of Corprus flesh being smuggled anywhere but Red Mountain, through the hidden networks at Kogoruhn, ancient bastion of House Dagoth.

Had he succeeded, Dagoth Ur would have made a Titan of Flesh, which would have exalted him and his House above all others. Powered by the Heart of Lorkhan, it would have been his instrument of conquest, allowing him to fulfil his ambition - to Awaken as the New Dreamer. Rather than the Psijiic endeavour which seeks to coax us into awakening new realities, the Sharmat sought to take this one for himself.

Such a task achieved, he could then rule our reality, or worse - awoken, and ended the dream for good.
