The Athedorix Conundrums


by Mymphonus

</p>
[pagebreak]
<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>he great philosopher Athedorix, dutiful servant of Sheogorath and long-time inmate of the Glenpoint Asylum, once posed these questions to an audience he had been granted, and then gave them the answers.

The First Question:
"Why, my friends, did the Elves and the Atmorans go to war? What caused the Night of Tears, and why did the Atmorans never relent in their punishment?"

"In fact, they never had any intention of harming one another. But the cultures were so different that what was a handshake by one was a slaughter to the other. The Atmorans tried to show forgiveness when they returned with Ysgramor, but the same thing happened. Elves and Men are too different to exist beside one another. A peace may exist for a time, but in the end, the differences will tear them both apart."

The Second Question:
"To where did the Dwarves vanish?"

"The Dwarves like to use sound instead of magic. When the Red Mountain erupted, it turned them all into sounds. When you hear an echo, that is a Dwarf. When you hear a sound that had no cause, that was a Dwarf. When you hear the sound of someone weeping though cannot see them, that my friends is a Dwarf."

The Third Question: 
"What really happens in a Dragon Break?"

"Absolute free will. When the dragon break happens, you can make choices and they all happen. When the dragon break ends, only one thing happens when you make a choice, which means there wasn't a choice. Your destiny is controlled by Akatosh, and nothing you can do will change that."

The Fourth Question:
"How can one achieve eternal life?"

"Simple, one simply just dies. This causes one to be reborn eventually. Your memories cease to be, so you're barely yourself, just a regurgitated husk of soul-matter with no sense of identity. Again. You have done this a hundred thousand times already and you will never stop."

The Fifth Question:
"The legends said that dragons were immortal, and yet they are all dead now. What happened?"

"The dragons are still alive, but they could only eat a certain race of men, and they are extinct now. So they starved. The skeletons you see are in fact emaciated dragons whose muscles have atrophied into non-existence, and their wide-open maws are in fact eternal screams of agony."

The Sixth Question:
"How can we return to Aldmeris, to Old Ehlnofey?"

"Aldmeris was a real place, but it is lost to time. It is the opposite of what happened in Atmora, which is still a real place but time has all stopped in the ice. A time without place and a place without time. You know that they used to be one and the same? Split in two, like the mind of a mad god."

The Seventh Question:
"What is the true nature of Sheogorath? Why is it that he is the Lord of Pandemonium but he looks like a senior member of the Wayrest Civil Service?"

"His true appearance is as varied and winding as the tunnels under his realm. The great sprawling roots are the real Sheogorath, and they represent both action and stasis - neither are sane, neither are conscious, just two primal forces lashing out against each other. Where they meet, the Sheogorath that we know and witness occurs. I don't know where he gets his outfits."

The Eighth Question:
"Aldmeris and Atmora, action and stasis? So many forces in conflict! Is all of creation formed by such things?"

"Oh yes, and literally everything in creation. When you stand up, the up briefly conquers the down until you sit down again. Everything from the great schisms of heaven to the blood pumping in your veins is the balance of two forces. Neither of them are sane or conscious either. The thing you experience as the waking world is just the scream that reality makes when it can't face the fact it doesn't exist."

The Ninth Question:
"If none of this is real, what's the point in continuing to live?"

"Once you've tricked yourself into existing, the next part is to trick yourself into having a reason to continue. And whatever you do, don't listen to any of these questions, because you might just remember you don't exist. Then, you'll have to concentrate really hard on existing, which might just make you a god until it doesn't."

The nine questions were a roaring success at the institute, and several inmates and staff members evaporated into non-materia. At least one has suspended himself in a slow time-loop to avoid this, but the majority just laughed and clapped, because they were the ones too sane or too far gone to understand the conundrums.
