<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>he Soul Cairn is not well understood. It is recognisably a realm of Oblivion, but seems to have little that is identifiably Daedric.

It is the realm of the Ideal Masters, who are untrustworthy creatures and should not be bargained with except under the limited purview of the Mages Guild code. It is a place of death, a place where necromancers will find much to use.

Chapter One - Reaching the Soul Cairn

Attempts to Scry into the Soul Cairn are currently suspended following the fate of Magister Tullio. It is deemed possible but inadvisable.

Opening a portal to the Soul Cairn is possible. It involves the admixture of ground soul-gem fragments and treated bone-dust, with purified void salts poured into a mirror-sheet of unsanctified stonework. The sigils required should be left overnight (the longer the better, in fact).

Once the portal is opened, it is inadvisable to enter immediately unless the Mananaut is one of our undead scouts. If not, then a minor pact must be made with the Ideal Masters in which a fragment of the users soul is offered in return for nothing. Thus ONLY those mages who are extremely confident in finding this bit of soul should enter, unless they want to live the rest of their lives incomplete. It is possible to do so, we note, with only minor side effects, but it is not a pleasant experience.

Once part of ones soul is in the realm, it is possible to enter without being soul-drained by the Ideal Masters immediately.

Chapter Two - Navigating the Soul Cairn

The realm looks like a huge, never-ending graveyard. Stones jut up from the ashen soil that seem like gravestones but bear no name. Mausoleums and strange black towers dot the landscape in all directions, with grand black cathedrals in the distance. The sky is a sinister twilight purple.

Strange fungus-like husks sprout from the ground everywhere. These seem to have properties that are valuable, which will be discussed later, so it is worth picking these.

There are rents in the ground through which howling, moaning soul-stuff emerges. This creatia can be gathered in soul gems.

The ground is as sturdy as the ground of Tamriel, but the melancholic sensation is pervasive. It is good to bring things that will inspire yourself, including a stiff drink if necessary. 

The strange edifices can be entered. There are usually no doors, but there are sometimes cage-gates that must be activated somehow. Much transport takes the form of strange violet-flame platforms which teleport the wanderer around. Sometimes these lead up to floating islands upon which sit strange edifices. 

Ghosts are everywhere. They are those who have been ensnared by soul magic, trapped in black soul gems, and stuck in the realm once traded with the Ideal Masters. They can be spoken with, though many have forgotten much of what they did in life.

The dangerous inhabitants of these realms are the undead. The bonemen, mistmen and wrathmen are black skeletal warriors and archers far stronger than simple reanimated skeletons found in old tombs. More powerful 'Keepers' have also been reported, great bone-armoured titans. They should be circumvented if possible.

The Ideal Masters themselves seem to be strange purple crystals - possibly they are soul gems containing the real souls of the Masters. Nobody is certain, but many necromancers who have attempted to make deals with these beings have ended up in the soul cairn for good. These crystals, when approached, will drain the life force of the Mananaut very quickly. However, consumption of the soul husks, or drinking an extract made from them, interrupts this effect, allowing one to grab the treasures near to these crystals quickly.

End of chapter notes:

None know how the Cairn came to be. Given that the souls here are gathered by black magic, it may be that the realm was empty before Mannimarco discovered the secret of Black Soul Gems. It may be that Mannimarco created the realm. But some believe that the unusual pact-nature of the Ideal Masters links them to Clavicus Vile. Could this realm be part of some dark pact? Or do those strange crystals simply contain so many souls that they have become sentient, and all-powerful? Or is this land the realm of Molag Bal, who imprisons mortals for his own wretched amusement?

Chapter Three: The Wealth of the Soul Cairn

The amount of soul magic that can be harvested from this realm is colossal. If one can avoid being trapped by the Ideal Masters, then one can gain huge quantities of magickal wealth. It may even be sufficient to power the ancient moth-fortresses of our orders' antecedent.

There is also substantial magickal knowledge to be gained. The undead that are encountered here can be summoned, and the ghosts who dwell the plains are also able to be communicated with. Thus we may learn of things that happened long ago, especially from mages who were incautious and may appreciate assistance.

Chapter Four: Returning from the Soul Cairn

The portals used to reach the Soul Cairn tend to be stable. Simply retrace ones steps. Once home, be sure to seal the portal closed again, as they can leak necromantic energy into the edifice in which they are constructed.
