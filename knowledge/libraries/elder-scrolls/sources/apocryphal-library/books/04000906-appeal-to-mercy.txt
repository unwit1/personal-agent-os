<p align="center">

An Appeal to Mercy

for the wise Children of Ysgramor

by Prince Nalchare

</p>
</font>
[pagebreak]
<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>o the wise, the mighty Kings of the noble Atmoran people,

We acknowledge our crimes at Sarth-El. We brought to innocent people a doom which was undeserved, and which was rightfully punished by your shield-bearers, your hunters, your clever-men and your wise women.

Those of us who were present at the attack have all since been lain to rest, their bones with the Ehlnofey. They were slain in battle against your finest warriors, and we stood no chance. There is only one left now who was present, and that is myself.

I will not insult the memory of your fallen by attempting to excuse or even explain our actions on that night. We have spoken to our gods, who have chided us for our greed and for our treachery. They have offered us no respite from your attacks, and nor should they.

But this war has gone on for a long time now, and though we live a long time, I am the only one alive who remembers that night. Your ancestors who wrought our doom have lain their heads in clay and ice now.

We come to you and ask for mercy; not because I deserve it, or my fellows deserved it, but because our new generation does not. We have been punished many times over now; we beg you, stay your hand!

If it would cease your punishment, I would renounce my crown and receive my judgement at your hand, the last survivor of Sarth-El, whose complicity was as great as any. I would accept any punishment you might place on me, as long as it was on me alone.

Under the Bright Sun, Auri-El, I make this pledge.
In solemn prayer to Stendarr, I beg mercy.

Yours in everlasting peace,

Prince Nalchare
