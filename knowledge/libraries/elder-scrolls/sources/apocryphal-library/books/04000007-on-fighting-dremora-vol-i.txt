Martial Techniques: On Fighting Dremora
Volume I

by Haspian Witheric

<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>he legions of Kyn as they call themselves are better known to most as Dremora. They are an ancient race of Daedra often associated with the Prince of Destruction, Mehrunes Dagon, though they claim they were not always thus bound, and indeed many serve other masters.

They are extremely able fighters. Even the least among their kind, the Churls, are a potent threat as was demonstrated at the closing of the Third Era. They count among their number mages and archers, but it is their melee fighters, the dark knights of the Deadlands, the Kynvals and their orders and their betters who require the focus of this book.

The arms and armour of Kynval range from relatively crude to extremely dangerous. The most powerful of their weaponry is often simply known as 'Daedric', but is formed of ebony enfused with the souls of other Daedra. Daedric weapons are unmistakable, with black barbed blades glistening with a blood-red glow. Against such terrible weapons, something to match it is in order. 

Almost no weapons or armour can surpass that of the most elite Dremora warriors such as the Valkynaz, but weapons of glass or ebony will do something to even the field of battle. Armour that can withstand such blades is similarly hard to encounter, but again, Ebony and Glass will allow the wearer to abide a couple more blows than the next best alternative.

Assuming that one is equipped sufficiently to take on a Kynval knight, the next matter is matching their combat style. The school of war to which the Kyn adhere to does not have a name known by mortals, but it is characterised by very precise and well-disciplined movements. When armed with a shield, they will only drop their block-stance to strike. When armed with a two-handed weapon, they will guard themselves in the weapon's arc.

The arcs in which they can swing are very wide, so to combat them one must be ready to deflect the blow upwards (being cautious to keep one's head back, and helmed). On the return swing, the blow can usually be deflected downwards. Keeping a narrow angle for the shield or blade being used to block will reduce the impact on the armour, and lower the damage caused by a fully-tempered Daedric blade.

In the next volume, we will discuss the best methods of counter-attack.
