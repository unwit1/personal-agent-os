Father of Dragons

On the Nature of Akatosh and his Children

by Siric Taverner
[pagebreak]
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/M_letter.png'>uch has been written on the divine nature of Akatosh, but here we must focus on his physickal form. He appears as a dragon, and all dragons that have existed claim to be his descendents, even his very children. 

The Father of Dragons has appeared to mankind on very few occasions, but when he does so he is in the form of a dragon of pure gold. It is speculated that this colouration may be due to his association with Auri-El and the sun, or may simply be a demonstration by the god of his most high status.

Always his form is the same, with two legs at the rear and two wings. This shape is shared by all known dragons past and present, and the only morphologically draconic being to differ is the Daedric prince Peryite, who has a total of four legs and two wings.

And so the notion that Akatosh is the father of these dragons would appear to be truth. So why is it that revered AKA is gentle to all his creation, and his children are not?

Few answers satisfy. One scholar from Skyrim claims that Alduin, the Nord-mythic form of Akatosh that is painted as a destroyer, is in fact a lesser gradient of the divine that is more concerned with earthly matters, and that it is he who has convinced the other dragons to take on this destructive mantle. 

Another theory is that the dragons are in fact pieces of Akatosh's whole, and can only return to their whole-form by devouring and dominating creation. When all creation has been devoured, Akatosh will be whole and new once again, and will be able to self-annihilate to form the next kalpa. 

Whatever the case, it remains the fact that dragons are an existing threat, and the true soul of Akatosh is here to guide us through these struggles.
