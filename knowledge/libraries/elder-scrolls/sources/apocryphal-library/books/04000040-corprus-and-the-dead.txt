Report to the An-Xileel:
Corprus and the Dead

by Gild-Bone

</p>
[pagebreak]
<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>he recent trouble in Morrowind gave rise to endless speculation as to what role the Nerevarine was to play in the grand game of fate. But the crux of the matter is this: The Sharmat, Dagoth Ur, had been unleashing a terrible sickness onto Morrowind for generations. This sickness, called Corprus, was a special form of blight that tapped directly into one of the oldest objects in creation: The Heart of Lorkhan.

Using this ancient organ, Dagoth Ur created a sickness that would rot the mind and body of any who became infected. It would, however, make them impervious to age and resilient to much damage. Thus the afflicted would become like unto zombies, though never ageing, and not undead.

The most profound effects were on his own disciples, and those descended from the long-lost House Dagoth, the Sixth House of old Dunmeri tradition. They would undergo a transformation, whereby the eyes would atrophy, followed by the whole frontal eye cavity, until a monstrous appendage would emerge. Later forms were barely recognisable as mer at all.

All forms of the Sixth House afflicted could regrow their flesh very quickly, to the point where they would remove it and have it sent to Red Mountain where Dagoth Ur would use it as the construction material for a giant effigy he was building around the Heart. The reasons he chose to do this are doubtless very steeped in ancient mysticism, and unknown to I.

With their flesh fully regrown the dreamers, ash ghouls and so on could simply remove more, and so on. But what is of interest is the state of being that the afflicted found themselves in. It is a state comparable with various others, such as the Briar Heart, whereby the essence of Lorkhan produced a state that is not quite living and not quite dead. In the case of the Briar Heart, the reachman has his own heart replaced with that of a poisonous roseblossom which makes them extremely resilient but robs them of their mercy and interpersonal logic. 

The question that has puzzled necromancers since is: Can this corprus flesh be used in the pursuit of confounding Arkay's mortal law?

Following the destruction of Red Mountain, Corprus Flesh has become very difficult to acquire. Even before that, most traces had been burned by the Ordinators of the Temple and the remaining disciples of Lord Dagoth similarly put to the torch. However, small fastholds have been discovered from time to time, and enterprising necromancers have been able to get hold of small samples.

The bad news, or possibly good considering, is that Corprus flesh can no longer transmit the disease. The actual miasma seems to have perished alongside the Sharmat himself. But the flesh does seem to maintain some of the properties that cause it to outlast normal flesh. And when rendered down into the correct essential ashes, it can be injected into a dead body which does, for a short time, appear to act as if it was alive. 

So far this has only been successful with the very recently dead. A Breton man who had died not ten days hence was successfully brought back to life. He instantly became angry and began to attack, but it was noted that he was fully capable of speech. Magickal incantations to repel undead did not work on him. Eventually, he tired of his attacks and collapsed, once again quite dead.

With this knowledge, we might consider how the substance of Lorkhan might be used to awaken the dead once more. Perhaps more rational forms can be awakened in the future. When Red Mountain stops spewing ash and smoke, perhaps we can investigate the crater to see what remains, provided the remnants of House Redoran have not regrouped by then.
