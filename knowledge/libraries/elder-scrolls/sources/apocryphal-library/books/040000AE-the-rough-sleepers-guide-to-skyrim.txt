<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/H_letter.png'>ail, hearty traveller, hail and be hale! The year marches on towards another measly winter, and the foolhardy among you are already planning your next good night's sleep on an iceberg, in a bandit cave or, dare I say, in a sabre-cat. No such folly for the wise, though! Read on and learn how to get a good night's sleep anywhere in Skyrim - even in your own spouse's bed! Ha ha!

Your first port of call should ideally be an inn or tavern. There's plenty of these across Skyrim, be they in villages or towns or on lonesome roads. All but the fanciest are usually quite affordable, but stick with me if you're dirt poor- there's ways around it. A room to yourself can be a welcome luxury for the tired traveller - no snoring stranger in the neighbouring bunk, a roof over your head, perhaps even a bowl of warm stew and a mug of dark ale. Be sure to make full use of the fireplace before you turn your boots under the bed too - better to go to bed warm than wake up cold.
But Hanna, you plead, I have not the ten-to-thirty septims needed to grab myself a room for the night! Well, my impecunious friend, fear not - there's usually a few ways to pay for your night's pay.

First, offer to do some work for the landlord. You can usually chop wood for an hour or so, thus sparing the proprietor the time to serve more customers. Consider that room yours! Failing that, it's not uncommon for barmaids and landlords to accept a little barter - those strange leaves you found? Claim they're valuable herbs! By the divines, they even might be.

Weren't able to get the coin? All's not lost - there's always a chair. Landlords rarely kick a sleeping wanderer out of a chair they're sleeping in, but making yourself comfy by lying on a bar-bench will definitely get you tossed out in the snow. Mind you, getting to sleep on a chair is its own challenge.

Don't mind a little cold? Neither do the horses! That pile of hay in the stable just there is good enough for Skyrim's sturdiest steeds, which means it's good enough for you.

Now, chances are you might not be near a tavern or town, so you may have to get creative.

There are a number of isolated homesteads across the land - they are usually owned by crofters, farmers or other hermits. In the first era and earlier, ancient laws dictated that a home owner had to permit a traveller to sleep under their roof if there was nowhere else for them to go. These ancient hospitality laws are mostly forgotten, but doing a favour for a homesteader will often be enough for them to honour this venerable custom. Chopping wood, gathering crops, delivering goods - any of these might be enough to get you a bed for a night, maybe even on return visits.

No houses or taverns in sight? You might still find yourself a roof if you're clever. Skyrim is home to many old ruins, most of them second and third-era fortifications. Only a handful of these are still occupied by Imperial soldiers, meaning the rest are abandoned. Be careful! These forts are usually best avoided, because they are often refuge for all sorts of undesirables - bandits, warlocks and even the undead. Their battlements make good vantage points, so they're likely to be guarded well.

Abandoned watchtowers are sometimes safe - just keep low while approaching if you're not sure.

Better to seek instead the ancient tombs. These are sometimes visible from miles away - ancient angled roofs mostly collapsed. While some are habitat for bandits, most rogues are too clever to risk their demise in these reaches. After all, the walking dead are known to lurk far within.

Oh, fear not! The ancient draugr of Skyrim are usually further within than the first room. The entrance to a tomb is often a surprisingly safe place to lay low for the night.

No abandoned watchtowers or ancient tombs to speak of? Well, it's time to use a tent. I won't bore you with the specifics of camping other than this - make note of where the wind blows, and where a campfire can be seen from. Don't pick a spot where the snow will blow right into your shelter. Light your fire under tree cover so it can't be seen from the road - this can attract all sorts of strife.

No tent either? Well, you might be lucky in a cave. Might. Peek your head in first, and not with a torch lit. If you can be sure you're alone, you might have a safe place to rest. Otherwise, you'll be dealing with wolves, bears, trolls or the dread snow-people of legend.

No cave either? No shelter to speak of, and no tent, and the open road all empty before you?

Well, next time read this book BEFORE you head out. Keep walking and may Kyne watch over you!
