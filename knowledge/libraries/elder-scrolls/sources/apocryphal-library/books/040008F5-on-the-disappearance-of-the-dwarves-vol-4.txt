Our second contributor, the Redguard scholar Zarya, published her work in 3E 410. Its title, despite being a cheeky pun on the Null Hypothesis, bears little similarity to that of Varendelion's work. She postulates that the Dwemer erased themselves from existence using a long-debated process known as the 'Zero Sum'.


- Maura Calvaine

<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>he Tower. So many of our kind do not muse on the Tower, because we always walk the wheel, one way or another. The wheel is like the First Snake, always his teeth around his tail. But straighten out the snake and you get the tower. Or rather - look at him sideways. Do not straighten out the snake. That's one of the worst ideas. The snake is puzzled enough if he isn't coiled around something, let alone if he doesn't have his tail to eat.

I will explain the cosmos now as the Forbears and the Cyrodiilics understand it, since my audience here is formed of many of you. It is a wheel. It is made of the Mundus, around which is their Gods and around which is Oblivion. The wheel rotates around its centre, which the Empire says is their White Tower. The centre of known existence. 

Within the wheel we have our daily lives. We walk the same way the wheel turns, except for us, the Crowns - we can walk the other way too. That's not important right now, it's a topic for a more interesting lecture.

The Wheel, which we call Satakal, is everything. It's the world, it's the Planets, it's Oblivion. Outside that is the void, the void of Akel. It is defined by not being, so it is  not part of everything, even though Akel IS part of Satakal. The Wheel is where nothing meets everything, where yes meets no. I think some of you call it a Gray Maybe. 

Let us imagine that you are to contemplate the wheel in its totality. That you are to look into the eye of Satakal, and see his tail in his mouth. Move backward, look at him completely. If he is everything, and you are looking at him, then where are you? 

Let us describe the snake as a wheel again. The Elf-God Vivec said this about the wheel - if you look at it sideways, what do you see? It is a Tower. Not a built tower, like White-Gold or long-lost Orichalc, but a line. Like a number ONE, or the letter, and thus the word 'I'. A fusion of all that is, as One. That 'I', he says, is the Tower - and when you realised that you must also be an 'I', if you are looking at it too, then that too is the Tower. They call this CHIM, which is old Elf-talk for royalty, majesty. The Elf-God claims he reached this state, but I'm not so sure.

But they say it's hard to stay as an individual when faced with the crowned tower that represents everything. If you don't maintain the state, so it goes, then you cease. Either you realise that you can't be part of everything, and thus you are nothing, or you realise that there is nothing separating you from everything, and so you become part of the whole, separate from nothing. Either way, you would cease to exist as a separate, recognisable entity.

Now, a lot of you came here today to hear me talk about the Dwarves, and only now do I make mention of them. I hear you ask: Is this what I think happened to the Dwarves? Did they disappear because they reached this state, which the Elves call Zero-Sum? Yes, I think they did.

How did this happen? For one person to see the tower is possible, but for everyone in the same race? Impossible. But listen - the Dwarves were telepaths. They could link their minds, and we have found the devices they used to do it at Stros M'kai. Others have been found in Morrowind and High Rock. If all the Dwarves were linked at the same time, one Dwarf seeing Satakal sideways might make all of them see it.

So which one saw it? I think their chief magician, the Tonal Architect, Kagrnak. With his enemies, the Elves, getting closer, he took his forbidden tools and magics and used them on the Heart of Sep, which was hidden under Red Mountain. In that instant, the power of Sep's Heart showed him something.

Sep is what we call Shezzar here. You also call him Sheor and Lorkhan, and some of you believe this about him - that he knew how to do CHIM, but he failed on purpose so that the rest of us could learn how to do it right. 

When Kagrnak used the tools, he saw the Tower. He wasn't prepared. And that is why there are no Dwarves left. They all saw it at once, and not one was prepared.
