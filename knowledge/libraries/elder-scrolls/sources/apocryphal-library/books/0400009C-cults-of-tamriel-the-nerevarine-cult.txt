Cults of Tamriel

The Nerevarine Cult and the Sixth House

by Gwendoline Denwick
</p>
<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/I_letter.png'>n the late Third Era, Morrowind was under severe threat from the magical plague-weather that emanated from Red Mountain. This terrible sickness was known as the Blight, and the Tribunal Temple as it was in those times proclaimed that this was the work of their ancient enemy, Dagoth Ur, one of the captains of the long-deceased warleader of Resdayn, Indoril Nerevar. 

The Blight was unlike any ordinary plague, and the Imperial garrison had to rely on the wisdom of the Temple healers to combat it. It warped and sickened bodies and souls, causing death, disfigurement and also terrible madness and strange visions. Word of a 'soul sickness' began to spread, and the notion that one's very immortal essence could become plagued began to take root in the people of Morrowind.

At first, the Empire regarded this sickness as a severe but mundane threat. But as time went on, unusual occurences were noted and Imperial citizens became the target of local gangs and assassins. Both the Empire and he Temple became agitated by various groups - most of which were small bands of smugglers and a larher network of organised anti-Empire criminals, but foremost among them were the Nerevarine Cult and the Sixth House. 

The Sixth House, we now know, were the remnants of House Dagoth, one of the ancestral houses that founded ancient Resdayn. How their leader, Dagoth Ur, had come to survive since antiquity is still poorly understood - but mystics persist in their claim that certain ancient creation myths were indeed true.

Regardless, the Sixth House, led by Dagoth Ur, were indeed attempting to overthrow the Temple of Morrowind as well as remove the Empire for good, and establish in its place a new theocracy of unknown nature. 

The structure of the Sixth House was hierarchical - below Dagoth Ur there were a number of 'Ash Vampires' who were served by various minions, all taking the title Dagoth as well. Witness reports claim that the more prominent members of the House were quite unlike the ordinary Dunmer of Morrowind, being altered by the Blight in a way that supposedly benefited them in the use of arcana and physical strength. The lower ranking members seem to have been fairly unremarkable Dunmer. No non-Dunmer members of this cult have ever been reported. A small number of survivors of the cult claim that they were infected in their sleep by dreams of Dagoth Ur, who commanded them to do his bidding. With the reported death of Dagoth Ur, the dreams stopped and allowed them to return to normality. 

Dagoth Ur's death happened at the hands of the Nerevarine, and this caused the end of the Blight. The Nerevarine was guided on their path by the Nerevarine Cult who were operating in direct opposition to the Sixth House, who were attempting to destroy the Empire and the Temple. So why, then, was the Nerevarine Cult so persecuted by both?

As such things often are in the study of sects and cults, prophecy plays a huge part. Indoril Nerevar died after an ancient battle in the First Era, which the Temple usually blames on Dagoth Ur on the Dwarves. It was, in fact, this very battle that allegedly 'caused' that ancient race's disappearance.

However, one witness to the encounter - Nerevar's own battle companion, Alandro Sul - claimed that the Tribunal actually murdered him after the battle, and used the ancient magick of the Dwarves to reach godhood. Though mortally wounded and blinded, he passed on this knowledge to the Ashlanders, who formed the Nerevarine Cult. This cult believed that one day Nerevar would return as a reincarnated hero known as the Nerevarine, who would cast the Tribunal gods down as false gods, remove the Empire from Morrowind, and thwart Dagoth Ur.

The Cult survived almost entirely among the Ashlanders, the nomadic Dunmer who roam the volcanic wastes and plains of Vvardenfell and the southern Morrowind mainland. A few Temple scholars, while not connected directly with the cult, came to the same conclusions and formed the 'Dissident Priests', another group who would be persecuted by the Temple.

The Urshilaku Ashlanders of the north of Vvardenfell were the most sincere adherents of the cult, and their wise woman and Ashkhan, Nibani Maesa and Sul-Matuul, were the mer who would instruct the Nerevarine on the prophecy.

Being confined to the Ashlanders, the cult had little impact until the Nerevarine arrived. Various 'failed incarnates' had, through the years, attempted to thwart Dagoth Ur but had died in the attempt.

The success of the Nerevarine at Red Mountain ended the Blight and destroyed the Sixth House, so the notion that there was indeed a powerful supernatural source of the trouble is generally validated, though the reports of a 'Second Numidium' and the 'Heart of Lorkhan' are usually treated with some scepticism. Whatever the case, the aims of the Nerevarine Cult were validated, and along with the Dissident Priests they came to sway the new Temple doctrine in the coming years.

The Tribunal gods are indeed now departed from Morrowind, though it cannot be said with certainty that the Nerevarine is responsible. The alleged death of Vivec is often blamed for the fall of the 'Lie Rock' above Vivec city, which ultimately depopulated central Morrowind and led to its de facto separation from the Empire. The Temple and the Empire and the Sixth House were all gone, and the Nerevarine Prophecy, if it truly existed, was fulfilled.
