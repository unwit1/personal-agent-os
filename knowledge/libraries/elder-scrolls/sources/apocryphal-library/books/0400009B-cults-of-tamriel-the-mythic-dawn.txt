Cults of Tamriel

The Mythic Dawn

by Gwendoline Denwick
</p>
<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/T_letter.png'>he Mythic Dawn are, by now, probably the most infamous of all Tamriel's former sects. Their contribution to the end of the Third Era has solidified their place in the annals of the land's most vilified sects, and their swift annihilation in the years that followed is regarded as barely sufficient in terms of justice.

The formation of the Mythic Dawn is lost to time, though it is generally believed to be the case that they were founded by the same leader who led them until their collapse, Mankar Camoran, son of the notorious Camoran Usurper who fought against the Empire in the earlier Third Era. Though Haymon Camoran - the Usurper - had been a Bosmer, Mankar was an Altmer. His mother was also claimed to be a Bosmer, so this unusual shift in his lineage remains unexplained other than by fanciful references to Mehrunes' Razor. For it was in the name of Mehrunes Dagon that the Mythic Dawn was formed, presumably around the time of the Tiber Wars since the notorious 'Commentaries on the Mysterium Xarxes' can be provenanced to that period.

The cult worshipped Mehrunes Dagon, and their most sacred text was the aforementioned Mysterium Xarxes, a now-destroyed tome comprising fell knowledge of the times before the Dawn. Their goal was to bring forth an age of destruction, summoning Dagon into the Mundus, by destroying the divine safeguards that allow Akatosh's sacred liminal barrier between Nirn and Oblivion from being transgressed by the hordes of the latter.

At the end of the Third Era this was ultimately achieved. The Emperor, Uriel Septim VII, was executed by assassins shortly after the murder of his own sons. Before a new heir could be found to light the Dragonfires, the very fires which protect Tamriel from Oblivion, the swarms of Dagon attacked through portals across all of the realms.

The result of the Crisis was the eventual defeat of the powers of Dagon at the hands of Martin Septim, and these trials are well-documented elsewhere, so we shall continue to examine the cult themselves.

Their ability to infiltrate Imperial society seems to stem from a plan many years in the making. Many nobles and aristocrats were found to have been turned by Camoran's influence, as well as plenty of the realm's underclass. Folk of all races were bound by a common cause in the worship of Dagon, where they plotted until their moment came.

The moment came in 3E 433, with Uriel VII's popularity already in some decline following the Imperial Simulacrum and the deaths of many protestors who demanded that his heirs be submitted to inquisition - it was a popular belief that Jagar Tharn had replaced them with doppelgangers. In the midst of this unrest, the Mythic Dawn made their play for power. They pursued the Emperor and his bodyguard into the ancient subterranes below the Imperial Palace, and murdered him.

With their primary goal achieved, the Dawn was mostly content to let the Daedra go about their business, although they did continue to operate - mostly to prevent the Blades from sabotaging their plans. They based themselves in a secret location near Lake Rumare, whose location was supposedly concealed within the Commentaries - meaning that presumably they had been operating there for some four centuries.

They were ultimately unsuccessful, and Martin Septim, along with his Blades, was able to thwart Mankar Camoran and his cult and seal the threshold of Oblivion forever. In the following years, the Vigil of Stendarr was founded, and the remnants of the cult were hunted down and arrested or executed.

The motives for the cult's summoning of Dagon are bogged down in myth and mysticism. On the surface, they seemed to believe that Dagon's destruction of Nirn would return it to the time of the Dawn Era, before the formation of the material world by Shezarr and Magnus. This would align their goals with the myths of Aldmeris and the Altmeri belief that the material world is a prison to be escaped.

A reading of the Commentaries on the Mysterium Xarxes (there have been no reliable reports of the contents of the original book itself, though it was destroyed by the Blades during the crisis) suggests that Dagon was born in the time before the Dawn and heralded the end of some previous age, and that his destruction of Tamriel promises 'Nu Mantia', or 'liberty' from the wheel of creation. 

Very occasionally, a splinter remnant surfaces in some dark corner of Tamriel, only to be stamped out once again by the Vigil. These days it is unknown if any of the Mythic Dawn remain, waiting in the shadows to choke out the sky once more.
