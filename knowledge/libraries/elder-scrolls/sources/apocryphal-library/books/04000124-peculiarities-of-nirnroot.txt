<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/N_letter.png'>irnroot has presented an enigma to every botanist and horticulturalist at somepoint. It shares traits with very few other plants, and diverges from itself with alarming keenness.

The first peculiarity is the chiming sound that it makes. Bosmer hortimancers have claimed that this is the plant using some variance of thaumal harmony, using 'sound magic' the same way that Nords and Dwemer have been known to. The sound persists in all weathers and all environments, but stops the moment that the plant is unearthed.

Another peculiarity is the wine it can be made into. It is a difficult beverage to produce, and those who have made it and sampled it claim that it is delicious, but were never observed to drink any. The level of the liquid in the bottle slowly goes down, and the drinker gets drunker, yet never a drop gets drunk.

And then there is the matter of the flowers. It must certainly has flowers, for the seeds are born far and wide by birds. Ask an nirnroot grower and they will insist it has beautiful flowers, but none can ever describe them. And have you ever seen a nirnroot flower? Or for that matter, actually seen the seeds? Nirnroot growers indeed do exist, and their noisy farms of the high-pitched herb are a matter of fact. But how did they come to grow?

The secret is in the ground itself, for the Nirnroot's mystery is in its very name. It is the roots of the Earth Mother herself, her gentle song to her children for whom she died in the ancient past. Mother Nir, always rocking her children to sleep.
