As recorded by Hromi Swan-Tongue

<p align="left">
<img src='img://Textures/Interface/Books/Illuminated_Letters/I_letter.png'>t was in Hrothmund's time, some winters before his doom at the teeth of the dire-wolf Ondjage, that Hrodmir and Volgir, who were the sons of Asvald Dragon-Hide and Hyldegarde the Pale Death, were drinking in Kolbjorn's hall. Kolbjorn had died in battle, and he was the son of Huldmar the Screamer, and he had died at the hands of Alfreidr the Wolf-Queen who was daughter to Hircine the Hunt-King. The great banner-thanes, torc-guards, barrow-vaunts, hunters, falconers, skaalfolk and bear-saerkers had come to raise their horns and plant their swords in the old man's honour. Ysrit the Clever-woman was there, who was the first to steal cold from the sun, as was Ogrit the Shield-biter, who drank the crimson dawn, and Elgamar the Half-elf, last of the Snow Prince's line, and Turle the Wreaker who could find turquoise in unusual places.

At a moment when the spirits were high, Volgir fell dead. The clever-folk saw the froth coming from his mouth and said that he had been poisoned. When Hrodmir saw that his brother was dead, he howled with grief so great that even Shor wept in Sovngarde above. He demanded to know who had slain his brother thus, and the answer was soon clear. For Olnraed the Bonewright stood in the doorway, and then he fled. Olnraed was son of Kasvir the Night-Bellow, who was son of Hircine the Hunt-King, and he had tried to poison Hrodmir, who had slain Kasvir in the hunt for Alfreidr, but they had drunk from each others cups. He had hidden among the warriors in order to slay his quarry. Hrodmir bellowed with rage and charged after the man, but he had already turned into smoke in the night sky.

Hrodmir was a worthy and wealthy thane, but even after all the years travelling over Solstheim he was never able to corner the wolf-man, who had always been one step ahead. But after his many years of promised blood-vengeance were almost done, he did come to the place called Benkongerike, which had once been called Qethjunaar. It was here that he met Vulgrim the Sword in the Wind, who sold his blade to the Jarl or the King who would reward him in blood-gold. 

Hrodmir said to Vulgrim, "You are Vulgrim the Sword in the Wind, champion of Kyne the Hawk, your aspect speaks your name for you."

Vulgrim said "And you are Hrodmir, called the Broken because your days of unrested vengeance have been your second enemy."

Hrodmir said, "That is what they call me now, it is as you say. My wealth in the hall has dwindled over the years but I have gold still and a promise I must die before I abandon."

Vulgrim said, "Then we are decided, you will reward me in gold and the sword in the wind shall blow for you."

And so they hunted together. But time had been against them, and the forces of the Hunt-king had knotted their years together like the ropes of great tongues, and so they had become ready and well-armed by the time the two warriors crossed the Moesring mountains.

And on the slew of ice before them was the great hunt, and at its front was Hircine the Hunt-King himself, though he did not lead the fight because he was a god and it was his duty to behold.

Vulgrim descended on the pack like the wind of Kyne herself, and his blade cut through mist and ice as though they were one and the same. He had been chosen by the gods to fight, and this would be his final battle, and it would be witnessed by only two who were to survive, and those two were Hrodmir the Broken and Hircine the Hunt King.

He slew Wynjerle Broken-Torc, Kangthar the Twice-Blooded, Eldric the Man-Brother of Ondjage, Hundir Bloody-Pelt and the Three Children of Ulfgarne. He beheaded in one slice Eivar Eldenwulf, Ymris Moonshatter and the Nameless Wolfman of Lokken. And only when he had slain all but one did he fall to his knee, with foam brimming from his mouth, for he had been stabbed by Olnraed with poisoned dagger, and the worm was still in his man-form. And so the champion of Kyne died at the White Ridge.

But Olnraed had forgotten that Hrodmir was even there, for his fighting had not been so mighty, though he had slain Vorka the Wolf-Crone and Hadrik Bloodclaw. And so Hrodmir, who had Olnraed in full view now, did charge, and it was not enough that Olnraed made good on his wolf-form now, because Hrodmir closed on him too quickly. And they did battle, but it was not long before Hrodmir claimed his vengeance and was the bane of Olnraed as Olnraed had been his brother's.

And Hircine said, "You are Hrodmir the Broken, and you have been well named, and you are the last standing, which grants you all the honours of the hunt."

And Hrodmir said, "My hunt is done, Hircine Hunt-king, I have no need of your honours, but I respect them all the same."

Hrodmir did place the payment for the sell-sword at the place where he buried Vulgrim. And he raised three great stones, one to speak of Vulgrim, and another for his brother, and another to warn of the Hunt-king's fervour. And those stones were lain at White Ridge where the battle was won, at Benkongerike where he first met Vulgrim, and at Kolbjorn's Hall, which is now his barrow.
