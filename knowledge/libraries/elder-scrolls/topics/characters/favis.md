## Role
Favis is an elderly Stros M'Kai bell-tower watchman. He and Prnell form a harbor signaling pair: Prnell watches arriving ships and signals with a flag, while Favis rings the bell.

## Quest connection
Favis participates in the information network used during **Contact the League**, where Cyrus is trying to locate the Restless League and learn what happened to Iszara.

## Evidence-backed dialogue network
Favis has dialogue concerning:
- Iszara and people from the Restless League who had looked for her.
- the Restless League's reputation and recent activity.
- Crown and Forebear politics.
- Prince A'tor and the destruction of his flagship.

## Epistemic notes
Favis is useful precisely because his dialogue contains several different evidence modes.

### Reported hearsay
His account of A'tor's apparent death depends on sailors from the **Garamar**, which he identifies as second in the battle line behind A'tor's flagship. This should become a layered claim:
`Favis reports -> Garamar sailors witnessed -> destruction of A'tor's flagship / apparent death of A'tor`.

### Explicit non-eyewitness testimony
Favis says he did not personally see the dragon and instead heard about it from survivors. Claims about the dragon's location and treatment of prisoners therefore require hearsay provenance.

### Local perspective and opinion
His description of occupied Stros M'Kai, his assessment of Richton, and his judgments about Crowns, Forebears, and the League combine observations with political/evaluative opinion. These should not be flattened into omniscient narration.

### Fallibility
The bell interaction demonstrates that Favis can confidently misunderstand mundane events: Cyrus can determine that the bell is not broken while Favis concludes that Cyrus repaired it. This is useful evidence for the library's narrator-reliability model.
