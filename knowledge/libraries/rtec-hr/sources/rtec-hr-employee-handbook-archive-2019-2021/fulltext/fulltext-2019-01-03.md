0200 [Heading2] Kentucky Pregnant Worker’s Act
0201 Beginning June 27, 2019, Kentucky employers must provide reasonable accommodations to employees who are limited due to pregnancy, childbirth, and related medical conditions, unless it would impose an undue hardship on the employer to do so.
0202 The Kentucky Pregnant Workers Act amends the Kentucky Civil Rights Act (KCRA) and applies to employers with at least 15 employees within the state in each of at least 20 calendar weeks in the current or preceding calendar year. The Act also applies to any agent of the employer. Governor Matt Bevin signed the Act into law on April 9, 2019.
0203 Under the Act, “related medical conditions” that must be reasonably accommodated, absent undue hardship to the employer, include lactation and the need to express breast milk for a nursing child.
0204 Reasonable Accommodations
0205 The Act provides that an employee may not be required to take leave from work if another reasonable accommodation can be provided.
0206 The Act provides examples of reasonable accommodations an employer may have to provide to an employee who is limited due to her pregnancy, childbirth, or related medical conditions, including:
0207 More frequent or longer breaks
0208 Time off to recover from childbirth
0209 Acquisition/modification of equipment
0210 Appropriate seating
0211 Temporary transfer to a different job
0212 Modified schedules
0213 Light duty
0214 Private space, other than a bathroom, to express breast milk
0215 Undue Hardship
0216 In determining whether an undue hardship exists, an employer must consider, among other things, the duration of the requested accommodation and whether the employer has a policy of providing, has provided in the past, or is providing similar accommodations to other employees for any reason. If such a policy or practice exists, then a rebuttable presumption is created that the accommodation does not impose an undue hardship on the employer. This rebuttable presumption is sure to cause chaos for a while, as the statute is very broadly worded.
0217 Interactive Process
0218 The law expressly requires the employer and employee to engage in a timely, good faith, and interactive process to determine effective reasonable accommodations.
0219 Notice
0220 By June 27, employers must post a notice of the new law. In addition, employers must begin providing written notice to new employees upon commencement of employment of their right to be free from discrimination based on pregnancy, childbirth, and related medical conditions, and their right to be reasonably accommodated for such conditions.
0221 By July 27, 2019, employers must provide a similar written notice to current employees.
0222 Compared with Existing Laws
0223 The Kentucky Pregnant Workers Act significantly expands the scope and breadth of what must be provided under existing law. It requires employers to accommodate employees who are limited — not only considered disabled as under the Americans with Disabilities Act Amendments Act (ADAAA) or KCRA, or unable to perform an essential function of their job due to a serious health condition as under the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA). What does it mean to be “limited?” Who decides whether a pregnant employee is “limited”? These questions remain unanswered.
0224 In addition, employers covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) are required only to provide non-exempt employees with lactation breaks (subject to an undue hardship defense for smaller employers) and a private space, other than a bathroom, to express breast milk for up to one year. However, the new Kentucky statute also applies to exempt employees and does not limit how long an employer is required to accommodate a nursing mother.

0260 [Heading2] FAMILY MEDICAL LEAVE ACT (FMLA)
0261 The Family Medical Leave Act of 1993 requires employers with 50 or more employees within a 75-mile radius to offer workers up to 12 weeks of unpaid leave within a 12-month period for adoption or childbirth, or to care for a seriously ill child, parent, spouse, or the employee’s own illness.
0262 Military Family Leave: New Leave Entitlement. An eligible employee who is the spouse, son, daughter, parent, or next of kin of a covered service member who is recovering from a serious illness or injury sustained in the line of duty on active duty is entitled to up to 26 weeks of leave in a single 12-month period to care for the service member. This military caregiver leave is available during “a single 12-month period” during which an eligible employee is entitled to a combined total of 26 weeks of all types of FMLA leave.
0263 The 12-month period would be counted from the date of occurrence or the first day of the leave.
0264 In the event an employee used less than a 12 weeks leave and returned to work after 6 weeks, the employee would be entitled to use the balance of the 12 week period at a later date during the same year, should the need arise.

0534 [Heading1] VIDEO SURVEILLANCE & AUDIO POLICY/VIDEO AUDIO MAINTENANCE POLICY
0535 [NoSpacing] Rural Transit Enterprises Coordinated, Inc. (RTEC) places the highest priority on passenger and public safety. Placing video cameras & audio equipment on RTEC vehicles and property is one way to ensure passenger safety, proper driver management and general security.
0536 [NoSpacing] Posting Guidelines:
0537 [NoSpacing] RTEC shall post notices on each vehicle (and on its properties) where equipment is located. The notice shall be in substantially the following form:
0538 [NoSpacing] THIS VEHICLE OR PROPERTY HAS AUDIO AND VIDEO RECORDING EQUIPMENT. BY ENTERING THE VEHICLE (PROPERTY) AND RIDING WITH RTEC, YOU ACKNOWLEDGE THAT YOUR IMAGE AND/OR VOICE MAY BE RECORDED BY THE EQUIPMENT AND THAT YOU HAVE NO REASONABLE EXPECTATION OF PRIVACY IN YOUR IMAGE AND/OR VOICE. RTEC USES THIS EQUIPMENT FOR A VARIETY OF REASONS. YOUR IMAGE AND/OR VOICE MAY BE USED FOR ANY SUCH PURPOSE – INCLUDING FULFILLING REQUESTS FOR VIDEO/AUDIO TAPES FOR RIGHT TO KNOW AND PUBLIC SAFETY PURPOSES. RTEC’S AUDIO/VIDEO MAINTENANCE POLICY IS ON FILE AT RTEC’S ADMINISTRATIVE OFFICE AT 100 EAST MAIN STREET, MT. VERNON, KY 40456 AND ON OUR WEBSITE AT WWW.RIDERTEC.ORG.
0539 [NoSpacing] Ownership of Recordings:
0540 [NoSpacing] The ownership of any video/audio recordings from RTEC vehicles (or property) rests exclusively with RTEC.
0541 [NoSpacing] In the case of accident investigations, criminal investigations and/or for other purposes, RTEC may provide recordings to its solicitor, insurance adjuster and other persons who may require the use of such recordings to defend RTEC.
0542 [NoSpacing] Recordings may be subject to production/disclosure by RTEC pursuant to order of court, subpoena, or other legal process.
0543 [NoSpacing] Viewing and Monitoring Policy:
0544 [NoSpacing] RTEC may view cameras and/or listen to audio for any reason, including performance reviews of drivers or other employees, accident investigation, investigation of passenger and/or driver complaints, camera maintenance and/or testing, criminal investigations purposes, or other reason determined by RTEC.
0545 [NoSpacing] The Executive Director shall designate the Safety & Security Officer, Operations Manager, Human Resource Manager, and the IT technician as well as others to the individual’s privy to the review of recordings.
0546 [NoSpacing] RTEC may use equipment as a method of cooperating with law enforcement personnel to investigate accidents, crimes or other activities upon request of such a law enforcement officer. The decision to allow law enforcement officers to review recordings shall rest with the RTEC Executive Director. RTEC shall also produce recordings pursuant to lawful order of Court or unobjected to subpoena.
0547 [NoSpacing] Video/Audio Recordings Retention:
0548 [NoSpacing] In the event of accident-investigation retention, such recordings shall be retained for a period of three years after the event, or until such time as litigation has come to final resolution.
0549 [NoSpacing] In the event that a recording is retained for any other purposes, RTEC shall refer to its record retention policy.
0550 [NoSpacing] Recordings shall be retained in a manner that protects any digital and/or taped video/audio format, based upon available technology at RTEC.
0551 [NoSpacing] RTEC manages a variety of equipment. Each camera system retains recordings pursuant to that system. For this reason, RTEC does not guarantee for any certain period of time recordings will be retained under circumstances where RTEC is not on notice of any accident or other investigation.
0552 [NoSpacing] Maintenance:
0553 [NoSpacing] RTEC shall check the operation of its equipment on vehicles once every 3 months.
0554 [NoSpacing] Operators or maintenance personnel shall report malfunctioning equipment to the Operations Manager upon discovery of such malfunction.
0555 [NoSpacing] RTEC shall not make it a practice of checking equipment on daily pre-trip and/or post-trip inspections.
0556 [NoSpacing] RTEC shall use its best efforts to ensure that its equipment is operational on a daily basis. However, due to the mechanical nature of the equipment, RTEC cannot guarantee the operation of equipment at any given time.
