Introduction: Goals & Objectives

An effective preventative maintenance program and good record
keeping system is as important to a successful transportation system as
the purchase of the vehicles. Rather than waiting until something has
been put in place to keep repair costs at a minimum and extend the
useful life of the vehicle.

The driver plays a major role in the safety and preventive maintenance
program by conducting the Pre-Trip vehicle inspection each day prior to
the operation of the vehicle and also with a Post-Trip vehicle inspection.

Being responsible for the systematic daily inspection of the vehicle, the
driver must have full knowledge of the maintenance program in place.
Having these responsibilities will ensure that the driver notifies the
supervisor when there is a problem, or when it is time for the scheduled
maintenance to be performed.

This manual has been prepared as a guideline for vehicle operators and
discusses the correct procedures for conducting the daily vehicle
inspections, vehicle accident procedures, communications system, and
contains report forms and/or procedures used on a regular basis. Every
driver should be able to perform the daily safety check, know when the
vehicle needs repairs, and report it promptly to the supervisor.

Mechanics should be made aware of the minimum standards required
for the transit system’s vehicles. Certain maintenance requirements
vary for each type of vehicle.

The maintenance program is subject to change and will become
effective upon notification from employer.

Vehicle Inspections

The driver is required to conduct a pre-trip inspection prior to the
operation of the vehicle, and observation inspection of the vehicle
during vehicle operations, and conduct an after-trip (Post-Trip)
inspection of the vehicle at the end of the day. The inspections
are to be completed thoroughly and promptly with any unsafe
conditions to be reported to the supervisor, immediately. Daily
inspections must be documented electronically using assigned
Tablet or using Paper Form(s) as the daily inspections occur.

The driver must unlock all emergency exits prior to boarding any
passengers. The vehicle inspections will be conducted generally
as follows:

Pre-Trip Inspection Procedures

(1)    Front – Begin the inspection at the front of the vehicle.
Inspect for damages to the vehicle body, glass, lights, and
windshield, Check the condition of the windshield wiper blades.
Clean the head-lamps, other lights, and windshield. Check the
surface underneath the vehicle for fluid leaks and note any fumes
being present.

(2)     Right Side – Inspect the right side of the vehicle from front
to rear, including doors, glass, body, and tires, Check the tires and
wheels for damages and proper tire inflation. Unlock the
passenger door and cargo doors. Check the safety condition of
steps or running boards. Clean the mirrors and glasses.

                                                                        7

(3)      Rear – Inspect the rear of the vehicle including doors,
glasses, wide view lens, vehicle body, and bumpers. Unlock the
rear door(s) (this is an emergency exit). Inspect the spare tire, tire
hangers, exhaust system, jack and lug wrench. Check for the
presence of the emergency equipment: flares or triangle reflector
kit, flashlight, blanket, small tools. Clean the wide-view lens,
windows, tail lights, and license plate.

(4)     Left Side – Inspect the left side of the vehicle from rear to
front. Inspect doors, glass, vehicle body, and tires. Check the
tires and wheels for damages and proper tire inflation. Inspect
and clean the mirrors and glasses.

(5)     Engine Compartment – The last door to be unlocked is the
driver’s door. Pull the inside hood release and inspect the engine
compartment. Check fluid levels, belts hoses, and battery. Add
fluids when low.

(6) Interior Inspection – The interior inspection includes checking
the upholstery, passenger restraints, wheelchair locks and tie-
downs. Check the interior of the vehicle for cleanliness, etc.
Locate the presence and replenish the supplies of first aid kit, fire
extinguisher, reporting forms, Tablet, and other items required to
be on board the vehicle, as discussed within this manual.

(7)    Inspection of Driver’s Compartment – Adjust the mirrors
and driver’s seat. Observe the operation of the gauges on the
instrument panel and assure proper working order. Check all
exterior lights, brake lights, reverse alarm, holding power of the
foot brakes and parking brake.

Note: Notify your supervisor of any condition which is unsafe or
requires immediate attention. Record all other conditions on the
daily inspection form (electronic/paper). Buckle up and drive safely!

During-Trip Inspection Procedures

While operating the vehicle, observe the driving conditions,
mileage, any abnormal sounds or operations of the vehicle. Check
the gauges on the instrument panel for proper working order.

Re-fuel the vehicle at the end of the day. Avoid re-fueling while
passengers are on board.

Post-Trip Inspection Procedures

The after-trip inspection of the vehicle includes a thorough
inspection of the interior, exterior, and report of the driving
condition of the vehicle.

(1)     Inspect the interior of the vehicle from front to rear.
Ensure no passenger or passenger’s property has mistakenly been
left onboard. Lock the emergency exits, doors and windows.
Remove any debris or lost items. Replace all passenger restraints
in storage position. Check the side vents and benches for
damages which may cause injury to a passenger.

                                                                     9

(2)    Turn off all lights, radios, wipers, heater, or air conditioner
units.
(3)    Lock the doors and take the keys!
(4)    Exterior – Complete a thorough inspection of the vehicle’s
exterior, including tire, body, windshield, mirrors, and glasses.
Report any damages to the supervisor no matter how minor.
(5)    Complete all required reports and give these to your
supervisor at the end of the day or as required.

Required Inspections and Reports

A thorough inspection of the vehicle before, during, and after
operation is required. Failure to report to your supervisor
conditions which may result in the unsafe operation of the
vehicle, or accidental body damages reflects upon the reliability of
the driver and may result in disciplinary action or dismissal.

The daily inspection of the vehicle, proper use and care, along
with an adequate report of conditions will help the company to
maintain its vehicles in safe operating condition, reduce the cost
of maintenance, and increase the useful life of the vehicle. (See
Driver’s Daily Inspection Report)

Scheduled Maintenance Reports

The listed services are based upon the vehicle being operated in
dusty areas, idling for extended periods, and low-speed operating
conditions. Listed below are inspections which should be made at
intervals shown, to help ensure proper safety, emission
performance and dependability of the vehicle. Notify the
supervisor of any problems and have necessary repairs completed
promptly. For the driver’s safety, and that of others, any safety
related parts that could have been damaged in an accident should
be inspected at once. All needed repairs should be made before
operating the vehicle.

Item Service

1        ENGINE OIL AND OIL FILTER CHANGE – Change oil and oil
filters at 5,000 miles for Gas Buses (See notes below for Diesel).
2        INSPECT BRAKE SYSTEM – The brake system should be
checked when removing the wheels for tire rotation. Inspect lines
and hoses for proper hookup, binding, leaks,         cracks,   and
chafing. Inspect disc brake pads for wear and rotors or surface
condition;      also inspect parking brake. Check parking brake
adjustment. Inspect brakes every other oil change. Inspect brakes
more often if driving conditions result in frequent braking.
3        CHASSIS LUBRICATION – Every 5,000 miles lubricate all
grease fittings in suspension and steering linkage. Lubricate
transmission / transaxle shift linkage, hood latch, hood and door
hinges, parking brake cable guides, underbody contact points and
linkage.

                                                                24

4        ROTATE & BALANCE TIRES – rotate new tires at 7,500 miles
and then every 15,000 miles.
5        FRONT WHEEL REPACK – Clean and repack front wheel
bearings at each brake relining or 15,000 miles, whichever comes
first.
6        TRANSMISSION / TRANSAXLE SERVICE – For automatic
transmissions or transaxle, change both the fluid and filter (or
service the screen) every 30,000 miles, gas and diesel.
7        COOLING SYSTEM SERVICE – Drain, flush and refill system
with new coolant every 100,000 miles.
8        SPARK PLUG AND ENGINE WIRE SERVICE – At 96,000 to
100,000 miles, replace spark plugs with type listed in Owner’s
Manual. Clean wires and inspect for burns, cracks or other damage.
Replace wires as needed.
9        AIR FILTER REPLACEMENT – Replace more often under
dusty conditions every 15,000 miles (Gas & Diesel)
10       FUEL TANK, CAP, AND LINES INSPECTION – Inspect fuel tank,
cap, and lines (including fuel rails and injection assembly, if so
equipped) for damage or leaks. Inspect fuel cap gasket for an even
filler neck imprint or any damage. Replace parts as needed. Do this
every 15,000 miles for Gas or Diesel.
11       INSPECT EXHAUST SYSTEM – Inspect for noise, leaking
fumes, rust or damage. Replace or repair as needed; check every
15,000 miles.

Vehicle Accident Reporting

If an agency owned/operated vehicle is involved in an accident, the
vehicle operator shall follow these procedures:

1.      Stop at the scene. (Do not move the vehicle until instructed
by the police, except when necessary to prevent danger to
passengers and others or if law demands.)
2.      Keep calm and assist injured persons.
3.      Call the ambulance, police, and transportation director.
Give directions to the scene of the accident.
4.      Provide police and other driver(s) with an information
required by law.
5.      Do not make any statements admitting liability or criminal
negligence that may jeopardize your rights or the right of your
passenger or employer.
6.      Obtain names, addresses, and telephone numbers of
injured persons.
7.      Note the extent of property damage and obtain names,
addresses, and telephone      numbers of other drivers.
8.      Complete and submit the accident report with a copy of the
police report to your immediate supervisor as early as possible,
and before going off duty on the day of the accident.

Communications System

Antenna height and transmitter radiating power play a major role
in determining the coverage of your radio system. Because radio
waves travel in a straight line, the maximum range of your system
would normally be the horizon as it appears from your base station
or repeater antenna. Tall buildings and topographical features,
however, may affect range causing occasional “dead” spots within
the coverage pattern. Heavy forestation can also have a damping
effect on reception. Generally, drivers can overcome these
problems by moving vehicles a short distance. In fringe areas,
where the signal is weaker, drivers may be able to maintain
continuous coverage simply by keeping the vehicle moving.

Channel Sharing: As a channel sharer, you are expected to be polite
and to observe other sharers’ rights to the radio channel. Keep
your message brief. Use the radio only for business related
messages. Listen in to make sure the channel is clear before each
transmission. It is a breach of Federal Communications Rules to
break in on another’s transmission, or override his signal. Only in
genuine emergencies, such as a serious accident, are exceptions to
this rule.

It is necessary that radio operators monitor the channel briefly for
other users’ transmissions prior to their own.

Your responsibilities to the F.C.C.

General Operating Rules:
Under Federal law, you, as a station licensee, are solely responsible
for the operational and technical compliance with the Federal
Communications Commission’s Rules and Regulations. You should
know and understand the regulations that apply to you, your
organization and your personnel. The Federal Communications
Commission governs the operation of all radio transmitters in use
by non-federal government and private business organizations.
You have probably already been granted a radio system license.
You must have this license prior to the operation of your radio
system. This license must be renewed periodically, usually every
five years. It most also be posted at the site of the transmitter
control point.

How to Avoid F.C.C. Citations:
Here are two common reasons for issuance of citations by the
F.C.C. which can subject the licensee to fines and/or license
revocation – with suggestions for avoiding them.

       1.      Not posting or making available current station
       authorization at the control point of base
       transmitter.
       2.      Operating base station transmitter at other
       than the location authorized in the station
       license.

Ask your radio communications representative for help if you need
to change the function of your radio system.

through the windshield or interfering with any other safety device. This
mobile technology makes it possible to track individual drivers, vehicle
movements and times and allows access to more accurate mapping
systems and turn-by-turn directions. The technology gives drivers
multiple options for GPS navigation with an app, complete with traffic
views, alternate-route suggestions, and other features to ensure a
smooth trip. Drivers must not allow themselves to be distracted from
driving by the new Tablet systems. Driver’s should safely pull over and
park when sending, reading, accessing, browsing, transmitting, or

retrieving electronic data on the devices. Do NOT drive distracted. Do

DOT Inspections
From time to time, drivers may be asked to take an assigned RTEC vehicle
for the annual or any other periodically required DOT Inspection at an
approved DOT Inspection site to be determined by RTEC Administration
(Typically a Weigh Station or DOT Check-Point site). The Driver assigned
to this task is responsible for conducting a COMPLETE pre-trip inspection
of the vehicle prior to taking it to the DOT Inspection site (See procedures
in this manual for conducting vehicle inspections). If any areas of concern

Warranty Compliance
A warranty is an assurance from a manufacturer that a product will
perform properly for a specified time or usage level. Warranties cover
new vehicles, new or replacement parts, and most vendor work. If the
product fails to meet this assurance, the manufacturer is obligated to
make restitution. Restitution may be replacement or repair of the
defective product, or reimbursement to the owner for the cost of the
repair or replacement. Warranties may be formal written policies or
implied warranties.
Warranty claims should be pursued effectively and promptly. The
warranty of vehicles, physical plant, and equipment often is valid only if
a transit agency adheres to the manufacturer’s recommended
maintenance program. The Fleet Manager is ultimately responsible for
the warranty recovery system, warranty records, recall notices, and
annual summaries of warranty claims submitted and received.

Prior to performing repairs and seeking restitution, the Fleet Manager
will request approval to perform warranty repairs from the vendor or the
manufacturer. To facilitate the process of identifying warranty items, a
review of the individual vehicle’s history file is conducted.
Documentation is maintained on the date and vehicle mileage at time of
failure, vehicle identification number, description of work performed,
and any costs incurred so the part(s) can be matched with the warranty
claim.
