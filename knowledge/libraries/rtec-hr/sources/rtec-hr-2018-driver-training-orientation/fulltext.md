DRIVER TRAINING & ORIENTATION SYNOPSIS  
RTEC Driver Training

## RTEC Driver Training

Once an application for a posted job opening is received, it is reviewed by RTEC Administration. Administration checks the application for completion and mistakes. Prior experience and education are taken into consideration before the application is processed. A Driving Record Check and a Background Record Checks are performed. References are also contacted and checked. Once an application is approved for further review, an interview with the applicant is scheduled. At least two members of the Administration or Management Team conduct the interview. If the interview goes well, then the applicant is called and made a pre-offer of employment pending completion of a Department of Transportation Physical and Pre-Employment Drug Test (see below for further details). Once an applicant is chosen, the following must also be completed in a timely fashion.

## Completion of Paperwork (Prior to Beginning Employment Work)

- Application: Must include "Applicant Letter" agreement to participate in the Drug & Alcohol Program with ongoing testing (Check for prior experience and completion; check references; interview)
- Employment Eligibility Verification
- Agreement to Assure Confidentiality; Agreement with Cabinet for Health & Human Services
- Agreements with Employer
- Driving Record Release Form
- Background Record Release Authorization Form
- Drug-Free Agreement & Agreement to Participate in Drug & Alcohol Program
- Tax Forms
- Job Description and Requirements Reviewed
- Verification of Receipt of Policies/Procedures
- New Driver Orientation Routing Sheet
- Previous Employer Drug & Alcohol Testing Release Form completed and Previous Employer contacted regarding testing records information as required by DOT/FTA

## Medical

Employee must have a Department of Transportation Physical & Pre-Employment DOT Drug Collection/Testing; RTEC must have negative drug test results back before operation of a vehicle or performance of safety-sensitive function. The applicant completes the D.O.T. Physical and must be defined as qualified by meeting standards in USDOT 49 CFR 391.41. An applicant/candidate must produce a verified negative drug test result prior to being hired to first perform a safety-sensitive duty as required under USDOT 49 CFR Part 40 as amended. Once results from the D.O.T. Physical are received and a verified negative drug test result is received, any remaining Pre-Employment Paperwork is finished and an official start date for the applicant/candidate is assigned. D.O.T. Physicals may also be reviewed by National Toxicology Specialists, and RTEC will adhere to the recommendation of the M.R.O.

## Background Record

Application references and criminal background record search and driver record search must be completed prior to performance of safety-sensitive function. Additional searches must be performed by searching the "Sex Offender Registry" and the "Office of Inspector General Search for Excluded Individuals" websites respectively. Findings must meet Office of Transportation Delivery and RTEC qualification standards.

## On-The-Job Training

Drivers must train on-the-job with an experienced driver for a minimum of 30 hours after Orientation and Driver Basics Training is completed. This on-the-job training is necessary for the new hire to gain experience in dealing with passengers, agencies, and performance of required job duties. This on-the-job training includes experience in a wheelchair/mobility device accessible vehicle. Training time may exceed 30 hours or may be reduced to less depending on the new driver's experience and proficiency of job requirements. Training documentation is to be reviewed by the immediate supervisor and the Operations Manager to see if the employee performance level is satisfactory. If training level is not satisfactory, then the Operations Manager will implement a corrective plan of action to Human Resources and the Training Coordinator in a timely manner. Training documentation of successful completion of on-the-job training must be forwarded on to the Training Coordinator prior to the employee being assigned a vehicle and placed on the job. Additional on-the-job training hours may be necessary.

## Reading and understanding important literature

During the orientation process, company handbooks, policies, and procedures are distributed to the Driver. This literature may include (but not limited to): Personnel Policy Handbook, Driver Handbook, Vehicle Maintenance Program, and other company policies/procedure manuals. Verification of receipt is collected for each. The employee receives a great deal of information pertaining to their job and the requirements thereof. RTEC understands this is a lot of information to receive all at once. Therefore, it is understood that the Driver will be given two (2) weeks to read and understand this important literature. Administration is available to the Driver if any questions arise during this time regarding company policy or procedure.

## Driver Training Plan (Times are approximate and may vary)

### Day 1

- Basic Orientation, Policies & Procedures
- Drug & Alcohol Awareness & Policy Training (2 Hours Minimum): Lecture using workbooks from National Toxicology Specialists and/or Training Videos; Quiz on Drug & Alcohol Awareness.
- Bus Evacuation Training (30 Minutes Minimum): Training Video viewed; hands-on practice with seatbelt cutter; discussion/lecture; review emergency exits and practice evacuation drill to be completed during Passenger Assistance Certification Training.
- Blood borne Pathogens Awareness & Prevention of Disease Transmission (1 Hour): this training may also be completed and/or supplemented when employee completes CPR & First Aid certification.
- System Safety and Security Training: National Transit Institute "Warning Signs" Training Video (30 Minutes); RTAP Video on "Problem Passengers & Challenging Situations" (30 Minutes and includes segment on Seizure Awareness).
- Defensive Driving (1 Hour Minimum; typically 1-3 Hours Credit completed within initial training period): Coaching the Van Driver Video Series; Lecture & Tests Follow; In Vehicle Ride & Evaluation on Driving Skills; if time does not permit in vehicle ride/evaluation, then on-the-job training with experienced driver and evaluation by trainer will substitute (Good driving license record is verified prior to hire). Defensive Driving Training may also consist of a Behavioral Driving Program called P.A.C.E. (P.A.C.E. stands for Plan ahead, Analyze your surroundings, Communicate with others, and Execute Safely). Defensive Driving Training is offered annually and/or as needed.
- ADA & Disabilities Awareness Introduction: Videos and/or DVDs (30 Minutes to 1 Hour)

### Day 2

- First Aid & CPR Training for Adult/Child/Infant (8 Hours): First Aid (Recertification required every 2 years minimum); Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR) & Choking Rescue Skills (Recertification required every 2 years minimum); First Aid training includes Seizure Awareness; Blood borne Pathogens Training is also inclusive of CPR & First Aid (Recertification required annually). RTEC has a Certified Instructor authorized to teach First Aid, CPR, and Blood borne Pathogens. RTEC may also outsource this training if the in-house instructor is not available.
- Fire Extinguisher & Fire Safety Training (Annual Fire Extinguisher Training Required)
- Vehicle Maintenance & Inspection: Training provided by Fleet Manager and/or ASE Certified Mechanic on Maintenance Program and Inspection Procedures (45 Minutes to 1 Hour Minimum). There is also overlap training in Daily Inspections & Defensive Driving.

### Day 3

- Passenger Assistance Training and Wheelchair/Occupant Securement Certification (8 Hours): ADA & Disabilities Awareness Continued; Seizure Awareness Review; Communication Skills (Video on the Communicating with Passengers with disabilities); Body Mechanics (handling and lifting techniques); Wheelchair and Occupant Securement Systems (Lecture, demonstration, and hands-on practice and evaluation); Lift Operating Procedures (Lecture, demonstration, and hands-on practice and evaluation); Lower-floor Mini Van with Ramp (If applicable/available); Assisting person with low vision impairment (Lecture/Demo/Practice hands-on and evaluation); "Common Wheelchair" regulation updates; Securement accessories (webbing loops and postural belts); Bus Evacuation emergency exits demonstrated/practiced (drills); Assistance with boarding/de-boarding and assistance with seatbelts; Other topics as time permits; Passenger Assistance Manual Review and Exit Exam. Periodic Refresher Training for Passenger Assistance is offered to all Drivers and is recommended at a minimum of every 2 years (Refresher hours vary based on needs).

### Day 4

- Nonviolent Crisis Intervention (NCI) Training Program. The course is offered by The Crisis Prevention Institute, Inc. and the International Association of Nonviolent Crisis Intervention Certified Instructors. Crisis intervention is a small segment of time in which staff members must intervene with another person to address behavior that may escalate into disruptive or even violent incidents. The overall goal of the program is to provide the best care, welfare, safety and security at any given moment to the individuals in our charge. The program covers the basic elements of violent behavior and identifies how a crisis can escalate and conversely, de-escalate. It identifies strategies that have proven successful and builds confidence that help staff maintain control of situations Drivers may encounter on the RTEC bus. The course does have a Unit on Personal Safety. The primary focus of CPI's Personal Safety Techniques is to protect staff and acting out individuals from injury if behavior escalates to a physical level.
- Managing Threatening Confrontations Training DVD program (1 Hour) may be offered in lieu of NCI or until NCI can be provided (Usually completed during Initial 4-days of Training Scheduled).

### Day 5

- Driver Begins On-The-Job Training: driver trains with experienced driver for approximately 30 hours strategically scheduled by Area Manager; On-The-Job training may begin between scheduled events on Day 2-4 listed above, but driver may not perform solo safety-sensitive function until all required certifications on Days 1 through 3 listed above are complete.

## Training Completion

After RTEC Orientation, Driver Basics, On-The-Job Training, and initial training certifications are completed, the driver's performance is evaluated by a responsible member of Management. All training documentation shall be retained in the employee's Training File.

Periodic refresher training and re-certifications may be required and must be completed satisfactorily. RTEC will also offer corrective training when deemed appropriate. For example, if a Driver is involved in a vehicular accident then the Driver may be required to complete Defensive Driving Training again. Other training may be scheduled as deemed necessary by RTEC Administration. In order to qualify to be an RTEC Driver, all required training must be completed satisfactorily in addition to satisfactory job performance.

The Driver is given three (3) months to develop the skills and complete the training necessary to perform his/her job. At the end of the three-month introductory period, the employee is evaluated by the Supervisor and/or Human Resource Manager to determine if he/she is performing at a satisfactory level.
