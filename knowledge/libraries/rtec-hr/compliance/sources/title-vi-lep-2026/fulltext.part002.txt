RTEC Language Assistance Measures




(3) The Nature and Importance of the Program, Activity, or Service Provided
by the Program:

Rural Transit Enterprises Coordinated, Inc. d/b/a  RTEC  was  organized  for
the sole purpose of coordinating and operating public transportation.   RTEC
understands they play an important role in the lives of our community.   The
main function for the individuals riding our transportation service  is  the
need for  mobility  for  a  continued  quality  of  life.   A  LEP  person's
inability to use public transit may hinder their ability  to  access  health
care, education or employment.   RTEC  will  continue  to  outreach  to  all
individuals and promote their efforts.   This  will  increase  our  need  to
promote our services for individuals to realize the service is available.

RTEC operates lift-equipped  vehicles  for  wheelchair  users  and  provides
affordable  and   efficient   community   transportation   services.    RTEC
constantly works toward accessing new equipment to upgrade its fleet and  to
increase services.  These services are available to EVERYONE in the  general
population of the service area and are likely to be sought by  LEP  persons.


RTEC is taking steps in order to increase outreach efforts to  LEP  language
groups. For example, RTEC has incorporated Spanish verbiage on  the  website
and in all their printed material (brochures, schedules, advertisements  and
flyers) as well as Spanish translation portions on the telephone message.

4) The Resources Available to the Recipient and Costs:

How RTEC provides Spanish language assistance:
    . Oral language services include Spanish option on telephone.
    . Bilingual Customer Service Representatives. Spanish  speaking  callers
      will be directed to a Bilingual  Customer  Service  Representative  to
      make  reservations  or  answer  questions   regarding   transportation
      services.
    . In addition to these oral language services, several written  language
      services are available.  Any written correspondence will be  forwarded
      to a translator who  can  translate  the  document  into  English  and
      translate RTEC's response into Spanish.


How RTEC provides notice to LEP persons about the availability  of  language
assistance:
    . Spanish-language advertising is  also  used  to  promote  and  educate
      clients on how to schedule  transportation  with  RTEC  (See  attached
      Spanish RTEC Brochure).
    . RTEC website being available in Spanish.
    . Instructions to vehicle operators.  Vehicle operators  are  likely  to
      encounter LEP persons.  They have several methods to respond to an LEP
      individual. Some vehicle operators may be bilingual; however,  if  the
      vehicle operator is not bilingual, they can contact dispatch for radio
      assistance with a Spanish-speaking passenger or provide  RTEC's  phone
      number to ask for a bilingual CSR or ask if another passenger  on  the
      vehicle could serve as a translator.
    . Attend community meetings  and  events  to  inform  people  of  RTEC's
      service in general and that language assistance is available.


Currently, Matthew Getsi (Network Provider Manager), Jesus Noyola  (Driver),
Wilbur Torres (Driver), Dino Melgoza  (Driver),  and  Anne  Weber  (Mobility
Manager) are  RTEC's  bilingual  Customer  Service  Representatives.   Other
language  barriers   may   be   resolved   by   using   websites   such   as
www.freetranslation.com,          http://translate.google.com,           and
http://www.bing.com/translator for assistance  in  translation.   RTEC  will
acquire other resources or contract any additional help as necessary.

These additional language assistance tools will be utilized  to  assist  LEP
individuals at no additional cost.  The notice of  "language  assistance  at
no cost" has been added to our advertisements and website.


Staff Training


Part of RTEC's LEP plan is deciding what level of staff training is  needed.
 It is important that staff members, especially those  having  contact  with
the  public,  know  their  obligation  to  provide  meaningful   access   to
information and services for LEP persons. Even  staff  members  who  do  not
interact regularly with LEP persons should be aware of  and  understand  the
LEP plan. Properly  training  staff  is  a  key  element  in  the  effective
implementation of the LEP plan.

Customer  Service  Representatives  and  Operations  staff  is  trained   to
recognize when an LEP person is in need of language assistance  and  respond
by referring the LEP person to one  of  RTEC's  bilingual  Customer  Service
Representatives.    This  awareness  training  is  included   in   CSR   and
Operations Staff Orientation and Initial On-The-Job Training.   Regular  re-
training for staff is also provided.

RTEC is looking  into  additional  training  steps  to  provide  some  basic
Spanish speaking skills to all staff and drivers.  Some video  training  may
assist with this endeavor.


Safe Harbor Provision


After completing the  four-factor  analysis  RTEC  has  determined  that  on
average no language group actually meets the  five  percent  (5%)  or  1,000
rule in our service area.  However, the Hispanic or  Latino  language  group
is an increasing population in our  service  area.   Therefore,  RTEC  makes
available Spanish brochures advertising RTEC  services,  Spanish  option  on
telephone and RTEC services available in Spanish on company  website.   Even
though RTEC does  not  translate  all  written  materials  we  will  provide
written  notice  in  Spanish  of  the  right  to  receive   competent   oral
interpretation of written materials free of cost.

RTEC understands that these safe harbor provisions apply to the  translation
of written documents only.  They do not affect the  requirement  to  provide
meaningful access to LEP individuals  through  competent  oral  interpreters
where oral language services are needed and are reasonable.


Evaluation of the LEP plan


This plan is designed to be flexible, and should be  viewed  as  a  work  in
progress. As such, it is important to consider  whether  new  documents  and
services need to be made accessible for LEP persons,  and  also  to  monitor
changes in demographics and types of services, and to update  the  LEP  plan
when appropriate.  The plan will be  reviewed  and  updated  on  an  ongoing
basis.  Updates will consider the following:

    . The number of documented LEP person contacts encountered annually
    . How the needs of LEP persons have been addressed
    . Determination of the current LEP population in the service area
    . Determination as to whether the  need  for  translation  services  has
      changed
    . Determine whether RTEC's financial resources are  sufficient  to  fund
      language assistance resources needed

RTEC  understands  the  value  that  its  service  plays  in  the  lives  of
individuals who rely on this service, and the  importance  of  any  measures
undertaken to make the use of system easier.  RTEC is  open  to  suggestions
from all sources, including customers, RTEC staff, and other  transportation
agencies with similar experiences with  LEP  communities,  and  the  general
public, regarding additional methods to improve their accessibility  to  LEP
communities.


Title VI Equity Analysis

Title 49 CFR, Appendix C, Section (3)(iv) requires  that  "the  location  of
projects requiring land acquisition and the  displacement  of  persons  from
their residences and business may not be determined on the  basis  of  race,
color, or national origin." For purposes of this  requirement,  "facilities"
does not include bus shelters, as they are considered transit amenities.  It
also does not include transit stations,  power  substations,  or  any  other
project evaluated by the National Environmental Policy Act  (NEPA)  process.
Facilities included in the  provision  include,  but  are  not  limited  to,
storage facilities, maintenance  facilities,  operations  centers,  etc.  In
order to comply with the regulations, RTEC will ensure the following:
1.      RTEC will complete a Title  VI  equity  analysis  for  any  facility
during the planning stage with regard to  where  a  project  is  located  or
sited to ensure the location is selected without regard to race,  color,  or
national origin.  RTEC  will  engage  in  outreach  to  persons  potentially
impacted by the siting of the facility. The Title VI  equity  analysis  must
compare the equity impacts of various siting alternatives, and the  analysis
must occur before the selection of the preferred site.
2.      When evaluating locations of facilities, RTEC  will  give  attention
to other facilities with similar impacts in the area  to  determine  if  any
cumulative adverse impacts might result. Analysis  should  be  done  at  the
Census tract or block group level where appropriate to  ensure  that  proper
perspective is given to localized impacts.
3.      If RTEC determines that the location of the project will  result  in
a disparate impact on the basis of race, color,  or  national  origin,  RTEC
may only locate the project in that  location  if  there  is  a  substantial
legitimate justification for locating the project  there,  and  where  there
are no alternative locations that would have a less disparate impact on  the
basis of  race,  color,  or  national  origin.  RTEC  must  demonstrate  and
document  how  both  tests  are  met.  RTEC  will   consider   and   analyze
alternatives to determine whether those alternatives would have  less  of  a
disparate impact on the basis of race, color, or national origin,  and  then
implement the least discriminatory alternative.
RTEC has not recently constructed any facilities nor does it currently  have
any facilities in the planning stage. Therefore,  RTEC  does  not  have  any
Title VI Equity Analysis  reports  to  submit  with  this  Plan.  RTEC  will
utilize demographic maps for future Title VI analysis.


RTEC Board Members


RTEC currently has 7 Board Members.  These members are elected by other
volunteer board members.  RTEC Board meetings are customarily held bi-
monthly. The census data in each of RTEC's service county area shows 96%
white, 1.6% Black or African American and 2.2% Hispanic/Latino so we find
it difficult to obtain minority Board Members.  RTEC will make efforts to
encourage minority participation on the committee.  These efforts are made
by distributing information about the participation on the committee at
public meetings and throughout the transit system.
