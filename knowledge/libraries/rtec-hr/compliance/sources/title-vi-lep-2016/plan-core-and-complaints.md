# RTEC Title VI Plan — core plan/complaint section
## Source: Title VI and Limited English Proficient Plan Revised Sept 2016

The source records that RTEC's Title VI program was approved by its Board
during a meeting held October 17, 2016, with meeting minutes identified as
Attachment VIII.

## I. Plan Statement / Assurances

Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 prohibits discrimination on the
basis of race, color, or national origin in programs and activities
receiving Federal financial assistance. Specifically, Title VI provides
that no person in the United States shall, on the ground of race, color, or
national origin, be excluded from participation in, be denied the benefits
of, or be subjected to discrimination under any program or activity
receiving Federal financial assistance.

Rural Transit Enterprises Coordinated, Inc. (RTEC) is committed to ensuring
that no person is excluded from participation in, or denied the benefits of
its transit services on the basis of race, color, or national origin, as
protected by Title VI in Federal Transit Administration Circular 4702.1B.

This plan was developed to guide RTEC in its administration and management
of Title VI related activities.

### Key responsibilities of the Title VI Coordinator

- Maintain knowledge of Title VI requirements.
- Attend training on Title VI and other nondiscrimination authorities when
  offered by FDOT or any other regulatory agency.
- Disseminate Title VI information to the public including in languages
  other than English, when necessary.
- Develop a process to collect data related to race, gender and national
  origin of service area population to ensure low income, minorities, and
  other underserved groups are included and not discriminated against.
- Implement procedures for the prompt processing of Title VI complaints.

## II. Title VI Information Dissemination

Title VI information posters shall be prominently and publicly displayed in
the RTEC lobby, on the RTEC website and on RTEC buses. The source identifies
the counties of Bell, Clinton, Cumberland, Knox, Laurel, McCreary, Monroe,
Pulaski, Rockcastle, Russell, Taylor, Wayne, and Whitley. Title VI
coordinator contact information and the RTEC Title VI complaint form are
identified as available on the RTEC website.

Title VI information shall be disseminated to RTEC employees to remind
employees of RTEC's policy statement and their Title VI responsibilities in
daily work and duties.

During New Employee Orientation, new employees shall be informed of the
provisions of Title VI and RTEC's expectations to perform their duties
accordingly.

## III. Subcontracts and Vendors

All subcontractors and vendors who receive payments from RTEC where funding
originates from federal assistance are subject to Title VI. RTEC is
responsible for ensuring subcontractors and vendors are in compliance with
Title VI requirements.

Written contracts shall contain nondiscrimination language, either directly
or through the bid specification package that becomes an associated
component of the contract.

## IV. Record Keeping

The Title VI Coordinator will maintain permanent records including copies
of Title VI complaints or lawsuits and related documentation, records of
correspondence to and from complainants, and Title VI investigations.

The historical source states that RTEC had no investigations, complaints,
or lawsuits involving allegations of discrimination on the basis of race,
color, or national origin over the preceding three years, and points to an
attached spreadsheet of transit-related Title VI investigations,
complaints, and lawsuits.

## V. Title VI Complaint Procedures

Any person who believes she or he has been discriminated against on the
basis of race, color, or national origin by Rural Transit Enterprises
Coordinated, Inc. d/b/a RTEC may file a Title VI complaint by completing and
submitting RTEC's Title VI Complaint Form.

The source states that RTEC investigates complaints received no more than
180 days after the alleged incident and processes complaints that are
complete.

Once a complaint is received, RTEC reviews it to determine jurisdiction. The
complainant is to receive an acknowledgement letter within ten days by mail
stating whether RTEC will investigate.

The source gives RTEC 90 days to investigate. If more information is needed,
RTEC may contact the complainant. The complainant has ten business days from
the date of the request letter to send requested information to the assigned
investigator. If the investigator is not contacted or the additional
information is not received within ten business days, RTEC may
administratively close the case. A case may also be administratively closed
if the complainant no longer wishes to pursue it.

After review, the investigator issues either a closure letter or letter of
finding (LOF). A closure letter summarizes the allegations and states that
there was not a Title VI violation. A letter of finding summarizes the
allegations and interviews and explains whether disciplinary action,
additional staff training, or another action will occur. The source gives
the complainant seven days after the date of the letter/LOF to appeal.

The source also states that a person may file directly with the Federal
Transit Administration Office of Civil Rights, 1200 New Jersey Avenue SE,
Washington, DC 20590.

### Procedures for tracking and investigating complaints

The source states that all RTEC services/programs are subject to Title VI
requirements and that persons who believe they experienced or witnessed
disparate treatment/impact based on race, color, or national origin may file
a written complaint with RTEC or the Federal Transit Administration.

Complaints filed directly with FTA are described as needing to be mailed
within 180 days of alleged discrimination.

The historical plan assigns responsibility for tracking, investigating, and
documenting Title VI complaints to the Executive Director or designee.

### How to file a Title VI complaint to RTEC

1. A written complaint must include the person's name/contact information,
   the date of the incident, and the identity of the person, department, or
   service that caused the complaint. Complaints may be sent by mail, email,
   fax, or hand delivery.
2. A verbal complaint may be taken and must include the same core
   identifying/incident information.
3. A person may request a neutral third party to hear a verbal complaint or
   assist with a written complaint. The neutral third party is to be selected
   cooperatively by RTEC and the complainant.
4. Complaints are addressed to the RTEC Executive Director.

### RTEC Complaint Procedure

1. A person filing a complaint based on race, color, or national origin is
   informed that the complaint may be filed directly with FTA or with RTEC.
2. If filed with RTEC, the Executive Director directs the complaint to the
   appropriate department manager for fact-finding review. The manager
   prepares a written response for the Executive Director.
3. If the complaint is valid and supported by facts, the Executive Director
   orders corrective action.
4. The complainant is consulted about the adequacy of the proposed remedy.
5. If the remedy is not acceptable, the complainant may request a hearing
   with the RTEC Finance and Personnel Committee.
6. The Finance and Personnel Committee issues a response and recommends a
   remedy within ten days of the hearing.
7. If the remedy remains unacceptable, the person is advised of the steps to
   file with FTA.

## VI. Public Participation Plan — introduction

The source states that RTEC's Public Participation Plan was developed to
ensure that all members of the public, including minorities and Limited
English Proficient populations, are encouraged to participate in
decision-making. It describes outreach as providing effective access to
service information and convenient methods for receiving and considering
public comment before service changes. It identifies stakeholders including
other governments, community-based organizations, major employers,
passengers, the general public, low-income populations, minority
populations, LEP populations, and other traditionally underserved
communities.
