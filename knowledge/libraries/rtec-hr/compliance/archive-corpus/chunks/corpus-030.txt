ESTABLISHING AFFIRMATIVE ACTION GOALS


The long-range goal of RTEC’s Affirmative Action Program  is  to  achieve  a
RTEC workforce at all job levels in which minorities,  women,  veterans  and
individuals  with  disabilities  are  represented  in  proportion  to  their
representation in  the  “applicable  labor  market”.   RTEC  identifies  its
“applicable  labor  market”  as  those  areas  which  include:  Bell,  Knox,
Whitley,   Clinton,   Cumberland,   Monroe,   Russell,   McCreary,   Laurel,
Rockcastle, Pulaski, and Wayne counties.

To address this long-range goal, RTEC will continue to advocate  the  hiring
and utilization of minorities and women at rates reasonably proportional  to
their corresponding rates of availability in the “applicable labor market”.

RTEC will also make every good faith effort to:

   1. Promote equal opportunity and achieve a  workplace  that  is  free  of
      discrimination.


   2.  Ensure  opportunities  for  all   qualified   applicants,   including
      underutilized groups.

   3. Hire the most qualified applicant using a fair  and  nondiscriminatory
      process.

   4. Make career development and  promotional  opportunities  available  to
      interested and qualified employees, including minorities and women.

   5. To ensure fairness and nondiscrimination in the termination process.




                            EMPLOYMENT PRACTICES


With each job opening, the request is forwarded to Human Resources to  start
the hiring process:

   1. Approval is obtained from the CEO to advertise and interview.
   2. Ad is placed in the local newspaper; with special attention  given  to
      EEO, Minority encouraged to apply noted on Ad.
   3. Copy of the ad is sent to Office of Employment &  Training  (OET)  for
      that location, along with the application. Drivers  applications  also
      require:


         o Driving Record Release authorization
         o Authorization for criminal background check
         o Information release form
         o Copy of RTEC’s Drug and Alcohol Policy
         o Job Description
         o Voluntary Affirmative Action Data Record


   4. OET also advertises the position and selects candidates most qualified
      and forwards to RTEC.
   5. Applicant is scheduled for interview.  Significant items considered on
      Application: ex. Drivers:


         o Experience in driving, handicap, wheelchair, CDL
         o GED Minimum Education
         o Turnover rate for previous employment
         o Pay rate requested
         o Ability to perform the job as listed on the job description with
           or without reasonable accommodation
         o Application is complete and is signed
         o Score on pre-employment applicant driver test


To begin the interview, we provide  some  background  on  the  Company,  the
position, hours, pay rate,  some  policy,  and  review  of  Benefits.   Upon
completion of the interview, selected candidates’  application  is  sent  to
our Safety/Training/Accident/Drug/Alcohol  Program  Manager  for  background
checks.

RTEC currently has the following  pre-employment  screenings  in  place  for
safety sensitive positions:

         o Driving Record Check
         o Background Record Check
         o D.O.T. Physical (USDOT 49CFR391.41)
         o Pre-employment drug test (USDOT 49CFR part 40 as amended)
                         Criteria for Hiring Drivers


1.    Criminal background check must show NO prior convictions  for  violent
or sexual
      crimes.

2.    Must have a clear 5-year background check with NO felonies or drug  or
alcohol related offenses.

3.   Must have a clear 3-year background check of driving  records  with  NO
more than 2
       moving violations.

In order to be retained as a driver for RTEC, a driver must  not  have  more
than 2 moving violations during 3 years.  Any violation of driving  contrary
to the law may be grounds for suspension, or termination of employment.

A 10-year check of the Criminal or Driving Records  that  shows  a  habitual
offender of the law is  just  cause  for  not  hiring  an  individual  as  a
professional driver.


                      MOVING VS. NON-MOVING VIOLATIONS


Violations are also classified by whether or not the vehicle was  in  motion
at the time of
the incident and traffic tickets may therefore be for either moving or  non-
moving
violations.

Examples of moving violations include:

Speeding or driving below the minimum speed
Running a stop sign or red light
Driving without a seat belt
Drunk driving (DUI and DWI)


Non-Moving violations include:

Parking in a handicapped zone or other illegal parking
Driving with an invalid vehicle registration
Having expired or missing license plates
Leaving a vehicle unattended and running

The  majority  of  traffic  violations  in  Kentucky   are   classified   as
infractions, which require paying a fine up to $500.  More serious  offenses
carry higher fines and/or imprisonment.

The final responsibility and decision for hiring is at the  sole  discretion
of the Chief Executive Director.

             Invitation to Self-Identify for Protected Veterans

     1.  RTEC  is  subject  to  the  Vietnam  Era  Veterans’   Readjustment
        Assistance  Act  of  1974,  as  amended,  (VEVRAA)  which  requires
        employers to take affirmative action to employ and advance veterans
        in  employment.   VEVRAA  prohibits  discrimination  and   requires
        affirmative action in all personnel practices  regarding  protected
        veterans.  The  statute  covers  disabled  veterans,  Armed  Forces
        service medal veterans,  recently  separated  veterans,  and  other
        veterans who served during a war, or in a  campaign  or  expedition
        for which a campaign badge has been authorized.


     2. An invitation to veterans: If you are a recently separated veteran,
        other protected veteran, or Armed Forces service medal veteran,  we
        would like to include you under our affirmative action program.  If
        you would like to be included under the affirmative action program,
        please tell us.  The term “recently separated veterans”  refers  to
        any veteran during the three-year period beginning on the  date  of
        such veteran’s discharge or release from  active  duty.   The  term
        “other protected veteran” refers to a person who served  on  active
        duty during a war or in  a  campaign  or  expedition  for  which  a
        campaign badge has been authorized, under law  as  administered  by
        the Department of Defense.  The term “Armed  Forces  service  medal
        veteran” refers to any person who, while serving on active duty  in
        the  Armed  Forces,  participated  in  a  United  States   military
        operation for which an  Armed  Forces  service  medal  was  awarded
        pursuant to Executive Order 12985 (62 FR 1209).


        An invitation to disabled veterans only:  If  you  are  a  disabled
        veteran, we would like to include you  in  our  affirmative  action
        program.  If you would like to be included  under  the  affirmative
        action program, please tell us. This information will assist us  in
        placing you in an appropriate position and in making any  necessary
        accommodations for your disability.  The  term  “disabled  veteran”
        refers to a veteran who is entitled to compensation  (or  who,  but
        for the receipt of military  retired  pay,  would  be  entitled  to
        compensation) under laws administered by the Secretary of  Defense,
        or was discharged or released from active duty because of a service-
        connected disability.


        An invitation to both:  If you are  a  disabled  veteran,  recently
        separated veteran, other protected veteran, or Armed Forces service
        medal veteran, we would like to include you under  our  affirmative
        action program.  If  you  would  like  to  be  included  under  the
        affirmative action program, please tell us.

            RTEC'S EQUAL EMPLOYMENT OPPORTUNITY POLICY STATEMENT





Rural Transit Enterprises Coordinated, Inc. has a strong commitment  to  the
community we serve and our employees.  As an equal opportunity employer,  we
strive to have a workforce that reflects the community we serve.  No  person
is unlawfully excluded from employment opportunities based on  race,  color,
religion,  national  origin,  sex   (including   gender   identity,   sexual
orientation, and pregnancy), age, genetic information,  disability,  veteran
status, or other protected class.

RTEC's Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO) policy applies to  all  employment
actions, including but not limited to, recruitment,  hiring,  selection  for
training, promotion, transfer, demotion, layoff, termination, rates  of  pay
or other forms of compensation.

All applicants and employees have the  right  to  file  complaints  alleging
discrimination.  Retaliation against an individual who  files  a  charge  or
complaint of discrimination, participates in  an  employment  discrimination
proceeding (such as an investigation or lawsuit), or  otherwise  engages  in
protected activity is strictly prohibited and will not be tolerated.

RTEC is committed to proving reasonable  accommodations  to  applicants  and
employees who need them because of a disability or to  practice  or  observe
their religion, absent undue hardship.

As  RTEC's  Executive  Director,  I  maintain  overall  responsibility   and
