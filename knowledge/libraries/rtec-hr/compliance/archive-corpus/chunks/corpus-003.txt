The  Cumberland  Valley  Area  Development  District  (CVADD)  conducted   a
Demographic Analysis in 2000.  CVADD is composed of  eight  counties:  Bell,
Clay, Harlan, Jackson, Knox, Laurel,  Rockcastle,  and  Whitley.   In  2013,
CVADD comprised 5.42% of the state’s population.  There are 993  individuals
with a Limited English Proficiency in the CVADD area.   The  highest  amount
was Laurel County with 392 individuals with Limited English Proficiency.
Lake  Cumberland  Area  Development  District   (LCADD)   also   looked   at
Demographic Analysis for year 2000.  LCADD  is  composed  of  ten  counties:
Adair,  Casey,  Clinton,  Cumberland,  Green,  McCreary,  Pulaski,  Russell,
Taylor,  and  Wayne.   In  2000,  LCADD  comprised  4.79%  of  the   state's
population.

Data shows that the population is made up of 98% White  and  1.8%  Black  or
African American.  A major demographic change in  race  statistics  for  the
LCADD has been the Hispanic population.   The  Hispanic  population  tripled
for the LCADD counties between 1990 and 2000.  Counties such  as  Wayne  and
Clinton have seen  a  10  fold  or  greater  increase  during  this  period.
According to data provided by the LCADD,  these  massive  increases  in  the
Hispanic population reflect the struggling Mexican economy and  the  current
“hot button” topic of  illegal  immigration.   LCADD  noted  that  the  2000
Census tables actually show the number of Hispanics decreasing in Green  and
Pulaski Counties.  It is still unclear if these figures reflect  reality  or
data collection problems.

According to the Kentucky State Data  Center  Research  Report  79%  of  the
state  Hispanic  growth  occurred  in  metro  areas.   In  2010,   Hispanics
comprised 4.0 percent of the state  metro  population,  but  less  than  two
percent of micro and rural populations.

(2) The frequency with which LEP individuals come in contact with the
program:

The numbers are clearly showing an increase in the number  of  foreign  born
residents in Kentucky as well as  an  increase  in  the  number  of  Spanish
Speaking residents.  RTEC will continue to monitor changes  in  demographics
as they become available through Census and Studies.  RTEC  will  also  work
closely with Local Area Development  Districts  for  updated  and  available
information.

RTEC will monitor the frequency with which LEP individuals come  in  contact
with the program and services.  RTEC will report  all  encounters  with  LEP
persons on the OTD Section 5311 Monthly  Summary  Statistical  Report.   The
methods utilized for  this  assessment  include  analysis  of  Census  data,
examining phone inquiries, requests for translated documents.   Census  data
indicates that Hispanic or Latino is the most prominent LEP group in  RTEC's
service area.  On average RTEC receives approximately 28 Hispanic or  Latino
calls a month that require a translator.  Reporting  accuracy  will  improve
with ongoing training and tracking measures implemented.   From  January  1,
2020 to December 31, 2022 RTEC reported  980  Language  Assistance  requests
from LEP individuals and no requests for translated documents.

RTEC currently transports approximately 11 Japanese students every  Saturday
from Somerset  to  Lexington  to  attend  school.   There  are  no  language
assistance requests made at this time.


RTEC Language Assistance Measures




(3) The Nature and Importance of the Program, Activity, or Service Provided
by the Program:

Rural Transit Enterprises Coordinated, Inc. d/b/a  RTEC  was  organized  for
the sole purpose of coordinating and operating public transportation.   RTEC
understands they play an important role in the lives of our community.   The
individuals riding our transportation service main function is the need  for
mobility for a continued quality of life.  A LEP person’s inability  to  use
public transit may hinder their ability to access health care, education  or
employment.  RTEC will continue to outreach to all individuals  and  promote
their efforts.  This will increase our need  to  promote  our  services  for
individuals to realize the service is available.

RTEC operates lift-equipped  vehicles  for  wheelchair  users  and  provides
affordable  and   efficient   community   transportation   services.    RTEC
constantly works toward accessing new equipment to upgrade its fleet and  to
increase services.  These services are available to EVERYONE in the  general
population of the service area and are likely to be sought by  LEP  persons.


RTEC is taking steps in order to increase outreach efforts to  LEP  language
groups. For example, RTEC has incorporated Spanish verbiage on  the  website
and in all their printed material (brochures, schedules, advertisements  and
flyers) as well as Spanish translation portions on the telephone message.

4) The Resources Available to the Recipient and Costs:

How RTEC provides Spanish language assistance:
    • Oral language services include Spanish option on telephone.
    • Bilingual Customer Service Representatives. Spanish  speaking  callers
      will be directed to a Bilingual  Customer  Service  Representative  to
      make  reservations  or  answer  questions   regarding   transportation
      services.
    • In addition to these oral language services, several written  language
      services are available.  Any written correspondence will be  forwarded
      to a translator who  can  translate  the  document  into  English  and
      translate RTEC’s response into Spanish.


How RTEC provides notice to LEP persons about the availability  of  language
assistance:
    • Spanish-language advertising is  also  used  to  promote  and  educate
      clients on how to schedule  transportation  with  RTEC  (See  attached
      Spanish RTEC Brochure).
    • RTEC website being available in Spanish.
    • Instructions to vehicle operators.  Vehicle operators  are  likely  to
      encounter LEP persons.  They have several methods to respond to an LEP
      individual. Some vehicle operators may be bilingual; however,  if  the
      vehicle operator is not bilingual, they can contact dispatch for radio
      assistance with a Spanish-speaking passenger or provide  RTEC’s  phone
      number to ask for a bilingual CSR or ask if another passenger  on  the
      vehicle could serve as a translator.
    • Attend community meetings  and  events  to  inform  people  of  RTEC’s
      service in general and that language assistance is available.


Currently, Matthew Getsi (Network Provider Manager), Jesus Noyola  (Driver),
Paloma Quintero (Driver), Maria Sanchez (Driver),  Nancy  Gonzalez  Laureano
(Driver), and Anne Weber (Mobility Manager) are  RTEC’s  bilingual  Customer
Service Representatives.  Other language barriers may be resolved  by  using
websites such as www.freetranslation.com,  http://translate.google.com,  and
http://www.bing.com/translator for assistance  in  translation.   RTEC  will
acquire other resources or contract any additional help as necessary.

These additional language assistance tools will be utilized  to  assist  LEP
individuals at no additional cost.  The notice of  “language  assistance  at
no cost” has been added to our advertisements and website.


Staff Training


Part of RTEC’s LEP plan is deciding what level of staff training is  needed.
 It is important that staff members, especially those  having  contact  with
the  public,  know  their  obligation  to  provide  meaningful   access   to
information and services for LEP persons. Even  staff  members  who  do  not
interact regularly with LEP persons should be aware of  and  understand  the
LEP plan. Properly  training  staff  is  a  key  element  in  the  effective
implementation of the LEP plan.

Customer  Service  Representatives  and  Operations  staff  is  trained   to
recognize when an LEP person is in need of language assistance  and  respond
by referring the LEP person to one  of  RTEC’s  bilingual  Customer  Service
Representatives.    This  awareness  training  is  included   in   CSR   and
Operations Staff Orientation and Initial On-The-Job Training.   Regular  re-
training for staff is also provided.

RTEC is looking  into  additional  training  steps  to  provide  some  basic
Spanish speaking skills to all staff and drivers.  Some video  training  may
assist with this endeavor.
