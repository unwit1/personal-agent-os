RTEC PROCEDURES FOR TRACKING AND INVESTIGATING TITLE VII COMPLAINTS

Title  VII  of  the  Civil  Rights  Act  of  1964,  as  amended,   prohibits
discrimination on the basis of race, color, or national origin  in  programs
and services funded, in whole or part,  by  financial  assistance  from  the
United States Government.

All services and  programs  operated  or  sponsored  by  the  Rural  Transit
Enterprises Coordinated, Inc. (RTEC)  located  at  100  Main  Street,  Mount
Vernon, KY 40456 are subject to the requirements and  obligations  of  Title
VII.  It is the intention of RTEC to fully comply with Title VII.

Persons who believe that they have  experienced  or  witnessed  any  act  or
inaction, intentional or otherwise, in any  program,  service,  or  activity
operated by or sponsored by RTEC that results in or may result in  disparate
treatment or impact, or perpetuates the effects of prior  discrimination  of
the basis of race, color, or national origin may file  a  written  complaint
with RTEC or the U.S. Federal Transit Administration.

Complaints filed  directly  with  Federal  Transit  Administration  must  be
mailed within 180 days of any alleged discrimination. Complaints  should  be
mailed to:

            Federal Transit Administration Office of Civil Rights
            Attention Title VI Program Coordinator
            East Building, 5th. Floor – TCR
            1200 New Jersey Ave., SE
            Washington, D. C,  20590

It shall be the responsibility of the Executive Director, or  her  designee,
to track, investigate and  document  Title  VII  complaints.  If  complaints
cannot be resolved by RTEC, the Kentucky Office of  Transportation  Delivery
will be notified for assistance in reaching a resolution.

RTEC has one active investigation still open for  this  fiscal  year.   Case
was settled on July 26, 2011.


HOW TO FILE A TITLE VII COMPLAINT TO RTEC

A person with a Title VII or other complaint may also submit  the  complaint
to RTEC using the following procedures:

1.  A complaint may be submitted in writing and must  include  the  person’s
name and contact information, the date of the incidence,  and  the  identity
of  the  person  or  department  or  service  that  caused  the   complaint.
Complaints may be sent via mail, email, fax, or hand delivered.

2.  A complaint may be taken verbally and must  include  the  person’s  name
and contact information, the date of the incidence, and the identity of  the
person, department or service that caused the complaint.

3.  Persons with a complaint may request a neutral third  party  to  hear  a
verbal complaint or assist with a written complaint.   The selection of  the
neutral third party shall be made cooperatively between RTEC and the  person
filing the complaint.

4.  All complaints shall  be  addressed  to  the  RTEC  Executive  Director,
Shirley Cummins.

RTEC COMPLAINT PROCEDURE


1.  The person filing a complaint on the basis of  discrimination  based  on
race, color, or national origin will be informed that the complaint  may  be
either filed directly with the FTA or with RTEC.   The  contact  information
for the FTA will be provided should the person opt  to  file  directly  with
FTA.   RTEC’s Executive Director shall be  responsible  for  follow  up  and
monitoring FTA action.


2.  If the person opts to file the complaint with RTEC, the  complaint  will
be directed by the Executive Director to the appropriate department  manager
for a fact-finding review. The manager will prepare a  written  response  to
the complaint and submit it to the RTEC Executive Director.

3.  If the  complaint  is  valid  and  supported  by  facts,  the  Executive
Director will order corrective action be taken.

4. The person who filed the complaint will be consulted as to  the  adequacy
of the proposed remedy.   If acceptable, the matter is concluded.

5.  If the proposed remedy is not  acceptable,  the  person  who  filed  the
complaint may request a  hearing  with  the  RTEC  Personnel  Committee  for
purposes of stating their complaint and identifying an appropriate remedy.


6.  The Finance and Personnel Committee will issue a response and  recommend
a remedy within ten days of the hearing.

7.  If acceptable, the matter is concluded.  If not, the person  is  advised
of the appropriate steps to  file  the  complaint  with  the  Department  of
Transportation Delivery and FTA.

SEXUAL HARASSMENT POLICY STATEMENT

RTEC promotes an environment in which all can work, learn  and  live  in  an
atmosphere free from all forms of harassment and  discrimination.   RTEC  is
an equal opportunity employer.

Specifically, sexual harassment is a type of discrimination that is  illegal
under federal and state law and  RTEC policy.  It is  defined  as  unwelcome
conduct of a sexual nature that is so severe or pervasive that is creates  a
hostile environment for a reasonable person.   A  determination  of  whether
particular conduct constitutes sexual harassment depends on the totality  of
the  circumstances.   Sexual  harassment  may  occur  between   persons   of
differing power or between peers.  It may also involve a female harassing  a
male or a male or female harassing a person of the same gender.

RTEC is committed to creating and maintaining an environment and service  in
which all persons who participates  in  RTEC  programs  and  activities  can
work, learn and live together in  an  atmosphere  free  from  all  forms  of
harassment,  exploitation,  or  intimidation.   Sexual   harassment   is   a
violation of the law and RTEC policy.   We  are  required  by  law  to  take
proactive  measures  to  prevent  sexual  harassment  and  to  promptly  and
effectively resolve complaints when they occur.

All members of the RTEC employees  are  subject  to  the  Sexual  Harassment
Policy.  We are all responsible for ensuring an  environment  that  is  free
from all forms of harassment, exploitation or intimidation.

Employees who are sexually harassed are encouraged to discuss the  situation
with their Supervisor, EEO Manager, and Human Resource Manager or  with  the
Executive Director of the firm.


RTEC’S REASONABLE ACCOMMODATION POLICY

RTEC commits to making a reasonable accommodation to the known physical  and
mental limitations of qualified individuals with disabilities and  qualified
disabled veterans, unless such accommodation would impose an undue  hardship
on  the  conduct  of  its  business.   In  determining  the  extent  of  its
obligations, RTEC will consider business necessity and financial  costs  and
expenses, among other factors.

RTEC incorporates the following language in all job  vacancy  announcements;
“This  agency  provides  reasonable   accommodation   to   applicants   with
disabilities where appropriate.  Determinations on requests  for  reasonable
accommodation will be made on a case-by-case basis.”

In  accordance  with  Executive  Order  13164  RTEC  has  designated  Sheila
Stallsworth,  EEO  Administrative  Manager  as  a  contact  for   reasonable
accommodation requests. For assistance call 606-256-9835, Extension  223  or
toll-free at 1-800-321-7832, or TDD at 1-800-648-6056, or hearing and  voice
impaired 1-800-648-6057

No reasonable accommodation requests were made during this AAP year.


RTEC’S LIMITED ENGLISH PROFICIENT PLAN

RTEC commits to making a reasonable accommodation  to  any  Limited  English
Proficient employee or applicant.  The inability  to  speak  and  understand
the English language may potentially hinder  some  individuals.   Therefore,
RTEC has developed  a  Limited  English  Proficient  Plan  to  address  this
language barrier issue.

Part of RTEC’s LEP plan is deciding what level of staff training is  needed.
 It is important that staff members, especially those  having  contact  with
the  public,  know  their  obligation  to  provide  meaningful   access   to
information and services for LEP persons.  Even staff  members  who  do  not
interact regularly with LEP persons should be aware of  and  understand  the
LEP plan.  Properly training  staff  is  a  key  element  in  the  effective
implementation of the LEP plan.

This plan is designed to be flexible, and should be  viewed  as  a  work  in
progress.  As such, it is important to consider whether  new  documents  and
services need to be made accessible for LEP persons,  and  also  to  monitor
changes in demographics and types of services, and to update  the  LEP  plan
when appropriate.

Bilingual services for applicants, employees,  clients  are  available  upon
request.

A copy of the entire Limited English Proficient Plan is available.

INTERNAL AUDIT AND REPORTING SYSTEMS

The EEO Manager has the responsibility  for  developing  and  preparing  the
formal documents of the  AAP.   The  EEO  Manager  is  responsible  for  the
effective implementation of the AAP;  however,  responsibility  is  likewise
vested with each  department  manager  and  supervisor.   RTEC’s  audit  and
reporting system is designed to:

    • Measure the effectiveness of the AAP/EEO program;

    • Document personnel activities

    • Identify problem areas where remedial action is needed; and

    • Determine the degree to which RTEC’s goals and  objectives  have  been
      obtained.

The following activities are reviewed at least annually  to  ensure  freedom
from stereotyping qualified  individuals  with  disabilities  and  qualified
protected veterans in any manner,  including  that  which  may  limit  their
access to any job for which they are qualified:

    • Recruitment, selection, advertising, and job application procedures;

    • Hiring, promotion, transfers, layoffs, termination;

    • Rates of pay and any other  forms  of  compensation  including  fringe
      benefits;

    • Job assignments, job classifications, job descriptions, and  seniority
      lists;

    • Sick leave, leaves of absence, or any other leave;

    • Training, apprenticeships, attendance  at  professional  meetings  and
      conferences; and

    • Any other term, condition, or privilege of employment.


Managers and supervisors are asked to report any current or foreseeable  EEO
problem areas and are asked  to  outline  their  suggestions/recommendations
for solutions.  If problem areas arise, the  manager  or  supervisor  is  to
report problem areas immediately to the EEO Manager.   The EEO Manager  will
discuss any problems relating to significant rejection ratios, EEO  charges,
etc., with the Executive Director  and  the  EEO  Manager  will  report  the
status of RTEC’s goals and objectives to the Executive  Director.   The  EEO
Manager will recommend remedial actions for the effective implementation  of
the AAP.

ESTABLISHING AFFIRMATIVE ACTION GOALS


The long-range goal of RTEC’s Affirmative Action Program  is  to  achieve  a
RTEC workforce  at  all  job  levels  in  which  minorities  and  women  are
represented in proportion to their representation in the  “applicable  labor
market”.  RTEC identifies its  “applicable  labor  market”  as  those  areas
which include: Bell, Knox, Whitley, Clinton,  Cumberland,  Monroe,  Russell,
McCreary, Laurel, Rockcastle, Pulaski, and Wayne counties.

To address this long-range goal, RTEC will continue to advocate  the  hiring
and utilization of minorities and women at rates reasonably proportional  to
their corresponding rates of availability in the “applicable labor market”.

RTEC will also make every good faith effort to:

   1. Promote equal opportunity and achieve a  workplace  that  is  free  of
      discrimination.


   2.  Ensure  opportunities  for  all   qualified   applicants,   including
      underutilized groups.

   3. Hire the most qualified applicant using a fair  and  nondiscriminatory
      process.

   4. Make career development and  promotional  opportunities  available  to
      interested and qualified employees, including minorities and women.

   5. To ensure fairness and nondiscrimination in the termination process.




                            EMPLOYMENT PRACTICES


With each job opening, the request is forwarded to Human Resources to  start
the hiring process:

   1. Approval is obtained from the CEO to advertise and interview.
   2. Ad is placed in the local newspaper; with special attention  is  given
      to EEO, Minority encouraged to apply noted on Ad.
   3. Copy of the ad is sent to Office of Employment &  Training  (OET)  for
      that location, along with the application. Drivers  applications  also
      require:


         o Driving Record Release authorization
         o Authorization for criminal background check
         o Information release form
         o Copy of RTEC’s Drug and Alcohol Policy
         o Job Description
         o Voluntary Affirmative Action Data Record


   4. OET also advertises the position and selects candidates most qualified
      and forwards to RTEC.
   5. Applicant is scheduled for interview.  Significant items considered on
      Application: ex. Drivers:


         o Experience in driving, handicap, wheelchair, CDL
         o GED Minimum Education
         o Turnover rate for previous employment
         o Pay rate requested
         o Ability to perform the job as listed on the job description with
           or without reasonable accommodation
         o Application is complete and is signed
         o Score on pre-employment applicant driver test


To begin the interview, we provide  some  background  on  the  Company,  the
position, hours, pay rate,  some  policy,  and  review  of  Benefits.   Upon
completion of the interview, selected candidates application is sent to  our
Safety/Training/Accident/Drug/Alcohol   Program   Manager   for   background
checks.

RTEC currently has the following  pre-employment  screenings  in  place  for
safety sensitive positions:

         o Driving Record Check
         o Background Record Check
         o D.O.T. Physical (USDOT 49CFR391.41)
         o Pre-employment drug test (USDOT 49CFR part 40 as amended)

                      MOVING VS. NON-MOVING VIOLATIONS


Violations are also classified by whether or not the vehicle was  in  motion
at the time of
the incident and traffic tickets may therefore be for either moving or  non-
moving
violations.

Examples of moving violations include:

Speeding or driving below the minimum speed
Running a stop sign or red light
Driving without a seat belt
Drunk driving (DUI and DWI)


Non-Moving violations include:

Parking in a handicapped zone or other illegal parking
Driving with an invalid vehicle registration
Having expired or missing license plates
Leaving a vehicle unattended and running

The  majority  of  traffic  violations  in  Kentucky   are   classified   as
infractions, which require paying a fine up to $500.  More serious  offenses
carry higher fines and/or imprisonment.

EQUAL EMPLOYMENT OPPORTUNITY/AFFIRMATIVE ACTION

POLICY STATEMENT

INTRODUCTION

The Affirmative Action Plan for Rural Transit Enterprises Coordinated,  Inc.
(hereinafter referred to as  RTEC)  reflects  sincere  commitment  to  Equal
Employment Opportunity for all  employees  and  applicants  for  employment.
RTEC sets forth this policy in order for all to become aware of  the  policy
and practices of nondiscrimination within  this  company.  Equal  Employment
Opportunity and opportunities for processional growth will be  available  to
all employees regardless of race, sex, age  (forty  or  above),  disability,
national origin, religion, or veteran status. According  to  RTEC's  policy,
no employee or applicant for employment will be  subject  to  harassment  or
discrimination because  of  race,  color,  national  origin,  religion,  age
(forty or above), disability, sex, or veteran status.

RTEC's policy will recruit, employ, train and promote  persons  in  all  job
categories without  regard  to  race,  color,  religion,  sex,  or  national
origin. No employee  or  applicant  for  employment  who  has  the  required
qualifications to perform the job will be discriminated  against  by  reason
of physical or mental disability or because he/she is a disabled veteran  or
veteran of the Vietnam Era. Additionally,  it  is  the  policy  of  RTEC  to
comply with the provisions of the Age Discrimination in  Employment  Act  of
1967, as amended and applicable regulations.  This  policy  applies  to  all
personnel  actions,  including  but  not  limited  to  recruiting,   hiring,
classification/compensation,  benefits,  promotions,   transfers,   layoffs,
reinstatement, and educational programs.  We  expect  full  cooperation  and
assistance from  all  employees  involved  in  the  hiring,  developing  and
promoting of personnel to achieve a diverse workforce.


ASSURANCES

RTEC has adopted the Affirmative Action Plan for Region 5  &  12  Brokerages
and public transportation and shall use it to serve as a guide  designed  to
initiate  and  maximize  motivation   and   employment   opportunities   for
minorities, women, disabled individuals and  the  poor.  This  policy  shall
further serve to provide for Equal  Opportunity/Affirmative  Action  in  all
phases of daily action, inclusive of  any  and  all  employees  or  affected
individuals, participants, or beneficiaries.  RTEC  pledges  assistance  and
guidance to positively affect  Equal  Opportunity/Affirmative  Action  needs
within the community.

RTEC abides by all  applicable  Equal  Opportunity  laws,  regulations,  and
policies as set forth to insure nondiscrimination as  an  employer,  service
provider, and administering agency for delegated programs,  projects  and/or
activities. Specific laws and regulations shall govern all actions  of  this
Company. As RTEC has established its  Equal  Opportunity/Affirmative  Action
Policy, Plan and Program  in  accordance  with  the  intent  of  the  rules,
regulations, and guidelines of the various federal, state,  and  local  laws
an agencies having administrative authority in the Equal  Opportunity  area,
RTEC provides assurance of compliance with either human rights  policies  or
directives as may be created and implemented  during  future  periods.  This
Company establishes and implements an Affirmative Action  Plan  and  Program
to insure that positive action steps  are  taken  to  assist  the  Company's
internal operational system as well as any entities outside the Company.


ADMINISTRATION

Board of Directors

The Board of Directors shall be  responsible  for  the  approval  of  RTEC's
policies, plans and priorities. The Board shall also  approve  policies  and
procedures for the Company subject to grant guidelines.

The  Board  of  Directors  will  be  involved  and  advised  of  the   Equal
Opportunity/Affirmative  Action  Policy,  Plan   and   Program   to   insure
compliance with the applicable laws, regulations, and grant requirements.

The   Board   of   Directors   shall   approve   goals   for    the    Equal
Opportunity/Affirmative  Action  Plan  and  shall  approve   the   planning,
implementation, conduct and evaluation of the Equal  Opportunity/Affirmative
Action Policy, Plan, and Program performance.

The Board of Directors shall have the  authority  to  insure  and  guarantee
implementation in every  phase  of  employment  and  service.  The  policies
invoked by the Board of  Directors  shall  be  binding  for  all  staff  and
applicants for employment.

Executive Director/CEO - Shirley L. Cummins

The Executive Director shall have the responsibility for insuring the  Equal
Opportunity/Affirmative Action Policy  as  outlined  has  been  followed  to
assure compliance with all regulations.

The Executive Director shall appoint the   EEO  Administrative  Manager  and
shall establish appropriate duties, responsibilities and accountability.

The Executive Director and the   Equal  Opportunity  Administrative  Manager
shall strive to insure that every form of prejudice or discrimination  based
on race, color, creed, sex, national  origin,  age,  political  affiliation,
belief or disability is eradicated.


 EEO Administrative Manager –  Sheila Stallsworth


The EEO Administrative Manager implements activities relative to  the  Equal
Opportunity/Affirmative Action Policy, Plan and Program,  as  determined  by
the Executive Director and the Board  of  Directors.  Duties  of  the  Human
Resource, EEO Administrative Manager shall include, but not limited to,  the
following:

Provides assurance that the Equal Opportunity  Policy,  Personnel  Policies,
and  Affirmative  Action  Plan  and  Program  are  implemented  per  current
regulations.

Maintains   materials   and   working    knowledge    on    current    Equal
Opportunity/Affirmative  Action   laws,   implementation   procedures,   and
compliance.

Provides assistance and guidance in human  rights  areas  to  the  Board  of
Directors, Executive Director and other management.

Establishes and maintains a system for analyzing, evaluation, and  reporting
data as it relates to equal opportunity employment, and as mandated by law.

Collects and submits information and reports to the Board of  Directors  and
Executive Director as requested and or as needed.

Receives and assists in resolving complaints of discrimination and  provides
aggrieved persons with information and advice on grievance procedure.

Participates in  all  phases  of  the  personnel  process  to  insure  equal
treatment of all applicants and employees.

Analyzes skills and abilities of all staff, determines  training  needs  and
provides counseling to employees as needed.
