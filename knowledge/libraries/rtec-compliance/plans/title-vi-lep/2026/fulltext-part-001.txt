                       Title VI "STATEMENT OF POLICY"



Rural Transit Enterprises Coordinated, Inc. (RTEC) is committed to a  policy
of non-discrimination in the conduct of its business,  including  its  Title
VI responsibilities - delivery of equitable  and  accessible  transportation
services.  RTEC recognizes its responsibilities to the communities in  which
it operates and to the society it serves.  It is RTEC's  policy  to  utilize
its best efforts to assure their "Statement of Policy" is accomplished.

RTEC as a federal grant  recipient,  is  required  by  the  Federal  Transit
Administration (FTA) to conform to Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of  1964
and its amendments.  Section 601 of Title VI of  the  Civil  Rights  Act  of
1964 requires that "No person in the United States, on the grounds of  race,
color or national origin be excluded from participation in,  be  denied  the
benefits of, or  be  subjected  to  discrimination,  under  any  program  or
activity receiving Federal  financial  assistance.   Presidential  Executive
Order 12898 addresses  environmental  justice  in  minority  and  low-income
populations.  Presidential  Executive  Order  13166  addresses  services  to
those individuals with limited English proficiency.

RTEC is committed to enforcing the provisions of  Title  VI  and  protecting
the rights and opportunities of all  persons  associated  with  the  agency.
RTEC's commitment includes vigorously  enforcing  all  applicable  laws  and
regulations that affect the County and those organizations, both public  and
private, which participate and benefit through our programs.

RTEC will take positive and realistic affirmative steps to ensure  that  all
persons and/or firms wishing to participate in  its  program  are  given  an
equal and equitable chance to participate.

RTEC's   sub-recipients   and   contractors   are   required   to    prevent
discrimination and  ensure  nondiscrimination  in  all  of  their  programs,
activities and services.

RTEC is responsible  for  providing  leadership,  direction  and  policy  to
ensure compliance with Title VI of the 1964 Civil Rights Act in  respect  to
its  transit  services.   Any  person(s)  who  feels  that  they  have  been
discriminated against is encourage to report such violations in  writing  to
the Title VI Coordinator.

RTEC'S objective to:

   A. Ensure that the level and quality of public transportation service  is
      provided in a nondiscriminatory manner
   B. Promote full and fair participation in public transportation decision-
      making without regard to race, color, or national origin;
   C. Ensure meaningful access to programs and activities  by  persons  with
      limited English proficiency.
   D. Identify and address,  as  appropriate,  disproportionately  high  and
      adverse human health and environmental effects, including  social  and
      economic effects of programs and activities  on  minority  populations
      and low-income populations;
   E. Prevent the  denial,  reduction,  or  delay  in  benefits  related  to
      programs and activities that  benefit  minority  populations  or  low-
      income  populations  and  to  notify  beneficiaries   of   protections
      available under Title VI;
   F. Adhere to the Title VI reporting requirements of the  Federal  Transit
      Administration through the annual Certification and  Assurance  module
      in TEAM and provide written Title VI policy documentation every  three
      years as required in as required in FTA circular 4702.1A.
   G. Provide public notification of Title VI protections in all  buses  and
      public places where RTEC conducts business or operations.

The responsibility for carrying out RTEC's commitment to  this  program  has
been delegated to Clarissa Hodge, a Title VI Compliance  Officer,  who  will
receive  and  investigate  Title  VI  complaints  which  come  through   the
complaint procedure.   However,  all  managers,  supervisors  and  employees
share in the responsibility for making RTEC's Title VI  Program  a  success.
Additional information  concerning  RTEC's  obligations  and  the  complaint
procedure can be found  on  the  RTEC's  web  site  www.ridertec.org  or  by
calling 800-321-7832.

Introduction

Rural Transit Enterprises Coordinated, Inc. d/b/a RTEC recognizes  the  need
to provide equal service opportunities for all individuals  in  the  service
area.  Most  individuals  in  the  United  States  read,  write,  speak  and
understand English.  However, the inability  to  speak  and  understand  the
English language may potentially  hinder  some  individuals  from  arranging
transportation services needed.  Therefore, RTEC  has  developed  a  Limited
English Proficient Plan to address this language barrier issue.

There are two pieces of legislation that  provide  the  foundation  for  the
development of an LEP plan: Title VI of the Civil Rights Act  of  1964,  and
Executive Order 13166. In some circumstances, failure  to  ensure  that  LEP
persons can effectively  participate  in  federally  assisted  programs  may
constitute discrimination based on national origin under Title VI. In  order
to comply with  Title  VI,  agencies  should  take  reasonable  actions  for
competent language assistance. Executive Order 13166 clarifies  requirements
for LEP persons under Title VI. The Executive Order requires the  agency  to
examine the services it provides and
develop and implement a system by which LEP persons can meaningfully  access
those services.

The U. S. Department of Transportation  (DOT)  issued  its  Policy  Guidance
Concerning Recipient's Responsibilities to Limited English Proficient  (LEP)
Persons [Federal Register: December 14, 2005 (Volume 70, Number 239)].  This
policy states that DOT recipients are required to take reasonable  steps  to
ensure meaningful access to programs by LEP persons. This  coverage  extends
to the recipient's entire program. There are four factors  for  agencies  to
consider when assessing language  needs  and  determining  what  steps  they
should take to ensure access for LEP persons: 1) The  number  or  proportion
of LEP persons eligible to be served  or  likely  to  be  encountered  by  a
program, activity or service of the recipient; 2) The frequency  with  which
LEP individuals come  in  contact  with  the  program;  3)  The  nature  and
importance of the program, activity or service provided by the recipient  to
people's lives; and 4) The resources available to the recipient and costs.

Who is a Limited English Proficient Person

Persons who do not speak English as their primary language and  who  have  a
limited ability to read, speak, write or understand English can  be  limited
English proficient, or "LEP." These individuals may be entitled to  language
assistance with respect  to  a  particular  type  of  service,  benefit,  or
encounter.

Demographics by county:    RTEC  examined  the  2024  US  Census  QuickFacts
report by state and county for each of our service areas  and  was  able  to
determine that approximately 2.12% or 6,749 people within our  service  area
age 5 and older spoke a language other than English.  See chart below.   For
the RTEC service area people of the  Hispanic  or  Latino  descent  are  the
primary  LEP  persons  likely  to  utilize  RTEC  services.   Of  the  8,631
residents Hispanic or Latino 5 years and over by ability  to  speak  English
in the RTEC service area 1,114 residents  describe  themselves  as  speaking
English "not well" or  "not  at  all".   For  the  RTEC  service  area,  the
American Community Survey of the U.S. Census Bureau  shows  that  among  the
area's population 27.9% speak  English  "well"  and  "very  well".   No  one
identified as speaking another language other than Hispanic or Latino.

Kentucky Demographics:  According to a study by a group called  CensusScope,
the 2000 Census indicated that Kentucky was ranked 43 out of 50  by  percent
of population  Age  5+  Spanish  Speaking.   The  total  number  of  Spanish
Speaking in Kentucky was 219,500 out of a total population of 4,505,836  Age
5+ as of the 2020  Decennial  Census.  According to  these  statistics  and
information provided by CensusScope, 1.86% of Kentucky Residents Age  5+  is
Spanish Speaking.

The  Cumberland  Valley  Area  Development  District  (CVADD)  conducted   a
Demographic Analysis in 2000.  CVADD is composed of  eight  counties:  Bell,
Clay, Harlan, Jackson, Knox, Laurel, Rockcastle, and  Whitley.   Based  upon
ACS 2023 data, CVADD comprised 5.1% of the state's  population.   There  are
834 individuals with a Limited English Proficiency in the CVADD  area.   The
highest amount was Laurel County with 364 individuals with  Limited  English
Proficiency.

Lake  Cumberland  Area  Development  District   (LCADD)   also   looked   at
Demographic Analysis for year 2000.  LCADD  is  composed  of  ten  counties:
Adair,  Casey,  Clinton,  Cumberland,  Green,  McCreary,  Pulaski,  Russell,
Taylor,  and  Wayne.   In  2000,  LCADD  comprised  4.79%  of  the   state's
population. Data shows that the population is made up of 98% White and  1.8%
Black or African American.  A major demographic change  in  race  statistics
for the LCADD has been the Hispanic  population.   The  Hispanic  population
tripled for the LCADD counties between 1990  and  2000.   Counties  such  as
Wayne and Clinton have seen a  10  fold  or  greater  increase  during  this
period.  According to data provided by the LCADD,  these  massive  increases
in the Hispanic population reflect the struggling Mexican  economy  and  the
current "hot button" topic of illegal immigration.   LCADD  noted  that  the
2000 Census tables actually show  the  number  of  Hispanics  decreasing  in
Green and Pulaski Counties.  It is still unclear if  these  figures  reflect
reality or data collection problems.

According to the Kentucky  Annual  Economic  Report  2025  published  by  UK
Gatton College  of  Business  and  Economics,  "Kentucky's  racial  minority
population is concentrated  in  the  state's  metropolitan  areas;  in  2023
(based on the U.S. Census 5-year  estimate),  four  of  every  five  (81.2%)
racial minorities in Kentucky lived in metropolitan areas;  only  7  percent
in Kentucky's 60 mostly rural counties."
