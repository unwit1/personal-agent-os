# Semantic pass 13 — batch 03: Gilded Mine cover story and lure

This batch covers Falk Eransen (7 exact-Subject INFO), the captive Bandit (7 exact-Subject INFO), and one already-ingested Aslfur/Thaer mine-survey note.

## Public cover

Falk Eransen identifies himself as the owner of the Gilded Mine and repeatedly insists that the mine is closed (INFO 053A446E, 053A4472).

When confronted with a report that the mine is exporting ore, he denies it rather than explaining the discrepancy (053A4475).

He will still hire an outsider for work away from the mine, particularly Falmer/Chaurus killing and recovery of Dwemer material. This lets the operation interact with outsiders while discouraging entry into the mine itself.

## Independent reports of renewed exports

Thaer's annotated mine survey (BOOK 056E00CC) says the Gilded Mine was a corundum mine that went dry and then suddenly began exporting gold ore. The miners became reclusive.

A captive bandit independently says his boss received information that the mine was exporting gold and sent a group to investigate (INFO 0572C7CD).

The two reports converge on the renewed-gold-export story without depending on Falk.

## The apparent lure

The captive says:
- the front door had no lock;
- the gate leading to the ore chute was broken;
- the intruders initially interpreted this as excellent luck;
- after entry, their exit was blocked;
- he concludes that the gold and easy access were a trap.

His companions were then separated. He says Harral was taken toward the surface and may have been cut apart at the blood altar, while Salise was taken deeper toward the "farm" (0572C7CC).

He also reports a makeshift temple, a strange-language book, sacrificial altar, altered faces, and roaring from deeper in the mine (0572C7CE).

## Evidence boundary

The following are strongly supported:
- Falk publicly claims the mine is closed;
- multiple independent sources report renewed gold exports;
- a bandit infiltration team entered through unusually easy access and was captured;
- the Brethren operate captive-labor/sacrifice systems inside the mine.

The proposition that the mine deliberately advertises gold and leaves access open specifically to lure victims is the captive's reconstruction. It is plausible and consistent with the Brethren's need for captives, but should remain an inferred operational tactic rather than a directly admitted policy.
