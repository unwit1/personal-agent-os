# Semantic lore analysis — pass 08: contradictions, competing explanations, and unresolved identities

This pass adds an explicit conflict layer so Lorekeeper can preserve incompatible testimony without choosing a winner simply because one source sounds more authoritative.

## Conflict taxonomy

- **direct_doctrinal_contradiction** — two sources make incompatible claims about the same proposition.
- **perspective_conflict** — sources disagree about motive, legitimacy, or institutional character without necessarily contradicting concrete events.
- **official_record_vs_occult_reconstruction** — mundane documentation conflicts with later evidence suggesting concealment or incomplete reporting.
- **temporal_reversal** — the same character changes position over time; this is chronology, not simultaneous inconsistency.
- **partial_metaphysical_conflict** — sources use incompatible soul/divinity models, but the mod does not fully adjudicate the underlying metaphysics.
- **unresolved_identity** — multiple clues point toward an identity, but no source directly establishes it.

## Direct conflict: dragons as gods vs tyrants

Hrotggi Asgikverssen calls the dragons the first true Atmoran totem-animals and the rightful god-kings of mankind. He says their restored cult exists to prepare worship for their return.

The anti-dragon gem collective says the exact opposite: dragons are "no gods", merely powerful animals, and the Dragon Cult exists primarily to create slavery and hierarchy.

Both sides agree that the dragons are returning. Their disagreement is therefore not about the expected event but about ontology and legitimacy.

Resolution: unresolved in-universe. Both are partisan sources, while the gem route has extensive mechanical support for its own life-force system but not for its judgment that dragons are merely animals.

## Direct classification conflict: Meridia / Merid-Nunda

The in-world reference work *The Daedric Princes and Their Servants* places Meridia inside its Daedric-Prince taxonomy.

Grystar explicitly rejects the player's phrase "Daedric Prince Meridia" and calls Merid-Nunda a Magna-Ge who fled during the Dawn. He concedes that "Daedra" can broadly mean "not an Aedra", but argues that collapsing Merid-Nunda into the ordinary Daedric-Prince category is ignorant.

Resolution: unresolved taxonomy. The disagreement is narrower than "Daedra vs not-Daedra": it concerns whether Merid-Nunda belongs to the same ontological class as conventional Princes.

## Partial metaphysical conflict: Arkay and mortal souls

The Vigilant treatise says mortal souls are divine sparks gifted by the Aedra, rightfully reclaimed by Arkay at death, and reborn into later generations. Daedric soul-taking is presented as theft from this natural cycle.

The Gatekeepers call Arkay a dead god who offers only death, then treat souls as manipulable animus that can be trapped, sold, recalled, infused, and transformed into persistent wraiths through Soul-Cairn patronage.

The Gatekeepers' soul technologies are mechanically real inside the mod, but their existence does not by itself prove that the Vigilant reincarnation model is false. The conflict is over the default destiny, ownership, and sacred meaning of souls.

Resolution: unresolved metaphysics with strong evidence that souls are practically manipulable.

## Perspective conflict: the Synod

A Synod researcher calls the Synod the finest magical society in the Empire and describes branches across Cyrodiil.

Mirabelle Ervine describes the Synod and College of Whispers as political organizations seeking power through magic, says recruitment is exclusive and wealth/political utility matter, and says both groups are pushing into Skyrim.

These claims can coexist factually. The conflict is over institutional legitimacy and motive.

Resolution: preserve both as member testimony and rival-institution testimony.

## Official record vs hidden-event reconstruction: Gray-Wave children

The Gray-Wave census officially records the final 37 children as stillborn.

The legal archive records suspicious centralized births, obstruction of investigation, unsanctioned conjuration, and a witness to an abominable ritual at the hunting lodge. A later Hermaeus-Mora investigation finds Seeker evidence at the site and infers that the children were offered for forbidden knowledge.

Resolution: the broad Gray-Wave/Hermaeus-Mora occult connection is strongly supported; the exact fate of every one of the 37 children remains an investigative reconstruction rather than a directly witnessed event.

## Temporal reversal: Vigilant Toruld

Early Toruld recruits the player into service to Stendarr and manages ordinary Vigilant work.

Later Toruld says that if Stendarr exists he is dead or functionally powerless, argues that the Divines have failed, and adopts unnamed Soul-Cairn powers as "new gods" for an offensive war against Daedra.

This is not a continuity error. It is a documented collapse of faith and ideological radicalization.

## Why this layer matters

The mod repeatedly asks the player to operate without an omniscient source. Characters, cults, scholars, institutions, legal archives, field experiments, and gameplay mechanics all provide different kinds of evidence.

Future retrieval should therefore answer:
1. what happened;
2. who claims an explanation;
3. whether another source disagrees;
4. whether mechanics corroborate any part;
5. what remains unresolved.
