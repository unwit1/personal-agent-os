# Semantic lore analysis — pass 03: written lore and institutions

This pass prioritizes long-form books, manuals, research reports, and institutional testimony. The key methodological change is to distinguish practical handbooks, experimental reports, religious scholarship, propaganda, and reader annotations instead of treating every book as an encyclopedic authority.

## Witchhunter professional culture

Regalt and the Witch-Hunter's Primer establish an informal bounty-hunting profession rather than a centralized guild. The Whiterun-area "guild" is an old skooma den used by itinerant hunters. Regalt says hunters specialize in witches, warlocks, Daedra, werebeasts, vampires, and undead, while Grados sells intelligence and trophies are exchanged for standing bounties.

The Primer is both field manual and social document. It assumes no-questions-asked bounties on draugr, vampires, hagravens, and werewolves; treats illegal magical experimentation as killable work; recommends ambush and ranged attacks; and contains explicit professional prejudices against alchemists, rogue mages, witches, and some supernatural beings. Practical observations are useful evidence for the mod's monster-hunting economy, but broad biological or historical statements remain the manual author's claims.

## School of Julianos

Aslfur the Wise presents the School as a knowledge-preservation institution: gathering, restoring, copying, teaching, archiving hold records, advising Jarls, and rebuilding records lost during the Oblivion Crisis. The Restoration Instructions manuscript corroborates a careful conservation culture built around material matching, transcription, explicit marking of speculative reconstruction, and preservation of provenance.

The Skyrim school is said to be the only one in the province, with additional schools in Cyrodiil and High Rock. Its library is described as the largest general collection in Skyrim, while the Bards College and College of Winterhold specialize more heavily in artistic and magical works.

## Servitor soul-fragment experiment

The Servitor Experiment Log describes an attempt to modify a familiar spell so that the manifested construct isolates a learning/memory aspect of the caster's own soul. The construct grows more capable over repeated manifestations, becomes sapient and hostile, and eventually claims the experimenter's body and soul.

The sequence of events is a direct continuity fact because the log changes narrator at the end. The researcher's theory that a familiar literally isolates an aspect of the caster's soul is still a model proposed by the experimenter, not automatic universal metaphysics.

## Probable Thalmor black-research program

Patron Notes and Essence Extraction Results appear to come from the same research facility/program. Patron Notes explicitly references the essence-extraction work, a military branch, propaganda considerations, "Agent Ancano attached to the college", and a request for Justiciars to investigate surveillance. It also speaks of institutional hostility toward the Hist and coordinated resident researchers.

Those markers strongly imply a Thalmor/Altmeri Dominion black-research program, but neither report actually labels the facility "Thalmor". The identity is therefore stored as a strong inference.

The program investigates:
- patron attunement to non-Et'Ada entities;
- Aedric/aspect extraction and ingestion;
- military applications;
- population-scale "Lorkhic suppression";
- Hist, Magna Ge, Psijic-like beings, Ideal Masters, Mannimarco, Dreamsleeve phenomena, and other metaphysical targets.

Essence Extraction Results reports Heartstone/Lorkhan, Taproot/Y'ffre, and Ice-Wraith/Tsun-Zenithar-Z'en experiments. The author admits killing a Nordic test subject immediately after a Lorkhan-attunement observation and asks that the result be sealed. "Phase 2, Lorkhic suppression" is proposed for population-scale testing in Skyrim. These are direct facts about the research program; the claimed Aedric correspondences are experimental interpretations.

## Daedric reference corpus

The Daedric Princes and Their Servants is a large in-world reference work cataloging titles, spheres, realms, and servant types. It is valuable as a compact statement of what an educated occult reference text in this continuity teaches.

Some entries make stronger ontological claims—such as describing Seekers and Lurkers as corrupted mortals—and therefore remain attributed to the book. A reader annotation at the end infers that the Gray-Wave family's final generation bargained newborns to Hermaeus Mora to become Seekers; this annotation is separate evidence and should not be mistaken for the reference author's conclusion.

## Daedric script and magical writing

The Daedric Alphabet argues that Daedric letters have close Tamrielic analogues, speculates about a common language root, and suggests variant letterforms may resemble dialects. The book also says words and names have power among Daedra.

This intersects Quinergus's elemental-rune research and the Dagon cell's "sigil" craft. Quinergus explicitly wonders why Daedric letters, rather than Shalidor's script, are required for elemental rune ink. The Dagon cultist describes his own sigils as derivative conjuration devices rather than true high-Daedra sigils. Together these sources establish that this continuity treats written script choice as magically functional, while the linguistic explanation remains scholarly speculation.

## Elemental essence research

Quinergus develops a method for extracting relatively stable elemental power from atronach salts and transferring it into a Jazbay base. The resulting infusion can temporarily strengthen elemental Destruction, provide limited magical potential to non-mages, act as a weapon oil, or serve as rune ink.

His written draft further describes the Atronach Forge as a higher-quality production method. These are continuity-level experimental practices; his explanation of why the Forge works remains admitted uncertainty.

## Comparative theology of sexuality

Milanie Carius's Sexuality in the Daedric and Aedric Spheres compares Mara, Dibella, Sanguine, Mephala, and Molag Bal through "focus" and "intent". It contrasts fertility/creation with gratification, mutual family bonding with exploitative intimacy, and finally Molag Bal's domination as the stripping of agency from all of those systems.

This is an in-world theological framework, not objective proof of divine metaphysics. It is especially relevant to Lysara Carius and the Widows of Mephala because it supplies an intellectual framework for treating intimacy as a Mephalan instrument.

## Syndicate origin: Carsten, Bashnag, and the Falkreath witch

Operative Green says Carsten and cofounder Bashnag found a witch who taught them a ritual to summon a Daedra that empowered them. Carsten handled business and Bashnag fieldwork until Bashnag split away with the strongest spellswords.

A separate surviving mage from the precursor occult group tells a compatible story: a Falkreath witch entered the inner circle, learned the leader's power-granting ritual, shared it with two others, and the trio staged a coup during which a winged horror killed most members. The survivors/traitors then formed a group south of Kynesgrove and began building a criminal empire.

The two accounts strongly converge on the origin of the Syndicate/Gentleman's Club network, but neither names every member of the "treasonous trio". The exact mapping of Carsten, Bashnag, and the witch across both accounts therefore remains partially inferential.

## Mr. Aedwatch

Aedwatch is an Alinor-born or Alinor-established gem-and-gold magnate who moved to Skyrim during the Thalmor political rise and founded the Rift Bank and Mineral Exchange. He treats fame as a tradable resource: the player can publicly credit him for heroic work in exchange for crystals that permanently increase magicka.

An Aedwatch-associated note titled Expectations orders magically bound servants to acquire exceptional non-Aedric/non-Daedric items for a private collection. His curator likewise accepts unusual donations but refuses Daedric items. These sources establish an acquisitive magical power structure around his estate, but they do not establish that Aedwatch is connected to the separate probable-Thalmor research facility.

A Dagon cultist claims Aedwatch's departure from Alinor was an "evacuation" and repeats a rumor about Aedwatch's father and the Crystal Tower. That is hostile rumor and remains explicitly attributed.
